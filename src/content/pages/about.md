**Last updated:** 14 November 2026

Most construction cost content gives you a range and stops. "$150,000 to $400,000." That range is technically correct and practically useless — it is wide enough to include a garage conversion and a coastal custom build.

Every Line Item does one thing instead: **we model a specific scenario and price every phase of it.**

## What we cover

Every Line Item covers the cost of building an accessory dwelling unit (ADU) — and residential construction more broadly — in California: municipal plan-check and permit fees, school and park impact fees, utility capacity charges, and the construction costs themselves.

As of 14 November 2026, the site covers **164 jurisdictions** with **538 verified fee lines**. Every fee figure links to the official source document it came from — the city or county fee schedule, the school district's developer-fee resolution, or the utility district's charges — with the date we last verified it. Figures we cannot verify from an official source are marked "verify with city" and excluded from every total. Fee schedules are re-verified on a rolling schedule, with the next full pass in December 2026.

## The six-phase ledger

Every build on this site is broken into the same six phases:

1. **Soft costs** — design, permits, plan check
2. **Site work and foundation** — excavation, footings, utility trenching
3. **Framing and structure** — lumber, labor, trusses, sheathing
4. **Building envelope** — roofing, windows, siding
5. **Systems and interior** — electrical, plumbing, HVAC, drywall, kitchen, bath
6. **Landscaping and hardscape** — paths, planting, finish grading

The schema never changes. That is the point.

Because every build uses the same six rows, you can hold any two projects side by side and the lines still match up. An 800 sq ft new build against a garage conversion. A coastal build against an inland one. That comparison does not exist in any public dataset we are aware of, and it is the reason this site exists.

It also means you can take your own contractor quote, sort it into these six rows, and see exactly which line does not match.

## The law behind the numbers

California ADU fees are set by state law as much as by each city. Three rules shape almost every figure on this site:

- **Impact fees: 750 sq ft.** Under [California Government Code §66311.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?chapter=13&division=1.&lawCode=GOV&part=&title=7.), ADUs of 750 square feet or less pay **$0** in impact fees. Above 750 sq ft, impact fees are charged in proportion to the primary dwelling.
- **School fees: 500 sq ft.** Under the same section, ADUs under 500 square feet are exempt from school developer fees. School fees themselves are levied under California Education Code §17620; the 2026 Level I cap is **$5.38 per square foot**.
- **SB 1117 (from 1 January 2027).** For up to two ADUs per parcel, impact fees apply only to the area **above** 750 square feet. Connection and capacity charges are not covered by that rule.

Fee schedules change — cities, counties, school districts, and utilities typically update theirs each July 1 or January 1 — so the "last verified" date on each page is part of the figure, not a footnote.

## What we do not do

This matters more than what we do, because it is what makes the numbers worth reading.

- **We do not build ADUs.** No construction company, no design-build arm.
- **We do not sell plans.** There is nothing on this site to buy that depends on you deciding to build.
- **We do not sell your information to contractors.** No lead forms, no "get matched with a builder," no data sharing.
- **We do not take money from anyone whose costs we publish.** No builder, modular company, or lender has paid for, reviewed, or influenced any figure here.

If a number looks bad for the industry, we publish it anyway. If we get one wrong, we correct it in public and log the change.

The site currently earns no revenue. It is configured for display advertising through Google AdSense. If that changes, it will be through display advertising, affiliate links to software tools, or paid resources we make ourselves — **never through contractor referrals or lead generation.** Any affiliate relationship will be disclosed on the page where it appears.

That constraint costs us the most lucrative revenue in this niche. It is also the only reason the numbers mean anything.
