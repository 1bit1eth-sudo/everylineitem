Most construction cost content gives you a range and stops. "$150,000 to
$400,000." That range is technically correct and practically useless — it's
wide enough to include a garage conversion and a coastal custom build.

Every Line Item does one thing instead: **we model a specific scenario and
price every phase of it.**

## The six-phase ledger

Every build on this site is broken into the same six phases:

1. **Soft costs** — design, permits, plan check
2. **Site work and foundation** — excavation, footings, utility trenching
3. **Framing and structure** — lumber, labor, trusses, sheathing
4. **Building envelope** — roofing, windows, siding
5. **Systems and interior** — electrical, plumbing, HVAC, drywall, kitchen, bath
6. **Landscaping and hardscape** — paths, planting, finish grading

The schema never changes. That's the point.

Because every build uses the same six rows, you can hold any two projects
side by side and the lines still match up. An 800 sq ft new build against a
garage conversion. A coastal build against an inland one. **That comparison
doesn't exist in any public dataset we're aware of, and it's the reason this
site exists.**

It also means you can take your own contractor quote, sort it into these six
rows, and see exactly which line doesn't match.

## What we don't do

This matters more than what we do, because it's what makes the numbers worth
reading.

- **We don't build ADUs.** No construction company, no design-build arm.
- **We don't sell plans.** There is nothing on this site to buy that depends
  on you deciding to build.
- **We don't sell your information to contractors.** No lead forms, no
  "get matched with a builder," no data sharing.
- **We don't take money from anyone whose costs we publish.** No builder,
  modular company, or lender has paid for, reviewed, or influenced any figure
  here.

If a number looks bad for the industry, we publish it anyway. If we get one
wrong, we correct it in public and log the change.

## How this site makes money

Currently it doesn't. If that changes, it will be through display
advertising, affiliate links to software tools, or paid resources we make
ourselves — **never through contractor referrals or lead generation.** Any
affiliate relationship will be disclosed on the page where it appears.

That constraint costs us the most lucrative revenue in this niche. It's also
the only reason the numbers mean anything.
