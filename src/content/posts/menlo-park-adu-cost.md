> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Menlo Park costs at least **$306,304, or about $383 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and anyone searching for Menlo Park ADU fees will find a fee stack that is unusual in two opposite directions at once. One line is priced at zero on purpose, in a table that charges single-family homes nearly $20,000 on the same line. Another line — the building permit, usually the easiest fee to find — cannot be priced at all, because the tables start above the size of the unit being priced. This post counts the zero, counts the school fee, and leaves the permit blank rather than borrowing a row that does not belong to this project.

## The $0 line and the $4,304 line

These two lines are the post, so they get stated first and exactly. The transportation impact fee table in the city's Development Guidelines, headed as of June 2026, prices an accessory dwelling unit at $0 per unit, against $19,430.37 for a single-family unit and $6,548.93 for a multifamily unit. A $0 that appears as its own row in a published table is a verified figure; a $0 inferred from silence would not be. The school fee is the mirror image — a full rate, split between two districts:

| Counted fee (Menlo Park, 800 sq ft, MPCSD-served parcel) | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| School fee, MPCSD 60% share: 800 × $3.228 | $3,228 |
| School fee, SUHSD 40% share: 800 × $2.152 | $2,152 |
| **School fee, counted** | **$4,304** |
| Transportation impact fee, ADU line (2026 table) | **$0** |
| **Counted fees, total** | **$4,304** |

The scope line matters. The $5.38 combined rate and the 60/40 split are confirmed for the Menlo Park City School District area by the district's resolution and live fee page. Part of Menlo Park — the Belle Haven area — is in Ravenswood City Elementary rather than MPCSD, and the Ravenswood feeder share was not confirmed in this round. The statewide Level I cap is the same $5.38, but a Belle Haven parcel should confirm its feeder share before using the $4,304 figure. A post that presented $4,304 as a single citywide rate without that sentence would be overstating what was verified.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. Menlo Park's own guidance mirrors the first half for city fees — ADUs of 750 square feet or smaller are exempt from city impact fees — which is why the $0 transportation line for an 800 square foot ADU still matters: it is priced, not exempted. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) changes the treatment for lots with two or fewer ADUs: only the area in excess of 750 square feet is charged, so an 800 sq ft unit would be charged on 50 sq ft. That future rule is not in this post's 2026 total, and it does not change the school fee, which is not a covered impact fee. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The permit table's missing rung.** This is the largest uncounted line and the most mechanical question in the post: what does the Building Division charge for an R-3 unit below 1,000 square feet — the 1,000-square-foot tier price, a prorated figure, or a minimum? The schedule's tier mechanics are verified across higher tiers (fee equals the base at the largest tier at or below the project size, plus the per-square-foot rate on the excess), so the division's answer will convert directly into a figure. Until then, any permit number for an 800 square foot Menlo Park ADU is borrowed from another row.

**2. The valuation-dependent lines.** The street impact fee (0.58% of valuation), SMIP ($0.13 per $1,000 of valuation), and the 9% technology and 8% General Plan update fees all wait on the same missing input: the valuation the city assigns to an ADU. The storm drainage question waits on a different answer — whether a detached ADU on an existing lot triggers a new lot connection at all. Both answers come from the city, not from the schedule text.

**3. The district edges.** The MPCSD and SUHSD shares are confirmed for MPCSD-served parcels, but the SUHSD rate table itself failed to load in this round, and the Ravenswood feeder share for Belle Haven parcels was not confirmed. [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) covers why a statewide cap cannot stand in for a district rate — and in Menlo Park's case, why the feeder map matters as much as the rate. Confirm the parcel's feeder before paying.

## Menlo Park vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Menlo Park (verified floor) | **$306,304** | $4,304 school fee + $0 transportation fee; permit and valuation lines excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Menlo Park sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: Menlo Park's figure is missing its entire permit bill and every valuation-dependent line, while the LA County figures include theirs. The honest reading is not that Menlo Park is cheap; it is that Menlo Park's countable fee is one school line plus a priced zero, and its permit is the open question.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $306,304 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, ask the Building Division for its below-tier rule for an 800 square foot R-3 unit and the valuation it will assign, confirm the parcel's school feeder (MPCSD versus Ravenswood) with the districts, and carry separate, explicit allowances for the permit, the street impact fee, the storm drainage charge, the state add-ons, and the utility connections. When the city and districts answer, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision. That is the point of keeping guesses out of the total in the first place.
