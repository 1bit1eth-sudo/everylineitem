Forty-two days from now, the most hated math in California ADU building changes. Right now, in many cities, an 801st square foot can cost you thousands of dollars — because crossing 750 sq ft triggers impact fees on the *entire* ADU, including the 750 feet that were free. On January 1, 2027, that cliff becomes a staircase.

Here is what SB 1117 actually says, in plain English.

## The bill, in one paragraph

**SB 1117** (Senator Sabrina Cervantes) is titled "Accessory dwelling units and junior accessory dwelling units." It became **Chapter 809, Statutes of 2026**, signed by Governor Newsom on **September 29, 2026**, and takes effect **January 1, 2027**. It amends exactly one section of state law — **Government Code section 66311.5**, the ADU fee section.

That is the whole bill. One section, one rule change, one effective date.

## The rule change, in plain English

**Before:** Cities could charge impact fees on an ADU of 750 sq ft or larger "proportionately in relation to the square footage of the primary dwelling." In practice, many jurisdictions interpreted this as: once you cross 750 sq ft, the full per-square-foot impact fee applies to the *entire* unit — the first 750 feet included.

**After (Jan 1, 2027):** Impact fees on the **first two ADUs** on a lot may be imposed **only on the livable square footage above 750 sq ft**. Not on the first 750. Not on the whole unit. Just the part that sticks out past the line.

The prior cliff rule is gone. In its place: marginal math.

## The math, with examples

Here is how an ADU of any size gets assessed under the new rule:

| ADU size | Billable square footage | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 749 sq ft | 0 | Exempt, as today |
| 750 sq ft | 0 | The line itself is still exempt |
| **800 sq ft** | **50 sq ft** | Our benchmark — fees on 50, not 800 |
| 900 sq ft | 150 sq ft | The extra 50 feet costs exactly that |
| 1,000 sq ft | 250 sq ft | Still proportional — but only above 750 |

The old rule made the 751st square foot the most expensive one on the drawing. The new rule makes every square foot above 750 cost exactly what a square foot costs — no more, no less.

**The third-ADU exception:** the marginal break applies to the *first two* ADUs on a lot. A third ADU is assessed on the entire unit. (The bill also clarifies when ADUs are exempt from utility connection fees — pending full-text verification of the exact mechanics.)

## What it covers — and what it does not touch

**Covered:** impact fees assessed under the **Mitigation Fee Act and the Quimby Act** — the development impact fees, park fees, traffic mitigation fees, and similar line items cities levy on new construction.

**Not touched:**

- **School developer fees.** These live under a completely separate regime (Education Code section 17620), charged by school districts, not cities. SB 1117 does not change them. A school fee of $4,304 on an 800 sq ft ADU is $4,304 before and after.
- **Building permit and plan-check fees.** Unchanged.
- **Utility connection charges.** Clarified separately by the bill; not reduced by it.
- **Permits pulled in 2026.** Nothing changes on a permit you pull this year. The marginal rule applies only from the January 1, 2027 effective date.

## What the savings look like, city by city

Dollar impact depends on each city's per-square-foot impact-fee rates and — this matters — **how each city implements the marginal rule**, which is still pending verification as 2027 fee schedules are published. With that caveat, here is the illustrative math for an 800 sq ft ADU, using our published city research:

| City | Current impact-fee bill (~) | Post-SB 1117 bill (~) | Savings (~) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Encinitas | $22,040 | $1,378 | **~$20,663** |
| Palo Alto | $8,000–$20,000 | $500–$1,250 | **up to ~$18,750** |
| Ontario | $18,798 | $1,175 | **~$17,623** |
| Salinas | $0 | $0 | **$0** — no city DIF on ADUs |
| Oakland | $0 | $0 | **$0** — no impact fees on ADUs |

Notes on the arithmetic: Encinitas charges $27.55/sq ft on the whole 800 sq ft today ($22,040); under the marginal rule, 50 sq ft x $27.55 = $1,377.50. Palo Alto's current proportional DIF runs roughly $8,000–$20,000 on an 800 sq ft ADU (our full research is [here](/posts/palo-alto-adu-cost/)); the marginal figure drops to roughly $500–$1,250. Ontario prorates ADU impact fees against the primary dwelling: an 800 sq ft ADU behind a 1,600 sq ft house in General City pays $18,797.50 today; marginal treatment brings it to roughly $1,175. Salinas charges no city DIF on ADUs at all, and Oakland has no impact fees on ADUs — so there is nothing for the bill to reduce.

**Pending verification:** these are illustrative calculations from current (2026) rate schedules. Exact 2027 figures depend on each city's published 2027 fee schedules and implementation, which we will verify in the December 1 quarterly re-verification.

## What homeowners should do now

1. **Check your ADU's size against the 750 line.** Under 750? Nothing changes for you — you were already exempt from impact fees. Over 750? The bill is your money.
2. **Look up your city's per-square-foot impact-fee rate.** The savings scale with the rate: steep-cliff cities (Encinitas, Palo Alto, Ontario) are where waiting for January pays; zero-DIF cities (Salinas, Oakland, Fremont, Santa Monica, Oceanside) make the timing irrelevant.
3. **If your project is on the January border, call your city's building division.** How each city will implement the marginal rule — billing systems, fee schedules, transition dates — is still being worked out. Get the answer in writing.
4. **Remember what does not move.** School fees, permit fees, utility connection charges, and 2026 permits are all outside this bill. Run the full all-in math, not just the impact-fee line.
5. **Do not rush a December permit to "beat" the law.** The bill only helps; it hurts nobody. A permit pulled in December gets 2026 rules — no penalty, but no marginal treatment either.
