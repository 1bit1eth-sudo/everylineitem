> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in El Cajon costs at least **$305,947, or about $382 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and that sentence carries a deliberate qualification. The number is a floor: construction phases carried from the series baseline plus only the fees confirmed from official documents. Anyone searching for El Cajon ADU fees will find plenty of round estimates elsewhere; this post is built the other way, from the fee schedule outward, and it leaves blank every line the schedule does not price.

El Cajon turns out to be a study in partial transparency. The city's current Schedule of Miscellaneous Fees, adopted as Resolution No. 036-26 on May 12, 2026 and effective July 13, 2026, is unusually legible: fixed charges are listed to the dollar, including a planning plan check line that names ADUs explicitly. Then, at the exact point where the money gets large, the document points elsewhere. The building permit is priced by valuation tiers drawn from a San Diego Chapter ICC valuation schedule that the miscellaneous schedule cites but never reproduces. Without that valuation table, the single biggest permit line cannot be computed from anything the city publishes. This post prices what can be priced, names what cannot, and keeps the two lists separate.

The school fee tells a similar two-half story. El Cajon is a split-district city: one elementary district and one high-school district, each levying its own share. One half is documented to the day; the other is published without a date. Both halves are counted here, and the weaker half is flagged rather than hidden.

The soft-costs phase is where El Cajon's paperwork lives, so it is worth unpacking: $14,000 of design from the series baseline, $315 of fixed city charges, and $3,632 of school fees. Everything else in that phase — the valuation-based permit, plan check at 65% of that permit, the tiered code enforcement surcharge, trade permits, and any utility capacity charge — is excluded, not estimated. A floor that quietly absorbed guesses would stop being a floor.

One scope note, because readers often ask for the companion figure: this post does not publish a verified total for a new 2,000 sq ft single-family home in El Cajon. The fee schedule prices permits by valuation and never prices construction, so no honest total can be derived from it. The 2,000 sq ft figure appears here only as the denominator that makes the example legible — an 800 sq ft ADU is 40% of a 2,000 sq ft primary home, which is the proportion the state's impact-fee rule uses once the 750 sq ft cliff is crossed.

Two details in that table deserve emphasis. First, the elementary district's rate is the best-documented number in the whole post: a board hearing, an adopted resolution, and an effective date of July 11, 2026, all stated on the district's own fee page, plus a written exemption for residential projects of 500 sq ft or less. Our 800 sq ft unit is above that exemption, so the full 800 sq ft is charged. Second, the high-school district's $1.20 rate is real — it is printed on the district's current fee page — but the page carries no effective date and its background note cites a 2016 justification study. A rate page that old in its sourcing deserves a phone call before money changes hands, and the fee autopsy says so rather than smoothing it over. Note also that the elementary district's $3.34 equals 62% of the state's $5.38 per-square-foot cap, the elementary share pattern used elsewhere in the county; that arithmetic does not prove anything about the high-school share, but it is one more reason to confirm the second half directly.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, formerly Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. El Cajon's own code chapter on residential zones now cites the successor ADU sections, 66314 and 66323, for the same framework. Connection and capacity charges are a separate category from impact fees, which is why sewer and water lines stay in their own row. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The valuation-based permit.** This is the largest unknown and it is not close. Depending on the ICC valuation the city assigns to an 800 sq ft dwelling, the permit tier, the 65% plan check, and the code enforcement surcharge could add anywhere from a few hundred to a few thousand dollars. The city's schedule publishes the tiers but not the valuation input, so no responsible estimate fits here. The practical move is to ask the Building and Fire Safety Division for the valuation figure for your plans before budgeting the permit line — that single answer unlocks three rows of this table at once.

**2. The high-school district's rate currency.** If the Grossmont Union High School District has raised its share since the rate shown on its fee page, the $960 half of the school fee rises with it. The $672 gap between the counted school total and the full state-cap figure is the size of the question, not an answer, and the district's own payment office is the place to close it. A related check costs nothing: confirm that your parcel falls in the Cajon Valley and Grossmont pairing at all. El Cajon's lots are covered by this pair, but school boundaries do not follow city limits exactly, and a different pairing would mean different rates on both halves.

**3. Sewer and water treatment of the ADU.** State law says an ADU is generally not treated as a new residential use for connection-fee purposes unless it was built together with a new single-family home. That protection covers connection fees and capacity charges, not impact fees, and it does not answer whether El Cajon's $900 sewer wet tap applies to a particular ADU lateral, or whether the water provider levies any capacity charge on the unit. Both stay excluded and flagged. From January 1, 2027, incoming state changes to ADU impact-fee treatment may also alter the picture for an 800 sq ft unit, so re-check the rules in force at permit time rather than relying on this post's snapshot.

## El Cajon vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| El Cajon (verified floor) | **$305,947** | $315 fixed city fees + $3,632 school fees; valuation-based permit excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

El Cajon sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: El Cajon's figure is missing its largest permit line, while the LA County figures include theirs. Once the valuation-based permit is priced, expect the gap to narrow, possibly by thousands. What El Cajon genuinely leads on is legibility — a fee schedule that names the ADU in its planning line and prices every fixed charge to the dollar is rarer in this series than it should be. The city publishes everything except the one table that matters most, which is a strange place to stop, but it is a better starting point than a schedule that publishes nothing at all.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $305,947 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, and carry a separate, explicit allowance for the three unpriced items — the valuation-based permit and its plan check, the sewer wet tap question, and the water provider's charge. When the city answers the valuation question, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision: the verified lines do not change, only the blank ones fill in. That is the point of keeping guesses out of the total in the first place.

## What this post does not claim

No pre-approved plan set is claimed for El Cajon, because none could be confirmed from the city itself. Builder marketing pages describe pre-approved El Cajon plans, but a marketing claim is not a city program, and counting a design saving that may not exist would understate the floor. Readers who want the background on plan programs generally should read [California's free ADU plans](/posts/free-adu-plans-california/) and then confirm availability with El Cajon's Planning Division before assuming any saving. Similarly, no impact-fee dollar figure is claimed, no sewer or water capacity figure is claimed, and no 2,000 sq ft home total is claimed.
