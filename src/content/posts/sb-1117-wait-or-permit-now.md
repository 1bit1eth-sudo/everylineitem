## The rule, in one paragraph

SB 1117 (Chapter 809, signed September 29, 2026, effective January 1, 2027) changes how impact fees work for a homeowner's first two ADUs: the fee applies **only to the square footage above 750**. A third ADU keeps the old rules. School district developer fees do not change, and connection/capacity charges are excluded from SB 1117 entirely. The question every ADU owner is asking right now — permit in 2026, or wait until January — comes down to one thing: how much of your city's impact fee is per-square-foot, and how much is flat.

## How the math works

Take Hemet. Its city development impact fee runs **$8.647 per sq ft**. For an 800 sq ft ADU under the 2026 rules, that is $8.647 × 800 = **$6,918**. From January 1, 2027, only the 50 sq ft above 750 is taxable, so the same line becomes $8.647 × 50 = **$432**. Waiting saves **$6,485** on that one line.

That calculation only works for per-square-foot lines. Most cities do not charge that way.

## The cities where nobody can give you a number yet

Of the 101 verified impact-fee lines in our data, **82 are flat amounts** — a single dollar figure per dwelling, not a per-square-foot rate. SB 1117 says the fee applies only to the area above 750, but it does not say how a flat fee converts. Until each city publishes its method, any January savings figure for these cities would be a guess, and we do not guess.

Nobody knows yet. That uncertainty is itself the story: the cities with the most to lose from SB 1117 are the ones that have said the least about how they will implement it.

## Waiting is not free

A fee comparison is not a recommendation to wait. Fee schedules adjust every fiscal year — several cities index to CPI — so the 2026 rates in this post may not be the rates in effect when you pull a permit. Delay costs money in other ways: financing, contractor scheduling, and months of rent or housing you do not have. Run the numbers for your city, then weigh them against what waiting costs you. If the savings are $6,485 in Hemet, waiting might pay. If your city's impact fees are already $0 at your size, waiting buys nothing.

## What to ask your city

If your city charges a flat impact fee, the single most useful question you can ask its planning counter is: "How will you convert the flat ADU impact fee to the SB 1117 excess-area basis on January 1?" When cities answer, we will update the calculator the same week. After January 1, we will publish a follow-up with what actually changed — city by city, with the same source links.

## FAQs

**Does SB 1117 change school fees?** No. School district developer fees are a separate regime (Education Code §17620) and are untouched.

**Does it apply to a third ADU?** No. The excess-area rule covers a homeowner's first two ADUs. A third ADU keeps the current rules.

**What about water and sewer connection charges?** Excluded. SB 1117 covers impact fees; connection and capacity charges are a different category and do not change.

**My ADU is 750 sq ft or smaller. Does anything change?** No. Impact fees are already $0 at 750 sq ft or less under current law, so SB 1117 changes nothing at that size.
