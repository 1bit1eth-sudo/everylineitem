The same 800 sq ft detached ADU. Sixteen cities. A building permit that costs **$1,006** in one and **$19,200** in another — a 19x spread for the identical stack of paper.

This is the purest ranking this site has published: one line item, every city, no construction costs, no impact fees, no school districts. Just the price of permission.

## The ranking

Every figure below is taken from that city's published post (keyFacts), for an 800 sq ft detached ADU. **The asterisk (\*) is doing real work:** it marks any figure that is partial, estimated, modeled from a valuation schedule, or priced from older rate tables — exactly as each city's post states. Unasterisked figures come straight from a published fee schedule.

Four pricing philosophies run the table. **Flat schedules** (Riverside, Chula Vista) tell you the number before you file. **Valuation-based fees** (the majority, and all three of the most expensive) multiply an assumed construction value by a tiered rate — which is why the top of this ranking belongs to the cities that assume the most expensive builds. **Square-footage-based** (Oceanside) prices the drawings by size. And **Oakland's 4–6%** is valuation-based with the steepest rate in the series — no asterisk, because the rate is published; only the assumed valuation moves the range.

## Five things the ranking shows

**1. The pricing model matters more than the city.** The three cheapest permits include two flat schedules; the five most expensive are all valuation-based. A city does not need high fees to land at the top — it needs a valuation schedule that assumes an expensive build and a high percentage. Oakland's 4–6% is the extreme: on a $320,000 assumed valuation, 6% is $19,200 before a single impact fee is charged.

**2. Most of this table is asterisked — and that is the point.** Eleven of sixteen figures carry a \*. The asterisk is not a shrug; it is the site's honesty mechanism. Oceanside's ~$7,541 is the table's own math from 2018 and 2021 rate tables with the ADU's fee classification unconfirmed. Anaheim's $1,672 and Burbank's $3,692 are modeled from valuation schedules the cities publish but do not pre-compute for ADUs. Each city's post documents exactly what its asterisk means. Read the asterisk before you read the number.

**3. Riverside owns the bottom — twice.** $1,006 is the series low (Fullerton's $1,223 held the record before Riverside's research), and Torrance merely ties its starting point. Riverside pairs the cheap permit with an 800 sq ft free pre-approved plan — the first city whose free plan matches our standard post size exactly, meaning both the design line and the permit line shrink at once.

**4. The flat-fee experiment is winning on transparency.** Chula Vista's $3,951 is mid-table on price but first on knowability: $476 intake + $533 plan check + $2,942 inspection, printed in one line of Fee Bulletin 10-400, no valuation math, no asterisk. Compare that with Glendale's ≈$8,550\* — nearly 2.2x the price, and an estimate. Transparency is not the same as cheapness, but it is the only one of the two a homeowner can bank on at the counter.

**5. Oakland's top is honest — and structural.** Oakland exempts ADUs from impact fees entirely (two city documents say so), so the permit carries what other cities spread across impact-fee lines: $12,800–$19,200 at 4–6% of valuation, plus the $4,136 school fee. The ranking's most expensive permit belongs to one of its most ADU-friendly fee philosophies. That is not a contradiction; it is the tradeoff, stated plainly.

## What changes January 1, 2027

Nothing in this table. SB 1117 narrows **impact fees** to square footage above 750 for a homeowner's first two ADUs; building permit fees, plan check fees, and taxes are not impact fees under the statute and are unaffected. Riverside's $1,006 and Oakland's $12,800–$19,200 are the same numbers on January 2 as they are today. The [48-day countdown](/posts/sb-1117-countdown-48-days/) covers what the new law does move.
