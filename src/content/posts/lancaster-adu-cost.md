Lancaster is the transparency story of this series — and not in a flattering way. The city announced a fee increase on October 1, 2025, published its development impact fee table in 2021, and requires school-fee receipts from two districts before issuing a permit. But the building permit rate table itself is nowhere in the public index. The DIF table is five years old and already contradicted by the city's own AB 1600 report. And only one of the two school districts has a confirmed 2026 rate. This is the thinnest total we have published, and the asterisks are the story.

Our six-phase total lands at **$305,000, or $381 per square foot** — the lowest headline number in the series, and the least complete. The permit, the current DIF, the high-school district's school fee, and sewer/water charges are all excluded. What survives on confirmed paper: **$3,184** in school fees (elementary district only), four pre-approved city plans at $469, and an ADU Assistance Loan Program worth up to $10,000 for qualifying owners. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Lancaster does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## The permit fee that isn't on the index

Every other city in this series publishes its permit math somewhere — a flat fee, a valuation schedule, even Santa Ana's phone number. Lancaster's Building & Safety page discusses the October 1, 2025 fee increase, links to the Accela Citizen Access portal for online filing, and publishes Appendix B for impact fees. The permit rate table itself — valuation-based or otherwise — did not appear in the public document index. This is not "buried on page 47 of a PDF"; it is absent from the index entirely. For a city that requires school-fee receipts from two districts before it will issue a permit, the permit's own price is the one number a homeowner cannot precompute. It sits in the fee autopsy as a blank, and it is the single largest reason the $305,000 total carries its asterisk.

## 799 square feet of irony

Of Lancaster's four pre-approved plans, the Goldenbrush is 2BR/2BA at **799 square feet** — forty-nine square feet over the 750 cliff. The city sells a plan, for $469, that triggers the impact-fee cliff its own ordinance framework could have avoided at 750. The Poppy (350) and Juniper (602) sit safely under the line; the Primrose (840) clears it without irony. The Goldenbrush is the series' purest cliff artifact: a city-designed, city-approved plan that walks straight into the fee its designers knew was there. Under SB 1117's marginal math (effective January 2027), only the 49 sq ft over the line would be fee-able, and the irony dissolves — see the note below.

## $3,184 — and the missing high-school half

The confirmed half is clean: Lancaster School District adopted **$3.98 per square foot** on April 21, 2026, effective June 6, 2026 — $3,184 on 800 sq ft, from the district's own public notice. The missing half is the Antelope Valley Union High School District. The city's permit checklist names AVUHSD explicitly — no receipt, no permit — so the fee definitely exists. But AVUHSD's official 2026 rate could not be found; the only figure in circulation is a 2024 news article's $4.08, which is not an official source and we do not count it. The arithmetic curiosity: $3.98 plus $4.08 would be $8.06, well over the state's $5.38 Level 1 ceiling — so the two districts presumably have a fee-sharing agreement that splits the ceiling. That agreement is also unconfirmed. $3,184 is a floor with a high-school-shaped hole in it.

## A DIF table from 2021

Appendix B is official, detailed, and five years old: about **$10,113–$10,613 per dwelling unit** for a single-family home in March 2021 dollars. Under state law, an 800 sq ft ADU would pay a proportional share of that — but the city's own AB 1600 report for FY2024-25 shows the Open Space line alone has moved from $315 to $361, which means the table is a museum piece, not a price list. Layered on top are two county add-ons: the LA County traffic impact fee ($1,578.17 per PM peak-hour trip) and the Fire District Area 3 fee at **$1.2010 per square foot** (2026) — a per-square-foot fire fee, unusual in a series dominated by per-unit DIFs, and itself $960.80 on 800 sq ft. Whether either county fee actually attaches to an ADU is not stated anywhere we could find. The entire DIF stack is excluded and marked "verify with city."

## The $10,000 loan that softens the opacity

The series rarity in Lancaster is not a fee but a financing program. The ADU Assistance Loan Program offers low-to-moderate-income owner-occupants up to **$10,000**, interest-free and deferred, covering exactly the soft costs this post struggles to price: design, permits, school fees, sewer charges. With five-year forgiveness terms, it is the only city in the series so far that pairs its ADU program with its own money. It does not fix the transparency problem — a loan does not publish a rate table — but it changes the homeowner's exposure to it. For qualifying owners, up to $10,000 of the unknowable becomes the city's problem.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Lancaster is the series' cleanest illustration of what that does. The city's own Goldenbrush plan at 799 sq ft would owe fees on 49 sq ft instead of 800 — the 49-square-foot irony becomes a rounding error. The per-square-foot Fire District fee ($1.2010) sits outside the DIF question and would presumably continue. We found no mention of SB 1117 in city materials, and no Lancaster ordinance text addressing the ADU proportional formula was found at all; the January 2027 quarterly review will check both the permit index and any revised DIF schedule. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and stay at $3,184 plus whatever AVUHSD's confirmed rate adds.
