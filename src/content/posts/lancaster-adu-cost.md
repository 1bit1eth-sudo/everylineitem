Lancaster is the transparency story of this series — and not in a flattering way. The city announced a fee increase on October 1, 2025, and requires school-fee receipts from two districts before issuing a permit. But the building permit rate table itself is nowhere in the public index. Two of the three gaps that made this the thinnest total in the series have since closed: the current impact-fee schedule (effective October 1, 2025) is verified, and both school districts have confirmed 2026 rates. The permit table is still the missing piece, and the asterisks are still the story.

Our six-phase total lands at **$310,000, or $388 per square foot** — under the LA County baseline, and still incomplete. The building permit and sewer/water charges are excluded. What survives on confirmed paper: **$4,256** in school fees (both districts), **$3,651** in prorated impact fees from the current schedule, four pre-approved city plans at $469, and an ADU Assistance Loan Program worth up to $10,000 for qualifying owners. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Lancaster does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## The permit fee that isn't on the index

Every other city in this series publishes its permit math somewhere — a flat fee, a valuation schedule, even Santa Ana's phone number. Lancaster's Building & Safety page discusses the October 1, 2025 fee increase, links to the Accela Citizen Access portal for online filing, and publishes Appendix B for impact fees. The permit rate table itself — valuation-based or otherwise — did not appear in the public document index. This is not "buried on page 47 of a PDF"; it is absent from the index entirely. For a city that requires school-fee receipts from two districts before it will issue a permit, the permit's own price is the one number a homeowner cannot precompute. It sits in the fee autopsy as a blank, and it is the single largest reason the $310,000 total carries its asterisk.

## 799 square feet of irony

Of Lancaster's four pre-approved plans, the Goldenbrush is 2BR/2BA at **799 square feet** — forty-nine square feet over the 750 cliff. The city sells a plan, for $469, that triggers the impact-fee cliff its own ordinance framework could have avoided at 750. The Poppy (350) and Juniper (602) sit safely under the line; the Primrose (840) clears it without irony. The Goldenbrush is the series' purest cliff artifact: a city-designed, city-approved plan that walks straight into the fee its designers knew was there. Under SB 1117's marginal math (effective January 2027), only the 49 sq ft over the line would be fee-able, and the irony dissolves — see the note below.

## $4,256 — both halves confirmed

The elementary half is clean: Lancaster School District adopted **$3.98 per square foot** on April 21, 2026, effective June 6, 2026 — $3,184 on 800 sq ft, from the district's own public notice. The high-school half is now confirmed too: the Antelope Valley Union High School District adopted **$1.34 per square foot** in Resolution No. 2024-25-16 (adopted November 21, 2024, effective January 20, 2025) — $1,072 on 800 sq ft, the district's 26% share of the Level I maximum. Together the two adopted rates are **$5.32 per square foot, $4,256 on 800 sq ft**, within the 2026 Level I cap of $5.38. That resolves the arithmetic curiosity that haunted the first version of this post: $3.98 plus the 2024 news article's $4.08 would have been $8.06, well over the cap — because $4.08 was never AVUHSD's share. The fee-sharing split was documented all along; it just took the resolution itself to see it. The city's permit checklist names AVUHSD explicitly — no receipt, no permit — and now both receipts have a number attached.

## A current DIF schedule, prorated

The 2021 Appendix B table is now history: about **$10,113–$10,613 per dwelling unit** in March 2021 dollars, superseded by the Citywide Fee Schedule effective October 1, 2025. The current Section 3 prices six impact fees per dwelling unit — traffic signal $1,399, traffic $2,227, park development $3,153, administrative offices $75, corporate yard $71, open space $372 — **$7,297 per dwelling unit** in total. Under state law, an 800 sq ft ADU pays a proportional share of that: half, against the 1,600 sq ft default primary, or **$3,651**. At 750 sq ft and under the DIF is $0. Three lines from the old table did not carry into the counted total, each for a stated reason: the drainage fee is now $0.48 per square foot of lot (lot-size dependent, no ADU rule published), the park in-lieu fee is bedroom-dependent ($591/$887/$1,184), and the biological fee is $770 per gross acre. Layered on top are two county add-ons: the LA County traffic impact fee ($1,578.17 per PM peak-hour trip) and the Fire District Area 3 fee at **$1.2010 per square foot** (2026) — a per-square-foot fire fee, unusual in a series dominated by per-unit DIFs, and itself $960.80 on 800 sq ft. Whether either county fee actually attaches to an ADU is not stated anywhere we could find, so both stay out of the total and marked "verify with city."

## The $10,000 loan that softens the opacity

The series rarity in Lancaster is not a fee but a financing program. The ADU Assistance Loan Program offers low-to-moderate-income owner-occupants up to **$10,000**, interest-free and deferred, covering exactly the soft costs this post struggles to price: design, permits, school fees, sewer charges. With five-year forgiveness terms, it is the only city in the series so far that pairs its ADU program with its own money. It does not fix the transparency problem — a loan does not publish a rate table — but it changes the homeowner's exposure to it. For qualifying owners, up to $10,000 of the unknowable becomes the city's problem.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Lancaster is the series' cleanest illustration of what that does. The city's own Goldenbrush plan at 799 sq ft would owe fees on 49 sq ft instead of 800 — the 49-square-foot irony becomes a rounding error. The per-square-foot Fire District fee ($1.2010) sits outside the DIF question and would presumably continue. We found no mention of SB 1117 in city materials, and no Lancaster ordinance text addressing the ADU proportional formula was found at all; the January 2027 quarterly review will check both the permit index and any revised DIF schedule. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and stay at $4,256.
