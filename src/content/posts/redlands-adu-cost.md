Redlands prices its ADU impact fees the way a grocer prices produce: by the pound, line by line, all from one document. **Eight per-square-foot lines, one ordinance (Ord. 2968), $5.4405 per square foot in total** — $4,352 on an 800 sq ft ADU. No flat fees, no pro-rata against the primary dwelling, no percentage of valuation. Every line carries a 751 sq ft minimum in the data, which means the 750 sq ft cliff is written into the fee schedule itself: build at 750 or under and all eight lines are $0. Our six-phase total lands at **$309,000, or $386 per square foot** — but it is a floor, not a finished figure. The school district fee and the building permit and plan-check fees are not yet verified, so they are not in the total at all.

The water lines are the surprise second act. Redlands charges $2.3657 per square foot for water capital improvements plus $0.6633 for water stocks and rights — $2,424 on our example — which is more than half the impact-fee total. Sewer adds $0.2696 per square foot ($216). Water and sewer together come to $2,640, the largest utility bill in this series so far for an 800 sq ft ADU. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Redlands does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## Eight lines, one ordinance, one cliff

Every city in this series charges ADU impact fees, and most of them scatter the lines across several documents and several methods — flat, per-square-foot, pro-rata, percentage of valuation. Redlands puts all eight in Ordinance No. 2968, all per-square-foot, all with a 751 sq ft minimum. Open Space and Parks ($1.1794) and Transportation ($1.3954) together are $2.5748 per square foot — 47% of the $5.4405 total. The smallest line, Solid Waste at $0.0702, is $56.16 on our example: real money, but barely a rounding error against the whole.

The practical effect is a clean cliff. State law (Gov. Code §66311.5) already zeroes impact fees at 750 sq ft or less, but in most cities the cliff has to be assembled from a mix of flat and per-square-foot lines, some of which need converting. In Redlands the ordinance does the work: every line starts at 751 sq ft. Build at 750 and the impact-fee total is $0; build at 800 and it is $4,352.40. There is no ambiguity to argue with the counter about.

## Water is the second-biggest fee

The water lines deserve their own paragraph because they are easy to miss and hard to ignore. Water Capital Improvement ($2.3657/sq ft) is the single largest per-square-foot rate in Redlands after Transportation — and the Acquisition of Water Stocks and Water Rights fee ($0.6633/sq ft) is a line no other city in this series charges. Together they are $2,423.20 on 800 sq ft. Add the Sewer Capital Improvement fee ($215.68) and water and sewer come to $2,638.88 — the largest utility bill we have published for an 800 sq ft ADU.

Unlike impact fees, water and sewer charges are not covered by SB 1117. Connection and capacity charges stay as the city sets them, and the 750 sq ft cliff does not apply to them. The 2027 rule changes the impact-fee column only.

## Two gaps that matter

This total is a floor, and the two gaps are the reason. We have no verified school district fee for Redlands: the Redlands Unified School District rate is not in our data, and at the $5.38 state maximum it would add $4,304. We have no verified building permit or plan-check fee either — no rate table, no flat fee, nothing to price. Both are real costs a homeowner will pay; neither is in the $309,000. When they are verified, this post will be updated and the total will rise.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Redlands is the cleanest case in the series for this rule — every impact line is per-square-foot, so the 2027 saving is exactly 750 × $5.4405, about **$4,080** on an 800 sq ft ADU. See the full comparison at [Redlands: 2026 vs 2027, line by line](/sb-1117/redlands/). School fees, permit fees and water/sewer charges are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and do not change.
