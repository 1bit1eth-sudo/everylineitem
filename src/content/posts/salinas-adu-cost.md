Every city in this series has a 750 sq ft cliff — except the ones that deleted the cliff entirely. Salinas is the first: **$0 in development and traffic impact fees on ADUs, at any size.** Not reduced, not pro-rated, not deferred to 2027. Zero, by written city policy, published on the city's official ADU page.

That single policy decision rearranges the entire fee picture. There is no 749-vs-800 optimization to run, no pro-rata math against the primary dwelling, no SB 1117 countdown that matters — the law arriving January 2027 changes nothing in Salinas, because there was never a cliff to remove. Our 800 sq ft benchmark lands at **about $316,000, or $395 per square foot**, and the money sits in different lines than anywhere else we have published.

## The valuation trap

Salinas's permit is valuation-based, and valuation is where the honest uncertainty lives. Our $6,226 example uses the Building Valuation Data table's R-3 Type VB rate ($170.80/sq ft → $136,640 valuation). Change the construction type and the valuation — and every fee derived from it — moves. A heavier build (Type III) raises the permit; the city, not this post, sets your valuation. Budget the permit as a range, not a number.

The city's own pre-approved plans hint at the workaround: two of the four designs (749 and 746 sq ft) sit just under 750. The cliff is meaningless for DIF in Salinas — but the **permit tiers and the school-fee math still reward smaller footprints**, and the plan drawers knew it.

It is the clearest demonstration in the series that **the cliff is a policy choice** — and that deleting it does not make ADUs free, it just moves the money to lines the city still controls.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: marginal impact fees above 750 sq ft. In Salinas, the marginal rate applies to a $0 base — nothing changes. School fees ($4,136) are outside SB 1117 and unchanged.
