Ontario is the first Inland Empire city in this series, and it lands with the most expensive total we have published: **$328,000**. The reason is a single line — the development impact fee. At $37,595 per dwelling in the General City area and $58,576 in Ontario Ranch, Ontario's DIF schedule is the heaviest base figure in the series, and even after the legal proration for ADUs, an 800 sq ft unit carries roughly $18,798 of it. That one fee is nearly half of all soft costs.

But Ontario's fee materials are also among the best-documented in the series. Ordinance 3296 (adopted February 2026) writes the 750 sq ft cliff directly into the development code — no guessing at exemptions, no relying on a fee bulletin. The school fee combines two districts' published rates. The permit table is current (effective August 2026) and the arithmetic is shown. Two gaps remain: the $6,955 sewer assessment (confirmed as a figure, unconfirmed in its application to ADUs) is excluded from the total, and there are no pre-approved plans at all — the vendor program page reads "COMING SOON." Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Ontario does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## The DIF is the heavyweight

Ontario's development impact fee schedule reads like two cities' worth. The General City DIF breakdown totals **$37,595 per detached dwelling**; Ontario Ranch — the city's master-planned growth area — totals **$58,576**, roughly 56% higher. Both are collected at building permit, both at 100% phase since July 1, 2026 after a phase-in that started at 50% in 2025. For ADUs at 750 sq ft or larger, the code prorates by square footage against the primary dwelling: our 800 sq ft example behind a 1,600 sq ft house pays half. The General City prorated figure, ~$18,798, is the largest single ADU impact fee in the series — larger than most cities' entire soft-cost stacks. And the Ontario Ranch premium is worth flagging separately: the same ADU there pays ~$29,288, a $10,490 difference driven entirely by which side of the Ranch boundary the lot sits on. Our published total uses General City; an Ontario Ranch build would land near $338,000.

## The cliff, written into code

Ordinance 3296, adopted February 18, 2026, puts the 750 sq ft cliff where it can't be misread: Development Code Section 5.03.010.G. Two things matter beyond the headline. First, the section defines "impact fee" narrowly — for this rule, only Mitigation Fee Act and Quimby Act fees count, which explicitly excludes water and sewer connection or capacity charges. Second, G.2 adds that ADUs created by converting existing space incur no new connection or capacity charges at all, and that any connection or capacity charge for other ADUs must be proportionate to the ADU's burden (by floor area or drainage fixture units). That proportionality rule is exactly why the $6,955 flat sewer assessment sits in the fee autopsy as unconfirmed: the city publishes the number but not its ADU application, and the code says the application must be proportionate. Until the city says how, the line stays out of the total.

## $4,192: the two-district school fee

Most of Ontario pays school fees to two districts: Ontario-Montclair for K–8 ($3.57/sq ft) and Chaffey Joint Union High for 9–12 ($1.67/sq ft) — $5.24 combined, or **$4,192** on 800 sq ft. The OMSD schedule (effective June 2024) is notable for naming "Accessory Dwelling Unit — Level 1" as its own line item. The territory split is the caveat: Chino Valley USD covers southern Ontario and Ontario Ranch at a unified $5.38/sq ft ($4,304), the Cucamonga study combines $3.71 + $1.67 to the same $5.38, and Mountain View SD's current rate could not be found on any official source (only a small eastern territory, so the dominant-case figure stands). Note the timing wrinkle: Chaffey's $1.67 rate was adopted July 14, 2026 and effective September 13, 2026 — permits issued before that date may sit under the prior rate.

## ~$3,013: the permit, done the long way

Ontario prices permits the old-fashioned way: valuation times a table. The current Citywide Fee Schedule (as of July 1, 2026, effective August 22, 2026) charges $1,288 plus $7.00 per $1,000 of valuation above $100,000 for the $100,001–$500,000 band; plan check is 80% of the permit fee; issuance is $50; and the state's SMIP earthquake fee adds 0.0001 × valuation. At a $150,000 valuation — the arithmetic, not a published flat: permit $1,638, plan check $1,310.40, issuance $50, earthquake $15 — the line lands at **$3,013.40**. Because Ontario's real permit floats with declared valuation, this figure carries an asterisk. It supersedes the 2025 schedule's lower figures, and a third-party aggregator's $993.75 base figure contradicts the official schedule and was not used.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Ontario's proportional formula — (ADU sq ft / primary sq ft) × dwelling fee — cannot survive that math unchanged: an 800 sq ft ADU behind a 1,600 sq ft house would owe fees on 50 sq ft, not 800, which collapses the ~$18,798 to a fraction of itself. This is the largest SB 1117 swing in the series, because Ontario's base DIF is the largest in the series. We found no mention of SB 1117 in city materials; the January 2027 quarterly review will check for a revised DIF breakdown. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and stay at $4,192.
