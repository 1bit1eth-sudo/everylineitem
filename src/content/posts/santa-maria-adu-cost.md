Santa Maria builds more ADUs than almost any city in California — and tells you the least about what they cost to permit.

The HCD numbers: **1,318 ADU permits from 2022 through 2024**, seventh statewide, in a city of about 110,000 people. The 2024 ordinance is modern and well-drafted — a 750 sq ft impact-fee cliff, proportional water/sewer charges, ADU sizes up to 1,000 sq ft. But the 2026 User Fee Schedule (274 fee types, adopted June 2026) publishes no readable residential building-permit dollar table, no plan-check rate, and no impact-fee dollar schedule we could find. The city that says yes to ADUs most often makes the fee math hardest to do from your desk.

So this post is different from the others in the series. The six-phase total below — **$306,000, or $383 per square foot** — is a **floor, not a finished number.** It includes everything we could verify: the $14,000 design line and the $4,304 school fee. It excludes four lines we could not price from public documents — the building permit, plan check, development impact fees, and water/sewer capacity charges — each marked below with exactly what we found and what we didn't. When those numbers surface, this post gets updated and the change gets logged.

## The fee autopsy: $18,000 of verified lines, four lines missing

**School fees: $4,304 (verified) — with a $7,984 shadow.** Santa Maria is the only city in the series served by **two** school districts: Santa Maria-Bonita (elementary, K-8) and Santa Maria Joint Union (high school, 9-12), splitting the Level 1 fee 71.43/28.57 under a [formal split agreement](https://content.myconnectsuite.com/api/documents/d52d15c7db1d495188d1380fe594271c.pdf). The verified baseline is the 2026 Level 1 statutory rate: **$5.38 per square foot** × 800 = **$4,304**. But both districts have *justified* Level 2 fees in past School Facility Needs Analyses — $7.02 (elementary, Feb 2025) and $2.96 (high school, Feb 2023) — which would total **$9.98 × 800 = $7,984**, the highest school-fee line in the series. Level 2 requires annual re-adoption by each board, and we found no current adoption resolutions; both analyses' validity periods have expired. So $4,304 is the number in the total, and $7,984 lives here as a conditional: *if both districts have adopted Level 2, add $3,680.* Call the districts' facilities offices before you budget — this is the single largest swing in the Santa Maria model.

**Building permit: verify with the city.** The [2026 User Fee Schedule](https://www.cityofsantamaria.org/home/showpublisheddocument/26642/639215444670700000) (274 fee types, effective August 1, 2026) exists and was adopted by council — but the residential new-construction dollar tiers could not be read from the public documents we found. Not estimated, not in the total.

**Plan check: verify with the city.** No plan-check rate (as a percentage of permit fee or otherwise) surfaced in public documents. Not estimated, not in the total.

**Development impact fees: structure confirmed, dollars missing.** The [2024 ADU ordinance](https://pub-santamaria.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=6291) (Section 11) is explicit: **no DIF for ADUs under 750 sq ft**; ADUs of **750 sq ft or more** pay fees **"proportionately in relation to the square footage of the primary dwelling unit,"** with amounts set by adopted fee resolutions. An 800 sq ft ADU always triggers the fee — but the fee resolutions with dollar amounts could not be located. Not estimated, not in the total.

**Water and sewer: proportional burden, dollars missing.** The same ordinance (Section 10) requires connection fees and capacity charges for new ADUs to be **"proportionate to the burden of the unit on the water or sewer system based on either its square feet or the number of its drainage fixture unit (DFU) values."** This is the series' only proportional-burden structure — not Oakland's $0, not Glendale's $0-by-ordinance, not a flat fee. The rate schedule with dollar amounts could not be located. Not estimated, not in the total.

**Design and engineering: $14,000 (carried).** The series-standard design line. We could not confirm a city-operated pre-approved ADU plan program from official sources — only a third-party mention of plan connections at a city resource fair. Full custom-design assumption stands.

## The 750 square foot cliff

| Fee at 800 sq ft | Santa Maria | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Development impact fees | **Verify with city** | Under 750: $0 by ordinance; 750+: main-house proportional |
| School district developer fees | **$4,304** (Level 1) | Two districts; Level 2 conditional at $7,984 |
| Water / sewer capacity charges | **Verify with city** | Proportional to burden (sq ft or DFU) — series' only such structure |
| Building permit + plan check | **Verify with city** | 2026 fee schedule exists; residential tiers unreadable |

Santa Maria's cliff is confirmed in ordinance but unpriced in practice — the worst combination for a builder. You know the rule (750 sq ft) and the mechanism (main-house proportionality) but not the number. Until the fee resolutions surface, the only honest advice is to get the DIF figure in writing from the Building Division before finalizing plans.

Starting 2027-01-01, [SB 1117](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB1117) changes the statewide rule so impact fees on the first two ADUs apply only to square footage *above* 750 — which would convert Santa Maria's main-house-proportional structure to a marginal one. The city's 2024 ordinance predates the law and will need updating; flagged for the December quarterly re-verification.

## What moves the number most in Santa Maria

**1. The four missing fee lines.** The building permit, plan check, DIF, and water/sewer charges are all real, all unpriced here. Together they could add several thousand dollars to the $306,000 floor. Get all four in writing from the Building Division — this post's total moves more on these than on any design decision.

**2. The school-fee Level 2 question.** $4,304 versus $7,984 is a $3,680 swing on a single line — the largest conditional in the series. Two phone calls (one per district's facilities office) resolve it.

**3. Your main house's size.** The DIF scales with primary-dwelling square footage. Larger main house, larger fee — get the proportion from the fee resolution.

**4. Whether the fee schedule becomes readable.** The 2026 User Fee Schedule exists; its residential tiers may simply need an in-person read at the permit counter. If you obtain the dollar tables, send them — see [Corrections](/corrections/).

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does an ADU cost in Santa Maria in 2026?**

About $306,000 for an 800 sq ft detached, single-story, new-construction unit with mid-grade finishes in Santa Maria — roughly $383 per square foot. That is our modeled floor: the building permit, plan check, impact fees, and water/sewer charges are excluded because they could not be priced from public documents. Construction phases are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline because Santa Maria does not publish phase-level construction costs.

**Does Santa Maria charge impact fees on ADUs?**

ADUs under 750 square feet are fully exempt under the city's 2024 ordinance. ADUs of 750 square feet or more pay fees proportional to the main dwelling's size — but the dollar amounts come from fee resolutions we could not locate, so the 800 sq ft figure is not in our total. Starting 2027, SB 1117 converts this to marginal taxation on square footage above 750.

**Do I pay school district fees on an ADU in Santa Maria?**

Yes — at least $4,304. Two districts serve the city (elementary and high school), and the verified Level 1 rate is $5.38 per square foot on 800 sq ft. Both districts have justified higher Level 2 fees in the past ($7.02 and $2.96), which would total $7,984 — but current adoption was not confirmed, so that figure is conditional.

**Why is Santa Maria's total lower than other cities?**

It isn't, necessarily — the $306,000 is a floor, not a comparable total. Four fee lines that other cities' totals include are excluded here because they couldn't be priced from public documents. Once those numbers surface, this total will rise. Read the exclusions before comparing.
