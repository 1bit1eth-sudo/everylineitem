Beverly Hills is the most expensive city in this series by reputation, and the cheapest by fee schedule. **Water capacity $0. Wastewater capacity $0. Planning fee for a building-permit-only ADU $0.** The FY 2025–26 master fee schedule prices all three at zero — and there is no impact-fee line for an ADU anywhere in it. What the city does charge is paperwork: a **$1,416 covenant recordation fee**, a $447 certificate of occupancy, and three small percentage-of-valuation fees that together come to about $649. Our six-phase total lands at **$305,000, or $381 per square foot** — but it is a floor. The school district fee is not yet verified, so it is not in the total at all.

The valuation assumption does real work here. Three fees are percentages of construction valuation — General Plan Maintenance at 0.187%, the Building Standards Commission fee (SB 1473) at 0.004%, and the Contractor's Business Tax Registration at 0.2125% — and the city does not publish a valuation for our example. We use **$161,120, the assumed valuation from the Torrance post**, and label it wherever it appears. On that valuation the three fees come to about $301, about $6 and about $342. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Beverly Hills does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## The $0 city

Three zeroes in a row is the story of this post. The water capacity charge and water supply fee: $0. The wastewater capacity fee: $0. The planning fee for a building-permit-only ADU: $0. All three are printed as $0 in the FY 2025–26 master fee schedule — not waived for ADUs, not missing from the schedule, priced at zero.

The water and wastewater zeroes are partly state law doing its work. Gov. Code §66311.5 bars a city from requiring a new or separate water or sewer connection for an ADU on an existing single-family lot, so an ADU that shares the existing service has nothing to pay on those lines in any city. Beverly Hills is unusual in printing the $0 rather than leaving the line out. The planning $0 is different: it is a city choice. A building-permit-only ADU — one that needs no discretionary review — pays no planning fee at all.

## The $1,416 covenant

If the city gives with one hand, it takes with the other, and the taking is a single line: **ADU covenant processing, $1,416**. The covenant is the recorded agreement that ties the ADU to the lot — the document that says this unit is an ADU, with the use restrictions that follow. Beverly Hills charges $1,416 to process and record it, more than three times the $447 certificate of occupancy and more than fifteen times the $92 permit issuance fee. It is the largest single flat fee in the post, and it is pure paperwork: no inspection, no capacity, no square footage. Every ADU in Beverly Hills pays it.

The rest of the permit column is small change by comparison. Permit issuance $92, the state SB 1186 disability-access fee $4, the vandalism remediation fee $35, the certificate of occupancy $447 — $578 before the covenant, $1,994 after it. The three valuation-based fees add about $649 on the assumed valuation. The whole permit column is about $2,643, which is less than the school fee alone in most cities in this series.

## Valuation does the work

Three fees are percentages of construction valuation, and the valuation is the one number in this post we do not have from the city. We use **$161,120 — the assumed valuation from the Torrance post** (800 sq ft × $201.40) — and every fee computed from it is marked with a tilde. On that valuation: General Plan Maintenance at 0.187% is about $301; the Building Standards Commission fee (SB 1473) at 0.004% is about $6; the Contractor's Business Tax Registration at 0.2125% is about $342.

If the real valuation is higher, these three fees rise with it — but they are small percentages, so the movement is small. A $50,000 higher valuation adds about $94 to the General Plan fee, $2 to the SB 1473 fee and $106 to the contractor tax: about $200 on a $305,000 total. The valuation assumption is not the risk in this post. The missing school fee is: at the $5.38 state maximum it would add $4,304.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Beverly Hills has no verified impact-fee lines, so SB 1117 changes nothing we can compute here — there is nothing to reduce. See the full comparison at [Beverly Hills: 2026 vs 2027, line by line](/sb-1117/beverly-hills/). School fees, permit fees and water/sewer charges are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and do not change.
