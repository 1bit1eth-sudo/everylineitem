The pitch for pre-approved ADU plans sounds like a shortcut past the hardest part of building: pick a plan the city already reviewed, skip the design bill, skip the plan-check queue. The reality, on the cities' own pages, is narrower and still useful. A pre-approved plan buys you a reviewed set of construction drawings. It does not buy you a reviewed lot. Every program below still checks your site, and one of them just demonstrated how a code update can shrink a whole catalog overnight.

## Three programs, checked against the cities' own pages

| City | What is pre-approved | Time claim (city's words, paraphrased) | Fee claim | The catch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| San José | Vendor-designed plans from 5 listed vendors, 476–799 sq ft, reviewed by city staff for code compliance, with a dedicated review team | Permits may issue the same day as the scheduled plan review meeting, if the site-specific plan and documents are accurate and complete | Described as the fastest and lowest-cost permit process; no dollar figure published | Lot must have a single-family home or duplex and must not be in a geohazard, landslide, flood, or Wildland-Urban Interface zone; deferred structural submittals, clearances, or variances take longer |
| Oakland | Off-the-shelf studio, 1-bedroom, and 2-bedroom plans, complete construction drawings conforming to the latest codes | Once all fees are paid and all issues resolved, the building permit issues for construction within 10 business days | Reduced permit fee listed as a benefit; no amount published | Pre-approved plans may not be an option in certain neighborhoods or districts; separate school district developer fees may still be owed before permits issue |
| Pasadena | One city-designed plan compliant with the 2025 code: a 374 sq ft studio, free of charge | Plan-check review takes about 3 to 4 weeks; resubmittal review about 2 weeks | The plan itself is free; no plan-check discount is stated on the page | Modifications void the pre-review and trigger a full plan review at standard fees; all prior plans expired December 31, 2025 and are now limited to Eaton Fire red-tagged properties |

Two patterns matter more than any single row. First, no city in this table publishes what a pre-approved plan saves in dollars — only that the process is faster, cheaper, or both. Second, the time claims all carry a completeness condition: same-day and 10-business-day figures assume your site-specific documents are accurate and complete. An incomplete site plan costs you the very speed you picked the program for.

## What "pre-approved" never covers

A pre-approved plan is a non-site-specific drawing. The site-specific half of the application is still yours:

- **A site plan** showing the ADU's location, setbacks, utilities, and existing structures. San José requires site-specific documents showing location, utility connections, and setbacks; Oakland requires a site plan showing existing structures, easements, utilities, and the proposed location.
- **Zoning and district fit.** Oakland's planning bureau reviews whether a pre-approved plan works for your location, and notes it may not be an option in certain neighborhoods or districts. San José excludes geohazard, landslide, flood, and Wildland-Urban Interface lots outright.
- **Fees the plan doesn't touch.** Oakland notes separate school district developer fees may be owed before permits issue. School fees, impact fees, and utility connection charges are assessed on your project, not waived by your choice of drawings. Our [750 sq ft cliff guide](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/) covers the impact-fee side; our [permit timeline](/posts/adu-permit-timeline/) covers what the clock actually looks like.
- **Corrections.** Oakland's process assumes corrections happen: if plans require corrections or more information, the city contacts you and the clock moves. Pre-approval shortens the design review; it does not abolish it.

## The 750 sq ft connection — and why catalog sizes cluster there

Look at the sizes in San José's approved list: 476, 499, 747, 748, and 749 sq ft, with two plans just over at 795 and 799. Several sit just under 750 sq ft, where California law exempts ADUs from impact fees entirely. That is not a coincidence to ignore, but it is also not a promise: the exemption turns on the unit's livable area, and a plan labeled 749 sq ft still has to be built at 749. If your site forces a bump over 750, the cliff math from our [Oakland post](/posts/oakland-adu-cost/) and the cliff guide applies in full. Pick the plan size with the fee rule in mind, not just the floor plan.

## The Pasadena reset: check the code cycle first

Pasadena is the cautionary tale in this post. Its standard plans were a real program — until the 2025 California Building Code took effect on January 1, 2026, and the prior plans expired the day before. Since then, the only generally usable city plan is the single new 2025-compliant studio, with additional plans promised as they are approved. Anyone who picked a Pasadena standard plan from an old blog post or video in 2026 picked a plan the city will no longer accept for a general lot.

The lesson generalizes: a pre-approved catalog is only as current as the code cycle it was reviewed under. Before you commit to a plan — or pay a vendor for one — confirm on the city's current program page that the plan is approved under the current code, not a prior one. A plan that was pre-approved in 2024 is not automatically pre-approved now.

## Four questions to ask before you pick a plan

1. **Is this plan approved under the current code?** Ask for the code cycle, not just the plan name. Pasadena's reset is what happens when the answer is last cycle's.
2. **Does my lot qualify?** Zones, hazard areas, and districts can exclude a lot from the program entirely. Check before you fall in love with a floor plan.
3. **What site documents will I still need?** Budget the site plan, and any survey, geotechnical, or utility work your lot requires. That work is not in the plan's price.
4. **What fee, exactly, is reduced — and by how much?** If the city page doesn't state it, treat a vendor's savings figure as a sales claim, not a city figure. Oakland's reduced fee and San José's lowest-cost description are real claims with no published amount behind them on the program pages.

## The bottom line

Pre-approved plans are worth using when your lot qualifies and you want the plan you see, unmodified. San José's same-day possibility and Oakland's 10-business-day window are the strongest time claims any California ADU program makes in this post — but both are conditioned on complete site documents, and neither publishes a dollar saving. Pasadena's single free studio is genuinely free, and genuinely the only generally usable city plan there right now. Use the catalog, verify the code cycle, and budget the site work the plan doesn't cover.
