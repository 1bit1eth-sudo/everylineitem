The most expensive misunderstanding in ADU budgeting is a category error: treating every fee a utility charges as an "impact fee." It is not. California law puts utility connection fees and capacity charges in a separate bucket from impact fees, with a separate test — and the 750 sq ft cliff that dominates every ADU fee conversation does not apply to that bucket at all. A 600 sq ft detached ADU owes no impact fee, but it can still owe a sewer capacity charge. A garage conversion owes neither. The difference is the statute, not the size.

## The statute: section 66311.5, not 66324

The governing section is **Government Code section 66311.5**. Many guides still cite section 66324 — that number was correct in 2025, when SB 477's recodification put the ADU fee rules there. SB 543 (Chapter 520, Statutes of 2025, effective January 1, 2026) renumbered section 66324 as section 66311.5, and the leginfo page carries that history on its face. If a guide cites section 66324 without the renumbering note, it is working from last year's numbering; the rules below are the current text, checked on leginfo on 4 November 2026.

The section draws one definitional line that controls everything else: an "impact fee" does not include a connection fee or capacity charge. So the impact-fee rules — no impact fee at or under 750 sq ft, and from January 1, 2027, SB 1117's excess-area-only calculation for up to two ADUs — say nothing about what a water or sewer provider may charge for connecting the unit. Connection and capacity charges are governed by three subdivisions of the same section, and they turn on how the ADU was built.

## Bucket 1: inside existing space — no connection, no charge

Subdivision (d) covers an ADU or JADU created within the existing space of a single-family dwelling or an existing accessory structure (the section 66323(a)(1) category, which allows up to 150 sq ft of expansion for ingress and egress only). For those units, a local agency or special district may not require a new or separate utility connection, and may not impose a related connection fee or capacity charge.

Two exceptions lift that bar: the unit was constructed with a new single-family dwelling, or the ADU is separately conveyed under section 66342 (the AB 1033 separate-sale path). A garage conversion built alongside an existing house, and not separately sold, is the clean case: no new connection, no capacity charge, as a matter of statute. If a district bills one anyway, the question to ask is which exception it claims — and to see it in writing.

## Bucket 2: new detached ADUs — a charge is allowed, but proportionate

Subdivision (e) covers ADUs outside that category — in practice, most new detached units. Here a new or separate utility connection may be required. But the charge has two statutory caps, and both matter at budgeting time:

1. **Proportionality.** The charge must be proportionate to the burden of the ADU, based on either its square feet or the number of its drainage fixture unit (DFU) values. A district that bills a detached ADU the same flat connection charge as a new single-family house has to explain how that figure is proportionate to a smaller unit with fewer fixtures.
2. **Reasonable cost.** The charge must not exceed the reasonable cost of providing the service.

Subdivision (b) reinforces the point from the other direction: for calculating connection fees or capacity charges for utilities, including water and sewer service, an ADU or JADU is not considered a new residential use — unless, again, it was constructed with a new single-family dwelling. The "full new-home charge" is therefore the exception, not the default, and it applies only to units built together with a new house.

One limit on this post's claims: section 66311.5 speaks of a new or separate utility *connection*. It does not grant a freestanding right to a separate *meter*. Meter policy is set by the serving utility or city. Budget a separate-meter capacity fee only if the utility confirms one applies to your configuration.

## Bucket 3: impact fees are a different calculation — and SB 1117 doesn't cross over

Because the two buckets are so often conflated, the interaction with SB 1117 deserves a direct statement. SB 1117 (Chapter 809, Statutes of 2026, effective January 1, 2027) amends only the impact-fee subdivision, section 66311.5(c)(1): for up to two ADUs on a lot, an impact fee on an ADU over 750 sq ft is charged proportionately and only on the area in excess of 750 sq ft; for lots with more than two ADUs, the old full-area proportional calculation survives. It does not amend subdivisions (b), (d), or (e). A detached ADU over 750 sq ft in 2027 therefore gets the new impact-fee math *and* still faces a proportional connection or capacity charge where a separate connection is required — plus school fees on area over 500 sq ft, plus permit and plan-check fees. Anyone who tells you SB 1117 "eliminates ADU utility fees" has merged the buckets.

## Four questions to ask your district before you budget

1. **Which bucket is my unit in?** Within existing space under section 66323(a)(1), or a new detached unit? The answer decides whether the charge can exist at all.
2. **What is the basis — square feet or DFU count?** Ask for the calculation in writing. A flat new-home charge with no proportionality showing is the figure to challenge.
3. **Is a separate connection actually required, and is a separate meter?** Those are different questions, decided by different authorities. Get both answers, separately.
4. **Which charges are impact fees and which are connection or capacity charges?** If a bill blends them, the 750 sq ft cliff and SB 1117 apply only to the impact-fee portion. The rest lives under subdivisions (b), (d), and (e).

## The bottom line

| ADU configuration | New/separate connection required? | Connection fee / capacity charge? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Within existing space (§ 66323(a)(1)): garage conversion, interior conversion | **No** — barred by § 66311.5(d) | **$0** — barred, except units built with a new single-family dwelling or separately conveyed under § 66342 |
| New detached ADU (outside § 66323(a)(1)) | **May be required** — § 66311.5(e) | **Yes, but proportionate** — by sq ft or DFU values, capped at reasonable cost; never a full new-home charge by default |
| Any ADU, for impact-fee purposes | — | Separate bucket: 750 sq ft exemption today; SB 1117 excess-area-only math from Jan 1, 2027 (up to two ADUs) — neither changes connection/capacity charges |

The one sentence to carry into a district office is the statute's proportionality cap: a charge on a detached ADU must be "proportionate to the burden of the proposed accessory dwelling unit." If the number on your bill cannot be traced to your unit's square feet or its fixture count, ask for the trace — that is the test the statute actually sets.
