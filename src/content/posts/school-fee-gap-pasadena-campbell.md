Same ADU. Same 800 square feet. Same state. **$2,528 apart** — and the difference comes from a line on a map you cannot see from the street.

School district boundaries, not city limits, set your ADU school fee. Pasadena and Campbell are 350 miles apart, but the lesson is local: in many California cities, two neighboring parcels can sit in different districts and pay different rates on identical ADUs.

## The two numbers

**Pasadena — $1,792.** Pasadena Unified School District charges **$2.24 per square foot** for residential construction (PUSD Developer Fee Instructions, revised July 1, 2025). On 800 sq ft: 800 × $2.24 = **$1,792**. That is the lowest school fee in our entire series — less than half the state Level 1 maximum. PUSD has not raised its rate in years.

**Campbell — $4,320.** Campbell sits in two districts that stack:
- Campbell Union School District (K–8): **$3.79/sq ft** → $3,032
- Campbell Union High School District: **$1.61/sq ft** → $1,288 (Level 1, approved May 7, 2026, effective July 7, 2026)
- Combined: **$5.40/sq ft** → **$4,320** on 800 sq ft

**The gap: $4,320 − $1,792 = $2,528.**

To put it in perspective: $2,528 is more than the entire building permit in Fontana ($3,195 covers most of it). It is a rounding error on a $320,000 build — 0.8% — but it is decided by nothing you control: not your design, not your finishes, not your contractor. Just which district's boundary your parcel falls inside.

## Why the rate varies

School developer fees are set by **school district boards**, not cities, under Education Code §17620. The mechanics:

1. **Level 1 (the cap):** The State Allocation Board sets a maximum per-square-foot rate, adjusted for inflation every two years. For 2026 it is **$5.38/sq ft**. Any district can charge up to this by board resolution, no special justification needed.
2. **Level 2 (above the cap):** Districts can exceed Level 1 only with a nexus study proving higher facility needs. Few bother.
3. **Stacking:** Where elementary and high school districts are separate — common in California — each levies its own fee. Campbell's $5.40 total is two districts ($3.79 + $1.61), each under the cap individually.
4. **The city is a cashier.** Cities collect school fees at permit issuance and pass them to the district. The city cannot waive, reduce, or negotiate them.

Pasadena's $2.24 is unusual because PUSD simply has not chased the cap. Most districts in our series cluster at $4.79–$5.38. The rate reflects each board's politics and facility needs, not your ADU.

## The 500 sq ft exemption

One statewide constant: **ADUs of 500 square feet or less pay $0** in school fees (Ed. Code §17620; confirmed in PUSD's instructions and the state ADU handbook). This is separate from the 750 sq ft impact-fee cliff — a smaller ADU dodges both, but the thresholds differ.

A 500 sq ft ADU in Campbell saves $2,700 in school fees versus an 800 sq ft one. In Pasadena it saves $1,120. The exemption is worth more where the rate is higher — another way district boundaries quietly shape your pro forma.

## What this means for your project

- **Check the district, not just the city.** Campbell parcels can fall in Cambrian or Moreland districts instead — different rates. Lakewood spans four districts ($2.97–$5.38/sq ft). Your city's published "school fee" may be wrong for your parcel.
- **The fee is non-negotiable.** Unlike impact fees (which cities can waive) or permit fees (which vary by model), school fees are set by an elected school board and collected as a pass-through. Budget it as fixed.
- **Size matters at two thresholds.** Under 500 sq ft: $0. Over 500: full rate on every square foot. There is no phase-in.

## The bottom line

$2,528 for the same 800 sq ft of housing, decided by a boundary line. School fees are the least-discussed of the big three soft costs — and the one you have the least power to change. Know your district before you budget.
