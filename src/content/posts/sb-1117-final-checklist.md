Twenty-five days. On January 1, 2027, the most expensive sentence in California ADU law gets rewritten — and the window between "knowing the new math" and "being assessed under it" is now measured in weeks. This is the final checklist: four actions, in order, before the law switches on.

## The law in one paragraph

[SB 1117](/posts/sb-1117-explained-plain-english/) (Chapter 809, Statutes of 2026), signed September 29, 2026, amends Government Code §66311.5. Effective January 1, 2027, impact fees on a homeowner's **first two ADUs** on a lot may be imposed **only on the livable square footage above 750** — marginal, the way tax brackets work, instead of the all-or-nothing [750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/) that charged the full per-foot rate on the entire unit. An 800 sq ft ADU gets assessed on 50 sq ft. The cliff dies; the bracket is born. ([The full mechanics](/posts/sb-1117-kills-750-sq-ft-cliff/).)

## Action 1 — Confirm your city's marginal-rate table

The statute sets the rule; **each city writes the implementation.** Before January:

- Pull your city's current fee schedule and look for 2027 language applying the above-750 marginal math.
- If your city uses a **flat impact fee** (Torrance's $2,243.47), ask how it becomes marginal — there is no published per-foot rate to slice, and the treatment is unconfirmed pending city language.
- If your city charges **per-unit fees**, same question — per-unit charges are not per-square-foot charges, and their treatment is unconfirmed.
- Confirm the **first-two-ADUs** definition as your city applies it: the statute's protection is per lot, not per owner lifetime.

Our [December re-verification](/posts/december-2026-fee-reverification/) is tracking city implementation language across all 23 published cities; confirmed findings land there as they are verified.

## Action 2 — Lock your school-fee number (SB 1117 doesn't touch it)

School developer fees live under the **Education Code**, not §66311.5. They are outside SB 1117 and **unchanged on January 1**. Pasadena's $1,792 and Campbell's $4,320 survive the new law exactly as published.

That makes the school fee the one line you can finalize today with zero timing risk. Find your parcel's district (not your city — see the [full school fee ranking](/posts/adu-school-fees-ranked-california/)), confirm the district's current board-adopted rate, and check for two-district stacking. It is also the one line your city cannot waive, so there is no strategic reason to wait.

## Action 3 — Start the utility timeline now

[SB 1196](/posts/sb-1196-utility-hookup-backlog/) — effective the same day, January 1, 2027 — orders the CPUC to set hard utility-connection deadlines with penalties, and lets you file the hookup request alongside the building permit. But the current reality is a **9–12 month wait** after construction for the connection, and the new deadlines phase in; they do not erase the existing queue.

The action: file the utility hookup request as early as your utility allows — alongside the permit application if they accept it — rather than after final inspection. A finished ADU with no power is rent you cannot collect, and no fee-law change fixes a queue you joined late.

## Action 4 — Run the permit-timing math

The decision most readers face: pull the permit in December under the old math, or wait for January's marginal math? The framework ([full analysis here](/posts/sb-1117-should-you-rush-permit/)):

- **Estimate your cliff height.** Our city-by-city arithmetic: Palo Alto ~$7,500–$18,750, Anaheim ~$9,750, Fullerton ~$6,000, Sacramento ~$5,370, Glendale ~$4,400, Riverside ~$1,390. In Salinas and Oakland, $0 — there was never a cliff. Torrance's flat fee: unconfirmed.
- **Price the delay.** Two months of waiting carry construction inflation, financing carry on land or pre-development costs, and the risk that your city's 2027 fee schedule moves other lines against you.
- **Remember the rule.** Permits pulled in 2026 are assessed under 2026 rules. There is no retroactive marginal treatment.

In Palo Alto, the savings can approach $20,000 — delay has a real prize. In Riverside, ~$1,390 may not survive two months of carry. The answer is city-specific, and the city's actual numbers beat any rule of thumb.
