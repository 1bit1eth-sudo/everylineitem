Since January 2020, California has required solar panels on most new homes. In the 2022 code cycle, the mandate was formally extended to ADUs — and if you are submitting a permit in 2026, you are under the 2025 Title 24 standards that took effect January 1, 2026. The surprise for most homeowners is not the requirement itself. It is how narrow it is: most ADU configurations are exempt, and the ones that are not have three well-defined exits.

## Which ADUs need solar: the three types

The Title 24 ADU fact sheet (the Energy Code Ace program's [Accessory Dwelling Units fact sheet for the 2022 Energy Code](https://buildinggeni.us/system/files/2024-11/FactSheet.SF_.ADU_.2022.pdf)) sorts every ADU into one of three energy-code types, and the photovoltaic requirement applies to exactly one of them:

| ADU type | What it is | PV required? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **New Construction ADU** | Detached, not attached to any other structure, entirely new construction | **Yes** |
| **Addition ADU** | Converting unconditioned space (e.g., a garage) or adding new conditioned area to an existing building | No |
| **Alteration ADU** | Converting existing permitted conditioned space (e.g., a bedroom) | No |

So the garage conversion, the basement conversion, and the JADU carved out of existing living space are all classified as alterations or additions — not new construction — and carry no solar mandate. The attached ADU added to the house is likewise an addition. Only the detached unit built from scratch triggers the requirement.

Two edge cases to handle carefully. **Manufactured ADUs** are regulated under Title 25 (HCD's factory-built housing program), not the California Building Code — so the Title 24 PV mandate does not apply to them, even though they are "new." And **detached conversions** (an existing detached garage turned into an ADU) are treated by industry guides as additions rather than new construction, which would exempt them — but the classification boundary between "new construction" and "addition" for a detached conversion is exactly the kind of question to confirm with your local building department, because enforcement is local.

## The three exits

Even a new detached ADU can escape the mandate three ways:

1. **The 1.8 kW floor.** Title 24 sizes the required system with Equation 150.1-C, using the ADU's conditioned floor area and its California climate zone. If the equation produces a required system smaller than 1.8 kW, no PV is required at all. Installer guides estimate this floor covers ADUs under roughly 600–700 square feet in most climate zones — treat that as a rule of thumb, not a code citation; the equation and your climate zone decide.
2. **The solar-access exit.** No PV is required if the roof offers fewer than 80 contiguous square feet of viable solar area — measured as Solar Access Roof Area (SARA) — because of shading from existing permanent natural or man-made barriers external to the dwelling: trees, hills, adjacent structures. This needs documentation with a CEC-approved solar assessment tool.
3. **The panels don't have to go on the ADU.** The required kilowatts can be installed anywhere on the property — including added to the primary home's existing array — or satisfied through a CEC-approved community solar program. If the main house already has panels, the ADU's requirement may already be met.

Battery storage is a separate question. Batteries are not mandated for most single-family ADUs; installer guides report that the 2025 code introduced battery-ready pre-wiring requirements (a dedicated circuit and reserved panel space for a future battery) — we have not verified that provision against the 2025 standards text, so confirm it in your Title 24 compliance report rather than budgeting from this sentence.

## What it costs: 2026 published figures

ADU-sized solar pricing is not published as its own category, so the honest approach is per-watt figures from 2026 California market data, applied to the 1.8–3.5 kW systems installer guides associate with 600–1,200 sq ft ADUs:

- **EnergySage (2026 marketplace data):** California averages **$2.30/W** — about $20,930 for a 9.1 kW system.
- **ConsumerAffairs (2026):** California averages **$2.44/W**, with a typical installation at $17,208.
- **Qcells (August 2026):** California sits at **$3.00–$4.00/W**, attributing the premium to labor and permitting costs — a 7 kW system at $21,000–$28,000.

Apply those per-watt figures to a 1.8–3.5 kW ADU system and the arithmetic gives roughly **$4,100–$14,000** before incentives. Two honest warnings about that band: small systems consistently price higher per watt than the averages above (fixed costs like permitting, design, and the utility interconnection spread over fewer watts), and installer quotes vary more by company than by equipment. Treat the band as a planning number and get the quote.

One market change to factor in: the **30% federal tax credit for purchased residential systems is gone** as of late 2025, per EnergySage's marketplace reporting — installer pricing held roughly flat through the change (median quoted prices rose about 3% from late 2025 to early 2026), but the effective cost to the homeowner rose because the credit disappeared. Any solar payback math written before 2025 needs redoing.

## The honest bottom line

For most ADU builders, the solar question resolves in one step: if the ADU is a conversion or an addition, there is no mandate, and the panels are a voluntary investment with a roughly 7-year payback in California at current rates. If it is a new detached unit, run the Title 24 equation — under ~600–700 sq ft you may fall below the 1.8 kW floor and owe nothing; above it, budget roughly $4,000–$14,000 for 4 to 8 panels, and remember they can go on the main house's roof instead. Either way, the requirement is one line in your Title 24 report, not a reason to redesign the project.

## Disclaimer

Energy-code summary, not legal or engineering advice. Title 24 is updated on a three-year cycle and local jurisdictions enforce it; confirm PV requirements, exemptions, and system sizing in your project's Title 24 compliance report before budgeting.
