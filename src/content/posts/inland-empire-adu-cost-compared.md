Thirty miles apart, three completely different theories of what an ADU should cost the city — and the homeowner. Riverside barely charges for the permit. Fontana's impact fee schedule starts at $20,563 per single-family unit. San Bernardino wrote the clearest 750 sq ft cliff in California code. This is the Inland Empire's ADU math, side by side.

## Three philosophies

**Riverside: the permit minimalist.** The $1,006 building permit is the lowest in the entire series — and the city pairs it with free pre-approved plans at exactly 800 sq ft, the benchmark size this series uses. No scaling down to dodge a cliff, no valuation surprises. Riverside's theory: make the paperwork cheap and the drawings free, and the ADUs will come. At $309,000 total, it's the series-low candidate among fully published cities.

**Fontana: the impact-fee maximalist.** The development impact fee schedule opens at **$20,563 per single-family dwelling unit** — then scales down proportionally for the ADU (roughly $10,967 on our 800 sq ft example). But Fontana also issues three pre-approved designs including a **738 sq ft plan** — twelve feet under the cliff, where the DIF line reads $0. The city's left hand charges; its right hand shows you the door. School fees at the $5.38 state ceiling ($4,304) complete the high-soft-cost picture, offset by a flat $3,195 permit with no valuation math.

**San Bernardino: the plain-language cliff.** Municipal Code §19.04.030 states the 750 sq ft rule in the clearest ordinance language we've found in California — no fee-schedule footnotes, no planner interpretation required. The DIF ($17,330.78 per single-family unit, scaled proportionally) and a rare area-based sewer formula round out a code-first approach: everything in the ordinance, nothing hidden in a spreadsheet.

## What the region tells you

**The Inland Empire is not "cheap" — it's bimodal.** Riverside's $1,006 permit and Fontana's $20,563 DIF schedule coexist in the same metro. The regional average means nothing; the parcel-specific fee sheet means everything.

**Free plans cluster here.** Two of the three cities offer free pre-approved ADU designs, and both sized them at or under the cliff (Riverside's 800 is the benchmark exception that proves the program's confidence). Inland Empire planning departments are competing on paperwork, not just fees.

**School fees are the constant.** All three cities sit at or near the $5.38 state ceiling — $4,304 on 800 sq ft, set by the school districts, untouched by any city policy. In the Inland Empire, the school fee is the floor everything else is built on.

## The bottom line

Pick Riverside for the cheapest permit in the series, Fontana if you can build at 738 sq ft and erase a five-figure DIF line, San Bernardino for the most readable code in California. Same region, same 800 sq ft box — three different answers to what it should cost. Read each city's full ledger before you pick yours.
