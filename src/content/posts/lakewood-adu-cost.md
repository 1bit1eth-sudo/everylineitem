In every other city in this series, the school fee is a number. In Lakewood, it is a question: **which street are you on?**

Lakewood — 9.5 square miles, ~80,000 people, due east of Long Beach — sits in **four school districts**. The same 800 sq ft ADU owes **$2,376** in the Paramount USD corner, **$3,832** in Bellflower USD territory (2022 rate), and **$4,304** in the Long Beach USD slice. That is a 2x spread inside one small city, the first multi-district case this series has published, and it makes the parcel — not the plan — the first variable in the budget.

Everything else about Lakewood is equally unusual. It is a **contract city**: LA County's Department of Public Works runs building permits, plan check, and inspections on the county's fee schedule. We found **no city development impact fee** — no DIF ordinance, no park fee, no Quimby schedule — consistent with Lakewood's historic low-fee posture, though the absence is not officially confirmed. Our 800 sq ft benchmark lands at **about $312,000–$314,000 (~$390–$393/sq ft)**, with the range driven by the school district lottery.

Paramount USD's $2.97 is the series' second-lowest confirmed school rate (after Pasadena's $2.24) — but it applies only to the district's Lakewood slice. The practical advice is blunt and non-negotiable: **verify your parcel's district with the county assessor before you budget.** Nothing else in this post matters if you use the wrong district's number.

## The contract-city permit

Lakewood does not run its own building department. Permits, plan review, and inspections come from **LA County Public Works** on the county's fee schedule — the city's 2025 RFP says so explicitly. What we confirmed: a $47.19 permit issuance fee and a $257.44 minimum plan check fee. What we could not: the valuation-table rows themselves (the county PDF 404s). The permit line is therefore the softest number in this post — use the county's DAPTS calculator or call the county before budgeting.

It is the series' best illustration of a general truth: **in California, the fee schedule that matters is often not the city's.**

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: marginal impact fees above 750 sq ft. With no city DIF found, Lakewood has little to rewrite — the state-law cliff remains the only cliff. School fees ($2,376–$4,304) are outside SB 1117 and unchanged.
