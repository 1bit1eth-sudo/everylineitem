An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Culver City costs at least **$309,954, or about $387 per square foot**, in our verified floor. That sentence is the whole post in miniature, and the word "floor" is doing the heavy lifting: this is the lowest total the city's own published documents support, not a prediction of what any one homeowner will pay. If you are comparing Culver City ADU fees with other cities in this series, use this number only alongside the exclusions below — the building permit, the park fee, and the utility charges are all real costs that this floor does not yet contain.

Culver City is unusual in this series for a happy reason: its documents actually say the word ADU. The planning fee schedule names accessory dwelling units in its plan-check tier, the mobility fee report gives ADUs their own rate line, and the ADU code names the exact impact fees an ADU of 750 square feet or more must pay. Where the city is silent — the park fee amount, the permit valuation, the utility connection — we say so, rather than filling the hole with another city's number.

### The school fee: $4,136, with a 2026 asterisk

The city's building permit fee schedule lists school fees for residential construction at $5.17 per square foot of new habitable area, applying at 500 square feet and over. An 800 sq ft ADU clears that threshold on the full area: 800 × $5.17 = $4,136. The schedule does not name the district in that line, but the fee database entry for Culver City attributes the rate to Culver City Unified, and the city collects the charge at permit stage. How school fees work generally is covered in [the school-fee explainer](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/). Note the schedule's date: it is marked revised September 2024, and the statewide Level I cap has since risen to $5.38. We searched for a Culver City Unified adoption of the higher rate and did not find one — other districts' 2026 adoptions are not evidence about this district. Until the city or district confirms the current rate, $5.17 stays in the floor and the $168 difference to $4,304 stays flagged. There is also a conflicting city figure to resolve: the FY2025-26 adopted comprehensive fee schedule lists a residential school fee of $4.20 per square foot, against $5.17 in the September 2024 permit schedule. The comprehensive schedule's commercial line ($1.03) exceeds the state Level I cap, which suggests that row may not be the statutory developer fee — but only the city or district can confirm which rate is collected at permit, and the fee database's CCUSD attribution for the $5.17 line itself rests on the comprehensive schedule's footnote rather than on a district adoption document. The school line therefore stays at $5.17 in the floor with this conflict flagged, not resolved.

### The mobility fee: $3,818, and the proportionality question

The city's development impact fee report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 2025 lists the mobility improvement fee by project type, and ADUs get their own line: $3,818 per dwelling unit, against $7,636 for a single unit. That is the figure in the floor total. The fee dates to June 2021 adoption, effective August 2021, and rests on a May 2021 nexus study.

But the ordinance complicates the flat rate: the ADU code requires an ADU of 750 square feet or more to pay all impact fees, including the mobility fee, scaled to the primary dwelling's size. The $3,818 ADU line and the scaling rule can both be true if the line already reflects a typical scaling — scaling the $7,636 single-unit rate by an 800 sq ft ADU against a 1,600 sq ft house gives exactly $3,818. Against a 1,200 sq ft house the same arithmetic gives $5,091; against a 2,000 sq ft house it gives $3,054. Those are illustrations of how the scaling rule moves the number, not city-published charges: the city has not published the ADU calculation method, and the actual charge depends on the specific house.

### The building permit: priced from a valuation the city does not publish for ADUs

The September 2024 schedule prices building permits from total valuation in tiers — the $100,001 to $500,000 band starts at $1,468.49 and adds $14.28 per additional $1,000 of valuation — with city plan check at 75% of the permit fee and a 4% technology surcharge on top. That is a complete pricing machine with one missing input: the valuation the city assigns to an 800 sq ft ADU. Other cities in this series publish a per-square-foot valuation for exactly this purpose; Culver City's schedule does not, and we will not substitute construction cost for valuation. The permit line is therefore the largest known unknown in this post, and the reason the total is labeled a floor.

### Two planning charges that look applicable but sit outside the floor

The FY2025-26 adopted comprehensive fee schedule, Planning Division, lists plan check for building permits in a Minor tier that names accessory dwelling units alongside single-family additions: $1,239.24. Separately, the ADU ordinance requires an ADU applicant to submit an address assignment request and fee before a building permit issues, and the same schedule prices address assignment at $580.45. Together those are $1,820 before the schedule's 5.44% planning technology surcharge (about $1,919 with it). They are confirmed schedule figures that appear to apply to this project — yet they do not appear in our fee database for Culver City, which carries only two verified items for the city. We itemize them here and flag the decision for review: if both charges apply, the floor rises to about $311,774 before the surcharge, or about $311,873 with it.

### The park fee: required, but not priced for ADUs

The same ordinance sentence that imposes the mobility fee also imposes in-lieu parkland dedication fees on ADUs of 750 square feet or more. That makes the park fee a required line for our 800 sq ft example — and an unpriceable one. The city publishes annual park dedication reports, and its October 2025 parks facilities study proposes a new fee framework, but the study itself describes in-lieu amounts as calculated case by case, and we found no adopted ADU park figure. Older park fee tables from the 1990s still circulate in council archives; they are not current charges and we do not use them. Until the city quotes a park figure for an ADU, this line stays at "verify with city" — the single largest gap in the floor.

### Sewer and water: state law narrows what can be charged

We found no Culver City ADU sewer or water connection figure. State law helps in one direction: an ADU is generally not treated as a new residential use for connection-fee purposes unless it was built together with a new single-family dwelling, and a separate connection cannot be forced on the standard state-exempt unit. That does not make the utility cost zero — trenching, meters, and capacity work inside the project still cost money, and they sit inside the construction phases — but no separate city connection charge can simply be assumed. Verify with the city and the serving utilities for a detached unit on your lot.

### The state-law box: the 750 sq ft line

State ADU law draws one line that every city must honor, and Culver City's ordinance repeats it. In paraphrase: no impact fee may be imposed on an ADU under 750 square feet, and any impact fee on an ADU of 750 square feet or more must be scaled to the primary dwelling's size. The provision was long cited as Government Code section 65852.2, subdivision (f), paragraph (3)(A); after the 2025 recodification it now appears as section 66311.5, and the school fee is a separate statutory charge outside that impact-fee definition. Culver City's code tracks the state rule closely, adding only the local detail that park fees join mobility fees on the larger side of the line. For the full mechanics of the threshold, see [the 750 sq ft cliff explainer](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Pre-reviewed plans: nine sets, still not free of fees

Culver City's Pre-Reviewed ADU program, known as PRADU, is one of the fuller offerings in this series. The February 2024 city handbook describes nine plan sets: three layouts — studio, one-bedroom, two-bedroom — each drawn in three styles. The plans exist to spare homeowners the cost of custom design for new construction, and they can be downloaded from the city's ADU pages. How city plan libraries compare across California is covered in [the free-plans guide](/posts/free-adu-plans-california/). What they do not do is waive anything else: the handbook is explicit that plan check and development impact fees still apply, and the applicant must still produce a site plan for the specific property and submit full-size plan sets for review. Our $14,000 design line stays in the soft-cost phase because even a prototype plan leaves site work, engineering adaptation, and review in the homeowner's hands; a reader who uses a PRADU set should expect design costs below the baseline, not at zero.

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. Whether the two planning charges belong in the total.** The $1,239 plan-check tier and the $580 address assignment are confirmed schedule figures that read as applicable to ADUs, but they are not in the fee database and the floor excludes them. Confirming them adds about $1,820, or about $1,919 with the planning technology surcharge. This is the smallest of the three movers and the easiest to resolve with one question to the planning division.

**2. The park fee and the mobility scaling.** The park charge is required but unpriced, and the mobility charge, while published at $3,818, is subject to a scaling rule tied to the main house. Together these two lines could move the total by thousands in either direction: a small house raises the mobility scaling, a large house lowers it, and the park fee adds an unknown amount on top. No responsible total can be stated until the city prices both for a specific lot.

**3. The school rate and the permit valuation.** If Culver City Unified has adopted the 2026 Level I cap of $5.38, the school line rises $168. In the other direction, the city's FY2025-26 comprehensive schedule lists $4.20, which would lower the school line by $776 — but that row's commercial figure exceeds the state cap, so it cannot simply replace $5.17 without confirmation. Separately, the building permit and its 75% plan check remain entirely outside the floor, pending the city's valuation for an 800 sq ft ADU. Between them, these two items are the difference between a floor of $309,954 and a real permit-stage bill — which is why the floor label matters more than the floor number.

## A note on SB 1117

From 1 January 2027, SB 1117 changes the impact-fee measurement for a homeowner's first two ADUs: fees apply only to the square footage above 750, not to the whole unit. For this 800 sq ft example, the chargeable slice would be 50 square feet. That would shrink both the mobility fee and any park fee that follows the same measurement — but by how much depends on the city's implementation, which has not been published. We therefore state no 2027 figure. Homeowners deciding between permitting in late 2026 and waiting for 2027 should weigh the fee saving against months of carrying costs and construction-price movement, not against a number we cannot support.
