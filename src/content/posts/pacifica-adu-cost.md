> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Pacifica costs at least **$302,960, or about $379 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and the schedule behind that number is unusually candid about where its own fees stop. Anyone searching for Pacifica ADU fees will find a FY 2026-27 citywide schedule that states its ADU exemptions in the fee lines themselves: no vehicle fee under 750 square feet, no pavement-cut fee under 750 square feet, and a simultaneous-build exemption for larger ADUs. What they will not find is a citywide total, because three of Pacifica's biggest fee lines depend on where the parcel sits or how the unit is classified. This post counts the one fee that is citywide, names the three traps, and refuses to average them into a number no parcel actually pays.

Pacifica is the batch's lesson in geography as a fee. A reader north of Paloma Avenue, south of Sharp Park, with a two-bedroom unit faces a materially different fee stack from a reader elsewhere in the same city — and a post that published one blended Pacifica total would mislead both of them. The floor here is therefore deliberately narrow, and the conditional lines beside it are the useful part.

## The full line-item breakdown

Six phases, the same six used on every build in this series. Our method is described in full on the [methodology](/methodology/) page; in short, phases 2 through 6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline because Pacifica does not publish phase-level construction costs, while phase 1 is built from Pacifica documents wherever possible.

| Phase | Cost | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft costs (design, permits, fees) | $14,960 | 5% |
| Site work + foundation | $38,000 | 13% |
| Framing + structure | $48,000 | 16% |
| Building envelope | $42,000 | 14% |
| Systems + interior | **$147,000** | 48% |
| Landscaping & hardscape | $13,000 | 4% |
| **Total** | **$302,960** | **$379 / sq ft** |

The soft-costs phase is $14,000 of design from the series baseline plus the $960 construction vehicle impact fee. Everything else that usually lives in that phase — the building permit, plan check, school fee, park fees, and wastewater charge — is excluded, not estimated. A floor that quietly absorbed guesses would stop being a floor.

One scope note, because readers often ask for the companion figure: this post does not publish a verified total for a new 2,000 sq ft single-family home in Pacifica. The fee schedule prices permits by valuation and impact fees by unit or bedroom count but never prices construction, so no honest total can be derived from it. The 2,000 sq ft figure appears here only as the denominator used elsewhere in this series — an 800 sq ft ADU is 40% of a 2,000 sq ft primary home, which is the proportion the state's impact-fee rule uses once the 750 sq ft cliff is crossed.

Two details in that table deserve emphasis. First, the counted $960 is a per-square-foot fee, not a per-dwelling fee, which is why it is counted in full rather than prorated: the schedule prices it on the ADU's own area, and the batch's precedent for per-square-foot impact fees is to count them at the ADU's area. The proration question that matters for Pacifica is reserved for the per-unit fees — the mitigation fee and the Highway 1 fee — where a proportional treatment could cut the figure substantially. Second, the park-fee columns are a genuine fork, not a rounding difference: for two bedrooms, new construction versus addition differs by more than $11,000 on the facilities fee alone, so classifying the ADU wrong would move the budget more than every counted fee combined. That fork is left open on purpose.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. Pacifica's own schedule implements the first half explicitly for the vehicle and pavement-cut fees, and our 800 sq ft example therefore pays the vehicle fee in full on its own area. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) changes the treatment for lots with two or fewer ADUs: only the area in excess of 750 square feet is charged, so an 800 sq ft unit would be charged on 50 sq ft — about $60 on the vehicle fee. That future rule is not in this post's 2026 total. Connection and capacity charges are a separate category from impact fees, which is why wastewater sits in its own row. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The parcel's zone, twice over.** North of Paloma Avenue adds the $2,450 mitigation fee at the full second-unit rate, subject to the proration question; the Highway 1 area adds either $3,311 or $1,142 at the full second-unit rate, subject to the same question. A parcel can be in both zones' logic at once, so the conditional stack for an affected parcel could exceed $5,000 before any permit or school fee is counted. Pin the parcel's zones before budgeting anything beyond the floor.

**2. The park classification and the wastewater fixture count.** The park fork — new construction versus addition — and the wastewater fixture count are both parcel- and design-specific, and both are large: the park fork alone spans more than $11,000 on one fee. Neither can be resolved from the schedule text; both need the city's answer for the actual unit, and the fixture count needs the actual plans.

**3. The permit bill and the school fee.** The building permit follows ICC valuation adjusted by the regional index, but no ADU valuation could be computed from the retrieved schedule text, and no district school rate was confirmed. [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) covers why a statewide cap cannot stand in for a district rate. When the city and district answer, both lines join the floor by addition.

## Pacifica vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pacifica (verified floor) | **$302,960** | $960 vehicle fee only; zone-dependent and classification-dependent fees excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Pacifica sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: Pacifica's figure is missing its permit bill, its school fee, and every zone-dependent fee, while the LA County figures include theirs. Add only the zone-conditional mitigation fee and Pacifica reaches $305,410 — still below the range, because the permit and school lines are still missing. The honest reading is not that Pacifica is cheap; it is that Pacifica's citywide fee is small and its parcel-specific fees are where the money is.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $302,960 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, pin the parcel's zones and the unit's park classification and fixture count, and carry separate, explicit allowances for the mitigation fee, the Highway 1 fee, the park fees, wastewater, the permit and plan check, and the school fee. When the city and district answer, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision. That is the point of keeping guesses out of the total in the first place.
