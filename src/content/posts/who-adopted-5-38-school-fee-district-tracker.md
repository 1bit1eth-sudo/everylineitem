On January 28, 2026, the State Allocation Board (SAB) raised the maximum Level 1 school facility fee to **$5.38 per square foot** for residential construction (up 4.06% from $5.17) and **$0.87** for commercial/industrial construction (up from $0.84). That number is a cap, not a charge. No district's rate changes until its own governing board adopts the increase — and the increase generally takes effect 60 days after that board action. So the question for any ADU owner is not "what did the state set?" but "what has *my* district adopted, and when did it take effect?"

This is the first monthly edition of that tracker. Every row below was checked against a district document or district page on November 3, 2026. Districts not listed here are **not yet verified in this tracker** — that is a statement about our verification, not about the district's action. If your district is missing, verify with the district before you rely on any number.

## Two things this edition corrects

**The calendar dates were not the adoption dates.** A planning calendar circulating earlier this year listed LAUSD as "08-11," San Diego Unified as "05-10," and Arcadia as "04-27." Those are effective dates (or, for San Diego, the last day of the old rate) — not board votes. LAUSD's board acted June 12, 2026; San Diego Unified's board acted March 10, 2026. If you are reconstructing which rate applied to a permit pulled in between, the board date and the effective date both matter, and they are two months apart.

**The LAUSD/LADBS flag did not reproduce as a number conflict.** An earlier note flagged a possible mismatch between the Los Angeles Unified district page and the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety (LADBS) notice. In this check, no conflicting rate was found on either side: the district's own page and its June 2026 payment information both show $5.38, and LADBS pages confirm the 500 sq ft rule without posting a different rate. What *is* worth knowing is the collection split: district staff collect impact fees for projects inside district boundaries but outside the City of Los Angeles, while LADBS collects them for projects inside the city under contract with the district. If your parcel is inside city limits, the fee is still the district's rate — it just passes through a different office. Confirm which office collects for your parcel; do not assume a second rate exists.

## What this means for an ADU

> **ADU box:** Under Gov. Code §65852.2(f)(3)(A) and §66311.5, as amended by SB 543 (Chapter 520, Statutes of 2025, effective January 1, 2026), a school district may not levy a school developer fee on an ADU of less than 500 square feet of interior livable space, and the same 500 sq ft floor applies to a junior ADU. At 500 sq ft and above, the district may charge its adopted rate on the total resulting increase in assessable space — not just the portion above 500. If your parcel sits in more than one district's territory, which district's rate applies depends on the parcel's district.

Three consequences follow:

1. **The 500 sq ft line is the school-fee cliff, not 750.** A 600 sq ft ADU pays school fees but no city impact fees (city impact fees are exempt at 750 sq ft or less under §66311.5(c)(1)(A)). A 499 sq ft ADU pays no school fee at all.
2. **SB 1117 does not change school fees.** From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 limits *impact fees* on the first two ADUs on a lot to the area above 750 sq ft — but school fees are levied separately under Education Code §17620, not as impact fees under §66311.5. Waiting until January does not shrink a school-fee line.
3. **The worked example, at the new cap.** An 800 sq ft ADU in Los Angeles Unified territory: 800 × $5.38 = **$4,304**. The same unit at 499 sq ft: **$0**. At the old $5.17 cap, the 800 sq ft unit was $4,136 — the SAB adjustment added $168 on this size. These are arithmetic on the adopted rate, not a quote: your district's adopted rate, and whether your parcel is in a fee-sharing pair, decide the real number.

**Fee-sharing is why "$5.38" is not what every district collects.** Non-unified districts split the cap with their partner district under a sharing agreement. Mountain View Los Altos High School District, for example, collects 33.33% of the cap — $1.79 per sq ft — while its feeder districts collect the rest. Huntington Beach City collects 61%. A parcel's district determines the share, so two neighboring parcels can owe different school fees on identical units. If your parcel's district is not in the table above, it is not yet verified in this tracker — verify with the district.
