Watsonville's impact fees are $0 three times over, and each zero has its own reason. The Public Facilities Impact Fee is **$0** because it applies only to new detached structures or additions over 1,000 sq ft — this 800 sq ft ADU is under the line. The Affordable Housing In-Lieu Fee is **$0** because the Development Fee Summary lists an accessory dwelling unit at $0.00, against $21,885 for a single-family detached home. And the Public Art Program Allocation Fee is **$0** because the 0.75%-of-valuation allocation applies only to projects with 2,000 sq ft or more of new floor area and $500,000 or more in valuation. None of those is a blanket exemption; each is a threshold this ADU does not cross. Our six-phase total lands at **$308,000, or $385 per square foot** — but it is a floor, not a finished figure. The school district fee is not yet verified, so it is not in the total at all.

The permit side is computed, not flat. The FY2026-27 fee schedule prices a new ADU building permit at **$3,655** — $2,455 base plus $4.00 per square foot over 500 sq ft — and plan check at 67% of the building payment, **$2,448.85**. What is not in the total is the school fee and the water/sewer side — no verified rows exist for either, so they are excluded rather than guessed at. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Watsonville does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## Three zeros, three thresholds

The $0 impact figure is not one decision but three, and each one is worth naming because each could flip on a different ADU. The Public Facilities fee is $0 by size: $0.40 per square foot applies only above 1,000 sq ft, so an 800 sq ft ADU is under the line — but a 1,200 sq ft ADU would pay it. The Affordable Housing In-Lieu fee is $0 by category: the Development Fee Summary prices an accessory dwelling unit at $0.00 outright, against $21,885 for a single-family detached home. And the Public Art allocation is $0 by two thresholds at once: 0.75% of valuation applies only at 2,000 sq ft or more of new floor area and $500,000 or more in valuation, and this ADU is under both.

That is why this post never writes "$0 impact fees" as a blanket. Each zero carries its threshold, and the thresholds are the information a homeowner actually needs — they say exactly where the $0 stops.

## The ADU law box

Three state rules frame every number above. Under Government Code §65852.2(f)(3)(A), a local agency may not impose impact fees on an ADU under 750 square feet; at 750 or above, fees are charged proportionately to the primary dwelling's size. Under §66311.5 (SB 1117, effective January 1, 2027), per-square-foot impact fees on two or fewer ADUs are charged only on the area above 750 square feet — 50 square feet on this 800 sq ft benchmark — and connection and capacity charges are excluded from that rule. School fees are a separate statute: under SB 543, ADUs under 500 square feet pay $0 in school fees, and at 800 square feet the full district rate applies. SB 1117 does not reduce school fees, and it does not change any line counted in this post: the three verified impact lines are already $0 for this ADU, and the school fee is untouched by SB 1117.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Watsonville's three impact fees are already $0 for this 800 sq ft ADU, each by its own threshold, so SB 1117 changes nothing we can compute here — there is nothing to reduce. See the full comparison at [Watsonville: 2026 vs 2027, line by line](/sb-1117/watsonville/). School fees, permit fees and water/sewer charges are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and do not change.
