San Diego has a 750 square foot cliff. Oakland deleted the cliff entirely. Long Beach found a third way: it kept the cliff but cut it in half.

The city's development impact fee schedule contains something we had not seen in any other jurisdiction in this series — **a separate column of ADU-specific rates**. Not an exemption. Not the standard single-family bill. A middle schedule, priced per dwelling unit, at roughly 40 to 49% of what a new single-family home pays. Fire facilities: $241.74 instead of $496. Parks and recreation: $1,781.39 instead of $4,613.04. Police facilities: $342.86 instead of $703.

So this post does two things. First, the standard six-phase breakdown for an 800 sq ft detached ADU in Long Beach — **$313,000, or $391 per square foot.** Then the part nobody publishes: an autopsy of the soft-cost line, fee by fee, from the city's own schedules — including the one fee line the schedule leaves genuinely ambiguous.

## The fee autopsy: $25,000, line by line

**Building permit: ≈ $1,924 (worked from the FY26 schedule).** Long Beach publishes its construction valuation table ([IB-077](https://www.longbeach.gov/globalassets/lbcd/media-library/documents/building--safety/information-bulletins/ib-077)): R-3 Type VB residential construction is valued at **$175.92 per sq ft**, so an 800 sq ft ADU carries a **$140,736** valuation. The [FY26 fee schedule](https://www.longbeach.gov/globalassets/finance/media-library/documents/city-budget-and-finances/budget/budget-documents/fy-26-adopted-budget/43-fy-26-adopted-fee-adjustments) then prices the building permit at $1,080 for the first $100,000 plus $4.98 per additional $1,000 — $1,080 + 40.736 × $4.98 = **$1,282.87**. The combination R-3 permit (all trades) runs at 150% of the building permit fee: **$1,924.30**. This is the most transparent permit line in the series so far — a published valuation, a published rate table, and arithmetic anyone can reproduce.

**Plan check: ≈ $962 + $310.** Standard plan check is 75% of the building permit fee per the FY26 schedule: $1,282.87 × 75% = **$962.15**. Planning plan check is billed separately — an actual 2025 ADU permit packet shows **$310.00**. Add permit filing ($90) and the standard surcharges (General Plan update, technology, records management, SMIP, green building — about $78 on the sample permit), and the counter total before impact fees is roughly **$3,360**.

**Development impact fees: $2,366 + the blurry line.** The city's [development impact fee schedule](https://clbepiuat.longbeach.gov/globalassets/lbcd/media-library/documents/fee-schedules/development-impact-fees) (effective 10/01/18, still the published schedule) sets ADU-specific per-unit rates: Fire **$241.74**, Parks & Recreation **$1,781.39**, Police **$342.86** — totaling **$2,365.99**. For scale, the standard single-family rates are $496 / $4,613.04 / $703: the ADU pays roughly half. A 2025 ADU permit packet's impact-fee notice confirms the same rates and cites the underlying ordinances (LBMC 18.17, 18.18, 18.22, 18.23).

**School fees: $4,304 — the series high.** Long Beach Unified School District charges **$5.38 per square foot** on ADUs over 500 square feet ([LBUSD developer fees](https://www.lbschools.net/departments/business-and-finance/facilities-development-planning/developer-fees), effective 6/15/2025): 800 × $5.38 = **$4,304**. The city's own [IB-044](https://www.longbeach.gov/globalassets/lbcd/media-library/documents/building--safety/information-bulletins/ib-044-development-impact-fees-guide) confirms the fee is due to the school district before building permit issuance. For context: Oakland charges $5.17 ($4,136), San Francisco $3.79 ($3,032). Long Beach's classrooms are the most expensive in the series.

**Water and sewer: ≈ $209 sewer, $0 water (existing meter).** The sewer capacity charge follows a published formula — equivalent fixture units × the Board of Water Commissioners rate ($109.16/EFU in 2022, adjusted annually each October) — but no ADU-specific schedule exists, and we could not reverse-engineer the **$208.98** charged on an actual 2025 ADU permit from public documents. Budget roughly $209 and confirm with LBUD Engineering. Water is cleaner: the [LBUD Rules and Regulations](https://www.lbutilities.org/files/sharedassets/utilities/v/4/about-us/documents/rules-and-regs-2024.pdf) assess a water capacity fee only for new service installations or upsized meters (3/4" meter: $2,578). Share the existing meter and the water line is **$0** — the city's ADU FAQ tells owners to decide the metering arrangement before design.

**Design and engineering: $14,000 (carried, with relief available).** The series-standard design line, carried from the baseline. But Long Beach is the rare city where the relief is real and operating: the [Pre-Approved ADU Program (PAADU)](https://www.longbeach.gov/lbcd/building/lbpaadu/) lets registered designers submit plans for advance city approval, and permit applications using PAADU plans may be approved over-the-counter with **same-day permit issuance** ([IB-068](https://www.longbeach.gov/globalassets/lbcd/media-library/documents/building--safety/information-bulletins/ib-068)). Plan purchase prices vary by designer and are not published, so we keep the full $14,000 in the model — but a buyer who finds a fitting PAADU plan should expect this line to shrink.

## The 750 square foot cliff, half height

State law ([Gov. Code § 66311.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=66311.5)) bars impact fees on ADUs of 750 sq ft or less. At 800 sq ft, Long Beach's ADU rates apply — but the schedule leaves one line genuinely ambiguous:

| Fee at 800 sq ft | Long Beach | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fire + Parks + Police (ADU rates) | **$2,365.99** | Published ADU column |
| Transportation improvement fee | **$663.75 – $1,125.00** | Tier unclear — see below |
| School district developer fees | **$4,304** | LBUSD, unaffected by the 750 rule |
| Water / sewer capacity charges | ≈ $209 sewer; $0 water | Per the formulas above |

The transportation fee schedule lists "Accessory, up to 220 sq ft" ($236.25), "Secondary, up to 640 sq ft" ($663.75), and full residential ($1,125.00) — but **no ADU-specific row**, unlike the other three fees. An 800 sq ft ADU exceeds 640, so a literal reading points to $1,125; but the 640 sq ft cap is a relic of the pre-2020 secondary-unit era, and the city's actual administrative practice for larger ADUs is not in any public document we could find. We carry the honest range rather than picking a tier. Note also: starting 2027-01-01, [SB 1117](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB1117) changes the statewide rule so impact fees apply only to square footage *above* 750 — which would shrink even this half-height cliff further.

One more geographic wrinkle: ADUs over 750 sq ft inside the Southeast Area Specific Plan area owe an additional **$0.25 per gross square foot** ($200 on 800 sq ft) under the SEASP resource impact fee. Most lots are outside it; check the map before you budget.

## What moves the number most in Long Beach

**1. The transportation tier.** The $461 spread between $663.75 and $1,125 is the single largest uncertainty in the soft-cost total — and it is a phone call to Development Services, not a modeling choice. Get the answer in writing before you finalize the size.

**2. Whether you use a PAADU plan.** Same-day over-the-counter approval is real, published, and operating. The design-line savings are unquantified (plan prices are private), but the schedule savings — weeks of plan check compressed to a day — carry their own financing value on a project with a construction loan.

**3. The school fee.** At $4,304 it is the largest single fee line in Long Beach's soft costs and the highest in the series. Unlike impact fees, it does not care about the 750 sq ft threshold the same way — LBUSD's trigger is 500 sq ft. There is no design-around; budget it.

**4. Meter strategy.** $0 water on the existing meter versus $2,578+ for a new 3/4" service is a binary decision the city's FAQ flags explicitly. Decide before design, not during.

**5. SEASP location.** The extra $0.25/sq ft only bites inside the Southeast Area Specific Plan. A $200 line you can check on a map.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does an ADU cost in Long Beach in 2026?**

About $313,000 for an 800 sq ft detached, single-story, new-construction unit with mid-grade finishes in Long Beach — roughly $391 per square foot. That is our modeled median: the soft-cost phase is rebuilt from the city's official fee schedules (a published valuation table, ADU-specific impact-fee rates, and a $5.38 per sq ft school fee), while construction phases are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline because Long Beach does not publish phase-level construction costs.

**Does Long Beach charge impact fees on ADUs?**

Yes, but at ADU-specific reduced rates — the "third way" between San Diego's cliff and Oakland's exemption. Fire $241.74, Parks & Recreation $1,781.39, Police $342.86 per ADU: roughly 40 to 49% of standard single-family rates. The transportation fee tier for an 800 sq ft ADU is ambiguous in the published schedule, carried here as $663.75–$1,125.

**How much are ADU permit fees in Long Beach?**

Worked from the published FY26 schedule: the $140,736 valuation (800 sq ft × $175.92) prices the building permit at about $1,283, the combination R-3 permit at $1,924, standard plan check at $962, plus $310 planning plan check and roughly $168 in filing and surcharges — about $3,360 at the counter before impact and school fees.

**Do I pay school district fees on an ADU in Long Beach?**

Yes — $4,304, the highest in this series. LBUSD charges $5.38 per square foot on ADUs over 500 square feet. Oakland charges $5.17 and San Francisco $3.79 by comparison.

**Does Long Beach have pre-approved ADU plans?**

Yes, and the program genuinely operates. PAADU plans from registered designers can be approved over-the-counter with same-day permit issuance — unlike San Francisco, whose published library holds zero approved plans.
