## Three fees homeowners most often get wrong

**1. Using the plan-era combined school fee.** The Consolidated Plan's $4.15 per sq ft combined figure ($1.74 high-school + $2.41 elementary) looks like a ready-made answer, and it is wrong in a specific way: the high-school portion has since moved to $1.66, so the combined figure no longer describes either district. The plan is a planning document, not a rate schedule, and its fee table carries no separate adoption date. The only current school-fee fact is the $1.66 high-school rate; the elementary rate must come from the elementary district, and until it does, there is no combined total to budget.

**2. Repeating $12.46.** A $12.46 figure for El Centro has circulated with only a bond preliminary official statement behind it — a financing document's table attributing the figure to the district, not a district resolution or rate schedule. No official district document containing $12.46 was found, and the figure is not used anywhere in this post. If a contractor, lender, or article quotes $12.46 for El Centro school fees, ask for the resolution. Until one appears, the verified high-school portion is $1.66 and the elementary portion is unknown.

**3. Treating the downtown waiver as an ADU waiver.** The Downtown Fee Waiver Program is real, current, and generous — planning, permit, encroachment, grading, and impact fees waived for qualifying projects on three downtown streets through April 2028. It is also not an ADU program: its eligible uses are commercial (studios, bars, offices, gyms, restaurants, retail), and it expressly excludes water/sewer capacity fees, state fees, and other-agency fees. An ADU outside the downtown corridor gets nothing from it, and an ADU inside it still needs a ruling that it qualifies. The separate deferral ordinance is useful — impact fees can wait until final inspection or one year from approval — but deferral is timing, not savings.

## The impact table that is not an ADU price

El Centro's published impact table is unusually legible: $3,932 per single-family unit, broken into five named components, plus a bedroom-based streets fee. It is tempting to treat $3,932 as the ADU impact fee, or to prorate it by an assumed ratio. Both moves fail the same test: the plan labels these figures per housing unit for new homes, and no El Centro document states an ADU proration method. An 800 sq ft ADU is over the state's 750 sq ft exemption line, so it is not exempt — but whether it pays half the table, a third, or some other share depends on the primary dwelling and a city method that is not published. The table is included above as context, not as a charge.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 1, 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. El Centro's unpublished ADU proration cannot survive that math unchanged — an 800 sq ft ADU would owe fees on 50 sq ft of prorated impact, not 800, shrinking the cliff penalty to a sliver of its current size. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition: the $1.66 high-school rate and the missing elementary rate are untouched by it. The January 2027 quarterly review will check for an elementary district rate, a city permit schedule, and a city ADU proration method.
