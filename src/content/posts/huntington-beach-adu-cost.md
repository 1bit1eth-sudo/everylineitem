An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Huntington Beach costs at least **$307,431, or about $384 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and anyone searching for Huntington Beach ADU fees will notice immediately that this city does something most cities in this series do not: it prices its impact fees for ADUs as their own lines, per unit, instead of forcing readers to prorate a single-family stack. The four-line ADU stack is $3,721, the sewer capacity block is $1,710, and everything else a budget normally needs — the permit, the plan check, the planning review, the school fee — is still blank. This post counts what the FY 2026/27 schedule actually prices for an ADU, and leaves the rest blank on purpose.

## The parkland line is the impact fee

Most ADU impact stacks in this series are a negotiation between a single-family rate and a proration fraction. Huntington Beach skips that negotiation. Its FY 2026/27 schedule, effective July 1, 2026, lists four lines explicitly for an accessory dwelling per unit, and the arithmetic is short:

| ADU impact line (FY 2026/27) | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Law enforcement facilities, DI-36 | $189 |
| Fire suppression facilities, DI-37 | $89 |
| Public library facilities, DI-38 | $219 |
| Parkland / open space facilities, DI-39 | $3,224 |
| **ADU impact stack, counted** | **$3,721** |

Parkland is $3,224 of $3,721 — about 87%. Compare that with the detached-dwelling parkland line in the same schedule, $17,051: the ADU line is roughly a fifth of the single-family figure, which is the schedule's own answer to the proportionality question, stated as a price rather than as a formula. A reader who assumes an ADU pays the single-family impact stack, or who prorates that stack by floor area, will overstate this line by thousands. The schedule already did the ADU math; this post just adds it up.

One scope note, because readers often ask for the companion figure: this post does not publish a verified total for a new single-family home in Huntington Beach. The schedule prices ADU lines per unit and detached-dwelling lines per unit, but never prices construction, so no honest home total can be derived from it. The $17,051 detached-dwelling parkland line appears here only to show the scale of the ADU discount the schedule itself grants.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. Huntington Beach's schedule already prices its ADU lines per unit, so this post counts the $3,721 stack in full for an 800 sq ft ADU rather than prorating a single-family rate. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) changes the treatment for lots with two or fewer ADUs: only the area in excess of 750 square feet is charged, so an 800 sq ft unit would be charged on 50 sq ft — about $233 on this stack. That future rule is not in this post's 2026 total, and it does not change the sewer capacity charge, which is a connection charge, not an impact fee. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The permit bill, whole.** The building permit and plan check are not small omissions here; in comparable cities they run into the thousands, and Huntington Beach's own valuation-based building table was not converted into an ADU total in this round. When the city prices the actual unit, that figure joins the floor by addition. Until then, any Huntington Beach budget that already includes a permit number should be asked which schedule edition it cites.

**2. The planning question.** The prior-year $2,684 planning item is the most likely next line to join the total, and the most likely to be misquoted: a reader who carries the FY 2025-26 figure into a 2026 budget without checking whether FY 2026/27 kept, raised, or dropped it is budgeting from the wrong edition. Confirm the current planning line before adding anything for it.

**3. The school fee and the sewer block boundary.** The school fee needs the parcel's district rate, confirmed from district documents; [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) shows why the statewide ceiling cannot stand in for that rate. Separately, remember the sewer block boundary: an ADU that grows past 1,000 sq ft pays a second $1,710 block, so a design change of a few dozen square feet across that line costs more than the same change anywhere else on this page.

## Huntington Beach vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Huntington Beach (verified floor) | **$307,431** | $3,721 ADU impact stack + $1,710 sewer block; permit, planning, and school fee excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Huntington Beach sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: its figure is missing its permit bill, its planning review, and its school fee, while the LA County figures include theirs. Once those blanks fill in, expect the gap to narrow. What Huntington Beach genuinely leads on is legibility: an ADU impact stack priced as its own lines needs no proration argument at all.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $307,431 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, carry separate, explicit allowances for the permit and plan check, the planning review, and the school fee, and watch the 1,000 sq ft sewer block boundary if the design grows. When the city and district answer, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision.
