A correction first, because it matters more than the numbers: our initial research matrix listed Palo Alto's impact fee at $76,385 and its permit rate at 5.42%. Neither figure survived contact with the city's official documents. Our first correction put the impact fee at ~$8,000, tracking a prior-year sheet. The current-year sheet — the FY27 sheet, as of August 17, 2026 — yields **~$18,090** on an 800 sq ft ADU after the proportional reduction, and this post now uses that figure throughout. The actual permit schedule (FY24) charges **1.76%** of valuation, not 5.42%. We got it wrong twice, we checked, and this post carries the corrected numbers. That is the policy.

With the correction in place, Palo Alto becomes the most interesting city in the series — not the most expensive, but the one with the **steepest per-square-foot cliff** and the **most dramatic SB 1117 effect**. See the full comparison at [Palo Alto: 2026 vs 2027, line by line](/sb-1117/palo-alto/). The total lands at **about $313,000, or roughly $391 per square foot** — level with Long Beach at the low end of the published ranking, built from the most lopsided fee structure we've published.

## The $18,090 question mark

Palo Alto's per-square-foot impact rates are the highest unit rates in the series — Parks alone is $18.75/sq ft, plus Community Center ($1.44), Libraries ($0.86), and two per-unit fees. The sheet says the fee for an ADU of 750 sq ft or larger is **proportional to the size of the primary unit**. Applied to the series assumption (800 sq ft ADU, 2,000 sq ft house), the arithmetic is:

| FY27 component (single-family rate) | Full primary-dwelling amount |
| --- | --- |
| Parks $18.75/sq ft × 2,000 sq ft | $37,500 |
| Community Center $1.44/sq ft × 2,000 sq ft | $2,880 |
| Libraries $0.86/sq ft × 2,000 sq ft | $1,720 |
| Public Safety Facilities $1,383 per unit | $1,383 |
| General Gov. Facilities $1,743 per unit | $1,743 |
| **Primary-dwelling total** | **$45,226** |

Proportional ADU share: $45,226 × 800/2,000 = **$18,090** (rounded from $18,090.40).

Two caveats, stated plainly. First, the sheet prints the principle — "Fee is proportional to the size of the primary unit" — but **not the formula**: the 800/2,000 multiplication is our application of that principle to the series assumption, not a number printed on the sheet. A different primary-dwelling size gives a different bill. Second, **transport fees are separate and not in the $18,090**: the same sheet lists a Charleston/Arastradero fee of $1,743 per residential unit and a citywide transportation impact fee of $10,099 per net new PM peak-hour trip. Neither is included above; confirm with the city whether either applies to your project. The prior ~$8,000 figure tracked a prior-year sheet and is superseded — it appears in this post only as that correction history.

The cliff is what makes it remarkable. At 749 sq ft: **$0** — full exemption, stated plainly on the city's fee sheet ("ADUs under 750 sq. ft. are exempt from Development Impact Fees"). At 750: the proportional fee switches on. One square foot, about $18,090. No other city in the series concentrates this much consequence into a single square foot.

## SB 1117 changes everything here

SB 1117 (signed 2026, effective January 1, 2027) reworks the cliff: impact fees apply only to square footage **above** 750, for the first two ADUs. On an 800 sq ft Palo Alto ADU, that's 50 sq ft of fee-able area instead of 800.

The arithmetic is dramatic precisely because Palo Alto's rates are high: ~$18,090 today becomes roughly **$1,130** in 2027 (50 feeable sq ft × the same proportional rate: $18,090 × 50/800). No other published city sees a bigger absolute drop. If you're planning a Palo Alto ADU and can wait for 2027 permits, the fee sheet rewrites itself in your favor.

## What we got wrong, and what we kept

The Batch 3 matrix entry for Palo Alto carried two unverified figures presented as confirmed. They weren't. The $76,385 appears to have been a confusion with the residential in-lieu fee ($99.57/sq ft — which does not apply to ADUs); the 5.42% permit rate has no source we can find. The city's actual documents say 1.76% and proportional DIF.

We corrected the matrix, flagged the source file, and wrote this post from the verified documents. The corrected total — ~$313,000 — puts Palo Alto level with Long Beach at the low end of the published ranking rather than the series-high we initially suggested. The story got better when the numbers got honest.

## The bottom line

Palo Alto is the series' cliff-maximalist: the steepest single-foot consequence, the most dramatic 2027 reprieve, and — after correction — level with the cheapest total we've published. Build at 749 sq ft and the fee sheet is nearly empty. Build at 800 and you're paying for the privilege. Come January, the question mark shrinks to about $1,130.
