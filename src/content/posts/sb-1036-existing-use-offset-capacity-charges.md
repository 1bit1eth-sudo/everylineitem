On June 30, 2026 — three months before the September housing-bill pile — Governor Newsom signed SB 1036. It is not an ADU bill. It amends the Mitigation Fee Act, the statute that governs nearly every development impact fee a city or special district can charge. And from **January 1, 2027**, it changes two calculations that show up on ADU projects more often than the bill's title suggests.

Here is what it does, in the order it hits your budget.

## 1. The existing-use offset — Gov. Code §66001(h)

**What:** When a development project **demolishes or changes an existing use**, the impact fee "shall be offset to account for the demolition or change so that the amount of the fee is attributable only to the development project's incremental impact on public facilities or services." (Chapter 54, Statutes of 2026; adds Gov. Code §66001(h).)

**The definition that matters:** "Changes an existing use" means **no demolition is proposed, but the use within an existing structure changes to a new use.** (§66001(h)(2).) A garage that becomes a dwelling is the textbook case. So is the example the bill's sponsor, SPUR, gives: an old store becomes housing, and the project receives credit for the store's existing impact, paying fees only for the added impact from the housing.

**The one-way valve:** If the offset is bigger than the fee, the excess is **not refundable** and cannot be used to offset any other fee. (§66001(h)(1).) The offset can zero a fee. It can never pay you back, and it can never shelter a different fee on the same project.

**Why this existed at all:** Before SB 1036, whether a redevelopment project got credit for the prior use depended on the jurisdiction. Some cities credited it; some charged the full "new development" fee as though the site were vacant dirt. The bill makes the credit uniform and mandatory statewide.

## 2. The capacity-charge rule — Gov. Code §66013(j)

**What:** "When a change in the capacity of an existing water or sewer connection is proposed, the local agency, in calculating the estimated reasonable cost of a capacity charge, shall only calculate an amount attributable to the change in the capacity." (§66013(j).)

**In plain English:** If your ADU needs the water meter upsized — say from 5/8-inch to 1-inch — the district may charge you for the **added capacity between the two sizes**, not re-charge you for the capacity you already have and already paid for. The same applies to a sewer connection whose capacity changes.

**The same one-way valve:** "If the existing capacity exceeds the proposed capacity, no amount shall be refunded, credited, transferred, assigned, or otherwise applied to offset any other charge or fee." Downsize a connection and you get no money back. The rule only ever reduces what you owe; it never creates a credit.

**The background rule it sits on:** Capacity charges already could not exceed the estimated reasonable cost of providing the service (§66013(a)). SB 1036 defines what "reasonable cost" means when an existing connection changes: the change, and only the change.

## What it means for an ADU, specifically

SB 1036 is a general development-fee law, not an ADU law — so its ADU effect is narrower than the headlines suggest, and worth stating precisely.

- **Garage conversions are the clearest ADU case.** A garage-to-ADU conversion changes the use within an existing structure without demolition. That is exactly what §66001(h)(2) defines. From 2027, the impact fee on that conversion must be offset for the garage's existing use. In practice the bite is limited: ADUs under 750 sq ft already pay $0 in impact fees, and larger ADUs already pay proportionally under Gov. Code §65852.2 — so the offset matters most for conversions of 750 sq ft or more, and for the non-ADU fees on the same project.
- **New detached ADUs mostly do not trigger the offset.** A backyard cottage does not demolish anything and does not change an existing use — the house stays a house. The offset rule has nothing to offset. The ADU-specific rules already govern: no impact fee under 750 sq ft, proportional fees above it, and no treatment as a new residential use for connection fees or capacity charges unless the ADU is built with a new single-family dwelling (§65852.2(f)(2)–(3)).
- **Meter and sewer upsizes are where §66013(j) earns its keep.** Many ADUs are served through the existing house connection, and some districts require a larger meter or a larger sewer lateral. From 2027, that upsizing charge must be priced on the increment alone. If your district's bill itemizes a full new-connection capacity charge for an upsized meter, that line is worth questioning after January 1.
- **Redevelopment ADUs on commercial sites benefit most.** Converting a storefront, office, or warehouse into housing with an ADU component is precisely the fact pattern the bill was written for — and the one where the old "charged as though vacant" practice cost the most.

## What SB 1036 does not do

- **It does not cap any fee.** It changes how the fee is calculated, not how high the underlying rate can be. A district with a high per-unit capacity rate still has a high rate; it just applies it to the increment.
- **It does not touch school fees.** School developer fees are levied by school districts under Education Code §17620, a separate regime. The $5.38/sq ft Level 1 cap and the 500 sq ft deeming rule are untouched.
- **It does not change building permit fees.** Plan check, inspection, and permit fees are not Mitigation Fee Act fees.
- **It creates no refunds.** Both new subdivisions say so explicitly. If you already paid a full new-connection capacity charge in 2026, SB 1036 does not get it back.
- **It does not apply to fees imposed before January 1, 2027.** The bill has no urgency clause. Fees imposed on or after the effective date follow the new rules; earlier assessments follow the old ones.

## Should you wait for January?

| Your situation | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Garage conversion ADU, 750 sq ft or more | **Ask about the offset** — from Jan 1, the fee must reflect only the incremental impact |
| New detached ADU, under 750 sq ft | **No reason to wait** — impact fees are already $0, and the offset does not apply |
| New detached ADU, 750 sq ft or more | **SB 1117 is the bigger lever** — fees only on square footage above 750 from Jan 1, 2027 |
| ADU needs a meter or sewer upsizing | **Worth timing** — from Jan 1, the capacity charge covers only the added capacity |
| Fee already assessed in 2026 | **No benefit from waiting** — SB 1036 creates no refunds for fees already imposed |

## The bottom line

Two rules, one principle: **you pay for what you add, not for what was already there.** The existing-use offset stops cities from charging redevelopment as though the site were vacant, and the capacity rule stops districts from charging a meter upsizing as though it were a brand-new connection. Neither rule cuts a rate or writes a refund — but for a garage conversion or a forced upsizing, the difference between "the whole thing" and "the increment" is the whole ballgame. January 1 is 55 days away.
