The single most common misconception in ADU planning: **"I'd have to live there."**

For a standard ADU — the 800 sq ft detached unit this series prices — **you don't.** State law prohibits local agencies from imposing owner-occupancy requirements on standard ADUs. Build it, rent it, rent the main house too, and live in another state if you want. The ADU doesn't care where you sleep.

JADUs are the exception. And a handful of local programs add their own strings. Here's the full picture.

## Standard ADUs: no owner-occupancy requirement

Under California's ADU statutes, a city or county **may not require the owner to live on the property** as a condition of permitting a standard ADU — detached, attached, or garage conversion. This is one of the pillars of the state framework, and it applies statewide regardless of what a local ordinance would prefer.

What that means in practice:

- You can live in the main house and rent the ADU.
- You can live in the ADU and rent the main house.
- **You can rent both and live elsewhere entirely** — another city, another state. This is fully permitted for standard ADUs.

The state also protects your rental options at the other end: ADUs must be rented for terms of 30 days or longer (no short-term rental conversion), but beyond that floor, the rental market is yours.

## JADUs: the exception — and AB 1154 narrowed it

Junior ADUs (JADUs) — the smaller, conversion-based units (max 500 sq ft, carved from existing space) — are a different regime. State law **does** require owner-occupancy for JADUs: the property owner must live in either the main dwelling or the JADU itself.

**AB 1154 (2026) narrowed this.** The owner-occupancy requirement now applies to JADUs that **share a bathroom with the main home** — the classic "JADU carved out of the house with a shared bath" configuration. JADUs with independent bathroom facilities fall outside the narrowed rule.

If you're deciding between a JADU and a standard ADU, this is a real variable: the JADU saves construction cost but attaches the occupancy string to the property. See our [JADU vs ADU comparison](/posts/jadu-vs-adu-vs-garage-conversion/) for the cost side.

## Local strings: check the specific ordinance

State law sets the floor, not the ceiling, on some details. Cities can add local conditions around the edges:

- **Incentive programs** with recorded deed restrictions (fee waivers, grants) often carry their own occupancy or affordability covenants. The state ADU statutes don't override a restriction you voluntarily signed onto in exchange for money.
- **JADU ordinances** vary city to city in how they implement the state occupancy requirement — some record a deed restriction, some require an annual affidavit.
- **SB 9 lot splits** are a different regime entirely (see below), and its rules get confused with ADU rules constantly.

The rule of thumb: **the specific city ordinance controls.** State law tells you what the city *can't* do to standard ADUs; the ordinance tells you what strings attach to *your* project.

## When you sell: the rule follows the property

Occupancy requirements attach to the **property**, not the person. When you sell:

- A standard ADU stays a standard ADU — no owner-occupancy requirement materializes for the buyer.
- A JADU's owner-occupancy requirement passes to the new owner — they must occupy one of the two units, same as you did.
- Voluntarily-recorded deed restrictions (incentive programs) run with the land and bind the buyer.

Buyers doing due diligence should ask for the ADU's permit file and any recorded restrictions. Sellers should disclose them.

## Don't conflate this with SB 9

SB 9 (lot splits) **does** carry owner-occupancy strings — the applicant must sign an affidavit of intent to occupy one of the resulting parcels as their principal residence for three years. That rule belongs to SB 9's lot-split regime, not to ADU law, and it gets incorrectly applied to ADU projects constantly. If someone tells you "California requires you to live there for your ADU," they are almost certainly thinking of SB 9.

## The bottom line

| Unit type | Owner-occupancy required? |
| --- | --- |
| Standard detached ADU | **No** — state law bars the requirement |
| Standard attached ADU / garage conversion | **No** — same protection |
| JADU sharing a bathroom with the main home | **Yes** — owner must occupy one unit (AB 1154, 2026) |
| SB 9 lot split | **Yes** — 3-year applicant affidavit (different regime) |

For the 800 sq ft detached ADUs this series prices: no, you don't have to live there. Check your city's ordinance for incentive-program strings, keep JADUs in their own lane, and don't let SB 9 confusion talk you out of a project state law specifically protects.
