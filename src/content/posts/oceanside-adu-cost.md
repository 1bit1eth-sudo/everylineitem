Oceanside's zoning ordinance contains the clearest ADU impact-fee sentence in this series: "The City shall not impose any impact fees upon the development of an ADU or JADU." No qualifier, no square-foot threshold, no sunset clause. Read alone, that sentence makes the 750 sq ft cliff irrelevant in Oceanside — an 800 sq ft ADU pays exactly what a 749 sq ft one does: **$0**. But the sentence is the ordinance's text as previously published: as of 6 November 2026, the city's ADU page no longer states the waiver, and the city says information relying on the ADU ordinance is no longer applicable while it certifies the ordinance with HCD. Treat the $0 below as unconfirmed, not settled.

Then the city's own consultants wrote a second document. An August 2025 park DIF nexus study proposed rebuilding the parks fee on a per-square-foot basis at a justified maximum of **$6.03/sq ft**, carving out only ADUs under 750 sq ft from the new charges — and that schedule has since been adopted (single-family $6.03/sq ft, effective January 20, 2026), though whether it applies to ADUs is not confirmed. On 800 sq ft, that is up to **$4,824** the ordinance says does not exist. Two official documents, two answers, one 800 sq ft ADU. Which one the counter charges when you pull the permit is the open question this post is built around — and we are not pretending we know the answer.

Our six-phase total lands at **$311,000, or $389 per square foot** — just under the LA County baseline, priced off the ordinance's $0 DIF, the confirmed $3,832 school fee, and the FY 2026-27 permit schedule ($5,378 before surcharges). If the park schedule applies to ADUs, the real bill could sit up to $4,824 higher. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Oceanside does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## The ordinance's $0 vs. the adopted schedule

This is the first city in the series where the central question is not what a fee costs, but whether it exists. Oceanside's zoning ordinance is unambiguous: "The City shall not impose any impact fees upon the development of an ADU or JADU." Oakland got to $0 by exemption; Salinas by DIF waiver; Oceanside by flat declaration in the ordinance itself. On the ordinance's face, the 750 sq ft cliff does not apply here — there is no cliff when the fee is zero for every size.

The park fee schedule complicates that. It exists because AB 602 forces cities to rebuild flat per-unit impact fees on a per-square-foot basis, and its math only exempts ADUs under 750 sq ft per state law (Gov. Code §65852.2). The schedule is now adopted — single-family $6.03/sq ft, effective January 20, 2026 — but whether it applies to ADUs is not confirmed. $6.03/sq ft is a justified *maximum* from the 2025 nexus draft, and nothing on the record shows the city has applied it to an ADU. But a city that adopts a $6.03/sq ft residential park fee is a city keeping its options open — and the ordinance section carrying the $0 promise carries no amendment date, so we cannot even tell you which document is newer.

We priced this post on the ordinance as previously published: **$0 DIF**. As of 6 November 2026 that is unconfirmed — the city's ADU page no longer states the waiver and the ordinance is under HCD certification. The $4,824 lives in this post as a labeled hypothetical, not a line item. If you are budgeting an 800 sq ft Oceanside ADU, do not budget the $0 until the city confirms it in writing with Development Services — because the one number you cannot afford to discover at the counter is the one the adopted schedule predicts.

## $3,832: confirmed, and below the ceiling

Oceanside Unified's $4.79/sq ft developer fee is one of the lower confirmed school fees in this series — a full $0.59 under the $5.38 state maximum that Glendale, Long Beach, Burbank, and Torrance all charge. On 800 sq ft, the gap between Oceanside and the top tier is **$472**.

Two caveats. First, the district's official page publishes the rate without a resolution number or effective date, so we could not tie it to a board action — the rate is confirmed, its paperwork trail is not. Second, Oceanside is a five-district city: OUSD covers most of it, but parcels can fall in Vista USD, Bonsall UESD, Fallbrook UHSD, or Carlsbad USD, and the school fee follows the parcel, not the city. The $3,832 assumes OUSD.

## The $5,378 permit bill (FY 2026-27 schedule)

Oceanside does not price building permits on valuation. It prices them on square footage, with a plan-check row and an inspection row for new single-family homes, plus two surcharges (General Plan Update 10%, Technology Update 2%). The city's FY 2026-27 Building Division Fee Schedule (Resolution 18-R0079-1) publishes the current rates: plan check $1,862.70 + inspection $3,515.40 = **$5,378.10** for a new single-family dwelling — about five times Riverside's $1,006. The schedule's smallest single-family row is 1,000 sq ft, so an 800 sq ft ADU pays the row total, and the city's ADU page settles the classification question: detached ADUs are assessed as a new single-family dwelling, while attached or conversion ADUs are assessed as an addition/remodel. That is $2,163 less than the ~$7,541 this post previously carried from the 2018/2021 tables.

Two notes, stated plainly. The surcharges add about $645 (10% + 2% of $5,378.10) at the counter — they are charged, but the schedule states them as percentages, so they sit outside the $5,378 line and outside the $311,000 total, the same way the fee page counts them. And the fire review (20%) and water review (15%) lines from the old tables do not appear in the FY 2026-27 schedule at all — whether they still apply is unconfirmed, not $0. The five open questions are now three: the park schedule's ADU application, the water/sewer proportional charges, and the school fee's resolution paperwork.

Water and sewer are the emptiest line in the post. The ordinance's principle is solid — an ADU is not "new residential use," and charges on a new detached unit must be proportional to its burden on the system. The proportional dollar amount, however, is nowhere in the public record. The only published numbers are the full single-family connection buy-ins ($24,640 for a 5/8" meter), which are a ceiling, not a quote. This line carries the honest label: **not found — verify with city**.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. See the full comparison at [Oceanside: 2026 vs 2027, line by line](/sb-1117/oceanside/). In Oceanside, SB 1117 collides with both documents at once. If the ordinance's $0 DIF survives, the new law changes nothing — $0 times anything is $0. The park schedule has now been adopted at $6.03/sq ft, so if it applies to ADUs, SB 1117 rewrites the math: the full-area $6.03/sq ft on 800 sq ft becomes $6.03 on the 50 sq ft above 750 — about **$302** instead of $4,824. Either way, the January 2027 quarterly review will check how the adopted schedule applies to ADUs and how the city reconciles it with the ordinance. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and stay at $3,832.
