Cost per square foot is the most quoted ADU number — and the most misleading one. A contractor who says "$400 a foot" sounds cheaper than one who says "$538 a foot." In this series' own models, the $400 figure belongs to the **more expensive** build.

The problem is not that per-foot math is wrong. It is that it hides which costs scale with area and which do not. Once you see that split, the number becomes useful again — as a secondary check, never as the headline.

## The paradox in this series' own numbers

All figures below are this site's published Los Angeles County models, not new estimates. The per-foot figures are simple division on those published totals.

| Model | Total | Size | Per sq ft |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 800 sq ft detached | $320,000 | 800 | **$400** |
| 400 sq ft detached | $215,000 | 400 | **$538** |
| Garage conversion (typical 2-car, ~450 sq ft living) | $190,000 | ~450 | **~$422** |

The 400 sq ft unit costs $105,000 less in absolute dollars and $137 more per foot — 34% higher. The garage conversion sits between them on both measures, because it reuses a shell but keeps a full kitchen and bath.

Regional project data across Southern California bends the same way: smaller studios often land near $500–$600 per square foot, while 800–1,000 sq ft units commonly land closer to $375–$450 when site conditions are comparable. A 500 sq ft one-bedroom around $300,000 (~$600 per foot) and a 1,000 sq ft unit near $425,000 (~$425 per foot) are the same curve, not different markets.

## Why the curve bends: fixed costs don't shrink

The full phase comparison explains it. Hold the 400 and 800 models side by side:

| Phase | 400 sq ft | 800 sq ft | What happens when you halve the size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Soft costs (design, permits, plan check) | $28,000 | $32,000 | Falls only $4,000 |
| Site work + foundation | $26,000 | $38,000 | Falls $12,000 |
| Framing + structure | $26,000 | $48,000 | Falls $22,000 |
| Building envelope | $25,000 | $42,000 | Falls $17,000 |
| Systems + interior | $102,000 | $147,000 | Falls $45,000 — still six figures |
| Landscaping & hardscape | $8,000 | $13,000 | Falls $5,000 |

The phases that shrink are the cheaper ones. The phases that barely move are the expensive ones. A kitchen for 400 sq ft is not half the kitchen for 800. A bathroom is a bathroom. The smallest heat pumps on the market are already oversized for many studio ADUs. Permits, drawings, structural calcs, Title 24 documents, a sewer lateral, and an electrical service run do not come in half sizes.

That is why **half the size is not half the price** — and why the per-foot number rises as the unit shrinks. See the full breakdowns in the [800 sq ft post](/posts/800-sq-ft-adu-cost-california/) and the [400 sq ft post](/posts/400-sq-ft-adu-cost-california/), and the [soft costs deep dive](/posts/adu-soft-costs-permits-california/) for the permit and design lines alone.

## Where per-foot pricing is real: the fee side

Some government lines genuinely are priced per square foot — and they behave differently from construction per-foot math:

- **School district fees** are levied per square foot of ADU, up to the 2026 Level 1 state maximum of $5.38 per sq ft. An 800 sq ft ADU in a district charging the maximum owes $4,304. School fees use their own 500 sq ft threshold, not the 750 line — see [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/).
- **Impact fees** are sometimes per-square-foot rates (Palo Alto's parks fee runs $18.65 per sq ft before proportionality), sometimes flat schedules, and sometimes dwelling-base fees prorated by the ADU-to-house ratio. The same ADU can owe $0 in one city and five figures in another at the identical size — see [the 750 sq ft cliff, explained](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).
- **Permit and plan-check fees** are usually valuation-based or flat ADU schedules, not per-foot rates — which is why soft costs fall only $4,000 between the 400 and 800 models.

So a quote that blends construction per-foot with fee per-foot into one rate is mixing two different rulers. Keep them apart: construction per foot for the build, fee schedules for the jurisdiction. Run your address through the [fee calculator](/compare/) for the fee side.

## How to use per-square-foot without fooling yourself

1. **Fix size first, then compare per foot.** Per-foot comparisons are only valid between units of similar size, scope, and finish level. Never compare a 400 studio to an 800 two-bedroom on per-foot alone.
2. **Compare totals, then phases.** Two bids at the same per-foot rate can hide different inclusions. Put every bid into the same six-phase ledger and find which line doesn't match.
3. **Separate the fee side.** Ask every bidder which permit, school, and impact fees are included — and check them against your city's verified schedule before you sign.
4. **Watch the cliff, not just the curve.** At 750 sq ft, city impact fees switch on or off entirely. A 749 sq ft plan and an 800 sq ft plan differ by 51 feet and, in some cities, by $10,000 or more in fees. Per-foot math smooths over that cliff; the fee schedule does not.
5. **Remember what per foot leaves out.** Contingency, loan interest, first-year tax on the ADU only, and vacancy are real year-one costs that no per-foot construction rate includes. See the [all-in cost post](/posts/adu-all-in-cost-california/) for that stack.

## The bottom line

Per-square-foot cost is a useful secondary metric and a terrible headline. It rewards bigger units even when the bigger unit costs more cash, and it hides the fixed-cost lines that dominate small builds. Use it to sanity-check bids of the same size. Use totals — construction phases plus your city's verified fees — to decide what to build.

### Check this number against your own address

Most California jurisdictions publish permit records online, including the **declared construction valuation** for each project. That declared valuation is the closest thing to a real local baseline you can get without paying anyone.

1. Open [EPIC-LA](https://epicla.lacounty.gov/) for unincorporated LA County, or the [LADBS Permit & Inspection Report](https://www.ladbsservices2.lacity.org/onlineservices/?service=plr) for addresses inside the City of Los Angeles.
2. Search recent ADU permits within about a mile of your address.
3. Compare the declared valuation to the phase totals on this page.

Declared valuations run below contract price — treat them as a floor, not a total. Full detail on [Methodology](/methodology/).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a good per-square-foot price for an ADU in California?**

There is no single good number, because per-square-foot cost falls as the unit grows. In this series' Los Angeles County models, an 800 sq ft detached ADU runs $320,000 ($400 per foot) and a 400 sq ft unit runs $215,000 ($538 per foot). Compare totals for the same size and scope, not per-foot figures across different sizes.

**Why is a smaller ADU more expensive per square foot?**

Because permits, utility connections, the kitchen, the bathroom, and much of the mechanical package are close to fixed. Halving the floor area mainly cuts lumber, drywall, flooring, and roofing — the cheaper phases. Absolute cost falls; cost per foot rises.

**Does cost per square foot include permits and fees?**

It depends on who is quoting. This series' per-foot figures divide the full six-phase total — including soft costs — by floor area. But some fee lines are priced per foot themselves, like school district fees up to the $5.38 per square foot state maximum, while others are flat or prorated, so a single per-foot rate never captures the fee side.

**Should I compare contractor bids per square foot?**

Only after you fix size, scope, and inclusions. A bid that excludes permits, utility work, or contingency will look cheaper per foot and cost more in total. Put every bid into the same six-phase ledger before comparing any per-foot number.

**What is the cheapest per-square-foot ADU size?**

In these models, bigger is cheaper per foot up to the point where lot, setbacks, or local rules stop you — but the total keeps rising. The 800 sq ft model costs $105,000 more than the 400 in absolute dollars. Per-foot efficiency is not the same as affordability.
