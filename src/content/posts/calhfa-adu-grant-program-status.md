The most important thing to know about California's $40,000 ADU grant is that **you cannot get it right now** — and anyone telling you otherwise is, in the state agency's own words, running a scam.

The California Housing Finance Agency (CalHFA) ran the ADU Grant Program to reimburse homeowners for the bills that pile up before construction starts. Its program page now carries exactly one update, dated **December 28, 2023**: "The latest round of ADU funding has been fully allocated." As of **November 4, 2026**, CalHFA has posted no new round, no reopening date, and no new funding announcement. The program is closed until CalHFA says otherwise.

## What the grant was

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum grant | Up to $40,000 per ADU |
| What it covered | Pre-development and non-recurring closing costs: site prep, architectural designs, permits, soil tests, impact fees, property surveys, energy reports |
| What it did not cover | Construction itself — labor and building materials |
| Status as of 4 Nov 2026 | Closed; latest round fully allocated 28 Dec 2023 |
| How to watch for a return | CalHFA program page + eNews signup |

The $40,000 matters because of where it lands in a build. On our [800 sq ft benchmark](/posts/800-sq-ft-adu-cost-california/), soft costs run into five figures before a single wall goes up — permits, school fees, impact fees, design. The grant was aimed squarely at that stack: it reimbursed the pre-development costs, not the build. It did not make an ADU cheap; it made starting one less punishing.

## Who qualified — under the last round

The last round's terms, for background only, were aimed at owner-occupants: homeowners who owned and lived in the property as their primary residence, with income at or below 80% of their county's Area Median Income, applying through a CalHFA-approved lender or partner rather than directly to the state. Grants went into construction escrow, not to the homeowner as cash.

None of that is current eligibility. With no round open, there is no income limit to check and no application to file. If funding returns, CalHFA will publish new terms — and they may differ. Do not let a contractor, lender, or consultant pre-qualify you for a program that is not accepting anyone.

## The scam warning

CalHFA's update is blunt: "If anyone approaches you saying they can help you get an ADU Grant, it is a financial scam." The agency asks homeowners to stop contact and forward any names or phone numbers to marketing@calhfa.ca.gov. That warning has sat unchanged on the program page since December 2023, which is itself the point: nearly three years later, the closed program is still being used as bait.

## What to do instead

- **Do not put the $40,000 in your budget.** Price the ADU on the financing you actually have. Our [financing comparison](/posts/adu-financing-compared-heloc-construction-loan/) walks through HELOCs, construction loans, and cash-out refinancing — the routes that are actually available.
- **Watch the source, not the rumor.** CalHFA's program page and its eNews list are where a new round would appear first. Secondary pages still describing the grant in the present tense are out of date.
- **Mind the fee cliffs anyway.** The costs the grant used to reimburse — permits, impact fees, school fees — are still the ones that swing a budget. An ADU under 750 sq ft avoids impact fees under Gov. Code §66311.5; [the 750 sq ft cliff, explained](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/) shows how that line works, and from January 1, 2027, SB 1117 limits impact fees for a homeowner's first two ADUs to the area above 750 sq ft.
