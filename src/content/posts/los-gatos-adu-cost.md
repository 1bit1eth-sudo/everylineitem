> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Los Gatos costs at least **$303,208, or about $379 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and this post is the one in the batch where the floor is the story. Anyone searching for Los Gatos ADU fees will find a school-fee page that answers half the question with admirable clarity and then, by its own terms, sends the reader to a different district for the other half. They will also find a town that has moved its permitting online and a transportation impact policy that was proposed, not yet priced, for ADUs over 750 square feet. This post counts one verified town fee, shows the one verified school half without pretending it is a whole, and explains why a $303,208 floor is more honest here than a $306,000 estimate would be.

Los Gatos is the purest case of the split-district rule in this batch. The rule is simple: confirm each district's rate from that district's own documents before writing a school fee, and if the boundary splits, do not pick a representative combination. Los Gatos does not merely split by parcel — it splits by collecting entity, with four different elementary feeder districts collecting on behalf of the same high-school district. There is no Los Gatos school fee. There are four different combined totals, depending on which feeder district the parcel sits in, and only one component of those totals is verified.

Six phases, the same six used on every build in this series. Our method is described in full on the [methodology](/methodology/) page; in short, phases 2 through 6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline because Los Gatos does not publish phase-level construction costs, while phase 1 is built from Los Gatos documents wherever possible — and here, phase 1 contains only the design baseline.

The soft-costs phase is $14,000 of design from the series baseline and $1,208 of construction impact fee. Every other fee that usually lives in that phase — permit, plan check, school fees, transportation impact, utility charges — is excluded, not estimated. A floor that quietly absorbed guesses would stop being a floor, and in Los Gatos the guesses would have to be large to fill a phase that is otherwise empty.

One scope note, because readers often ask for the companion figure: this post does not publish a verified total for a new 2,000 sq ft single-family home in Los Gatos. The town's fee documents located during this session do not price construction, so no honest total can be derived from them. The 2,000 sq ft figure appears here only as the denominator that makes the example legible — an 800 sq ft ADU is 40% of a 2,000 sq ft primary home, which is the proportion the state's impact-fee rule uses once the 750 sq ft cliff is crossed.

Two details in that table deserve emphasis. First, the high-school district's page is unusually candid about its own incompleteness: it names the four feeder districts, names the collecting entity for each area, and states that square footage is determined by the municipal planning department, not by the districts. That candor is why this post can describe the split precisely and still not sum it — the page gives the reader the map but only one of the two prices. Second, the $1,328 is not a rounding of a larger figure. It is $1.66 per square foot, a rate the district restated as of June 15, 2026, with a February 2026 justification study and an April 2026 authorizing resolution behind it. A verified half is worth reporting exactly because it bounds the question: whatever the elementary half turns out to be, the combined school fee for a parcel in this high-school district is at least $1,328, and the floor in this post would rise to $303,328 if only that half were counted — which it is not, because a half is not a school fee.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. Our 800 sq ft example therefore crosses the cliff in principle, and the town's 2025 proposal would apply its transportation charge on that same proportional basis. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) changes the treatment for lots with two or fewer ADUs: only the area in excess of 750 square feet is charged, so an 800 sq ft unit would be charged on 50 sq ft. That future rule is not in this post's 2026 total. Connection and capacity charges are a separate category from impact fees, which is why sewer and water lines stay in their own row. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The elementary half of the school fee.** This is the first thing to resolve, because it is the only fee in this post with a verified sibling. The parcel's feeder district determines both the rate and the collecting entity, and the district's own fee page is the place to confirm it. Until then, do not add $1,328 to the floor and call the result a school-fee total, and do not borrow another feeder district's rate as a stand-in. The school-fee explainer that covers how split districts work across the state is [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/).

**2. The town's current permit figure.** The FY 2022-2023 schedule is not a substitute for a current figure, and third-party permit ranges for Los Gatos (which run to five figures) are not verification. The town's online permitting system resolves fees at application acceptance, so the practical move is to open a pre-application and obtain the fee quote for the specific plans. That quote, once obtained, converts this floor into a budget by addition.

**3. The transportation impact charge.** If the September 2025 policy modification was adopted, an 800 sq ft ADU becomes chargeable on a proportional basis, and the proposal's own fiscal note suggests the amount is nominal rather than large. But the published bases conflict — $1,104 per new average daily trip in the AB 1600 reports (with no ADU trip figure) versus $6.10 per square foot in a November 2023 nexus study (listed as proposed) — and adoption of the October 2025 revision was not confirmed. Confirm adoption, the rate, and the proration denominator with the town. A nominal charge would move this floor slightly; an unconfirmed one must not move it at all.

## Los Gatos vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Los Gatos (verified floor) | **$303,208** | Construction baseline + $1,208 construction impact; permit, school, transportation, and utility fees all excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Los Gatos sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is at its most uneven here: Los Gatos's figure contains only one town fee, while the LA County figures include theirs. Do not read the gap as a saving. Read it as the size of the unanswered questions. Once the permit, the elementary school half, and the transportation charge are priced, expect Los Gatos to move toward — and possibly past — the LA County range, given the town's valuation levels.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $303,208 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, and carry a separate, explicit allowance for the four unpriced items — the permit, the combined school fee, the transportation charge, and any utility charge. When the town and the districts answer those questions, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision. That is the point of keeping guesses out of the total in the first place.
