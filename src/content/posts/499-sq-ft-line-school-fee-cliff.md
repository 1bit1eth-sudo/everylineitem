Most ADU fee cliffs are slopes in disguise. The school-fee line is not. It is a wall, one square foot wide, and at the 2026 state cap it costs $2,690.

An accessory dwelling unit with 499 square feet of interior livable space pays $0 in school developer fees. The same unit drawn at 500 square feet pays $2,690 at the state's maximum Level 1 rate of $5.38 per square foot. Nothing else changed. One foot of floor plan, one line on the ledger, $2,690.

This post is about that line: the sentence that creates it, the one-word ambiguity at its exact edge, and what it costs in the districts we have verified.

## The sentence that creates the cliff

Senate Bill 543 (McNerney), Chapter 520, Statutes of 2025, approved October 10, 2025 and effective January 1, 2026, added a deeming provision to Government Code §66311.5(c)(3). An ADU or junior ADU "that contains less than 500 square feet of interior livable space" is treated, for Education Code §17620(a)(1)(C) purposes, as "other residential construction that does not increase assessable space by 500 square feet."

That last phrase is doing all the work. Under Education Code §17620, "other residential construction" is charged a school developer fee only if the resulting increase in assessable space exceeds 500 square feet. An ADU under 500 square feet therefore does not trigger the fee at all. And when the fee does trigger, it applies to the full area — not just the square footage above 500. That is what makes it a cliff rather than a slope: the 500th foot does not cost $5.38. It costs the whole $2,690.

The $2,690 figure follows the statute's own words. It is 500 square feet times the State Allocation Board's 2026 maximum Level 1 residential rate of $5.38 per square foot, approved January 28, 2026. At the same rate, an 800 square foot ADU pays $4,304 in school fees — the figure our fee calculator shows for every district at the cap.

Sources: the "less than 500" exemption is in [Gov. Code §66311.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=66311.5), as amended by SB 543; the 2026 Level 1 rate and the SB 543 changes are summarized in HCD's [ADU Handbook (March 2026)](https://www.hcd.ca.gov/sites/default/files/docs/policy-and-research/adu-handbook-update.pdf).

## The table at the cap rate

| ADU size (interior livable) | School fee at $5.38/sq ft |
| --- | --- |
| 499 sq ft | $0 — exempt |
| 500 sq ft | $2,690 |
| 800 sq ft | $4,304 |

The jump from 499 to 500 is the whole story. The jump from 500 to 800 is just arithmetic.

## The one foot you must confirm: 500 exactly

Here is the honest complication. The statute says "less than 500." Some secondary summaries of SB 543 write "500 square feet or less." Those two readings put the cliff in different places:

- **Literal reading ("less than 500"):** 499 is exempt, 500 costs $2,690 at the cap.
- **"500 or less" reading:** 500 stays exempt, and the cliff moves one foot right — 501 costs $2,695.38 at the cap.

We do not get to pick which reading your district uses. Districts levy school fees under their own adopted schedules, and the schedule — not a blog post, including this one — is what your permit counter applies. If your plans land on exactly 500 square feet, get the district's answer in writing before you finalize the drawings. If you have any freedom in the floor plan, 499 is the safe side of the line under either reading, and 501 is the expensive side under both.

This is also why our own calculator draws the line where it does: under 500 square feet of interior livable space, the school line is $0; at 500 and above, the district's rate applies. That matches the statute's words. It does not settle what your district will do at exactly 500.

## Your district's rate is the second variable

The $5.38 figure is a cap, not a universal rate. The State Allocation Board sets the maximum Level 1 rate; each district adopts its own rate locally, typically effective 60 days after its board adopts it. That is why the same 500 square foot ADU costs different amounts in different districts — and why a rate you saw last year may already be out of date.

From our verified dataset (October 2026 verifications), 34 verified school-fee lines sit at the $5.38 cap, and 16 verified lines still show the old $5.17 cap. The same 500 square foot ADU:

| District (verified) | Rate | 500 sq ft school fee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At the 2026 cap (34 verified lines) | $5.38/sq ft | $2,690 |
| Still at the old cap (16 verified lines) | $5.17/sq ft | $2,585 |
| Pasadena (FI-270, verified 19 Oct 2026) | $2.24/sq ft | $1,120 |
| Baldwin Park (FI-157, verified 13 Oct 2026) | $3.48/sq ft | $1,740 |

The gap between Pasadena and the cap is $1,570 on the same 500 square feet. That is a district boundary, not a building choice. Some districts charge more than the Level 1 cap under Level 2 findings — Folsom, for example, charges $10.26 per square foot — but Level 2 is a separate mechanism with its own justification requirements, not the cap rate, and we do not mix it into this table.

## The JADU corner

Junior ADUs live on this same line by definition: a JADU is a unit of no more than 500 square feet of interior livable space, contained within a single-family residence. SB 543's language covers JADUs alongside ADUs, so a JADU under 500 square feet is exempt the same way. But a JADU drawn at exactly 500 square feet sits on the disputed boundary — the same confirm-with-your-district question, with even less room to redraw, since the JADU cap leaves nowhere to go but down.

## Two different rulers: 750 for impact fees, 500 for school fees

Do not confuse this line with the better-known one. Government Code §66311.5(c)(1)(A) exempts ADUs of 750 square feet or less from development impact fees, and JADUs of 500 square feet or less. That is the impact-fee ruler. The school-fee ruler is §66311.5(c)(3), and it reads "less than 500." A 700 square foot ADU pays no impact fee and still pays the school fee. A 499 square foot ADU pays neither.

SB 1117, effective January 1, 2027, changes the impact-fee ruler: for up to two ADUs on a lot, impact fees apply only to the area above 750 square feet. It does not touch the school-fee ruler. School fees are levied by districts under Education Code §17620, not by cities under the impact-fee section, and neither SB 1117 nor AB 956 amends that section. Your 2027 school-fee line is your 2026 school-fee line.

## What to do with this

1. **Measure interior livable space, not the footprint.** The statute's words are "interior livable space." A unit that measures 500 square feet outside-to-outside may already be under the line inside. The district's fee schedule defines how it measures — ask, and keep the answer with your permit file.
2. **If you can choose, choose 499.** It is exempt under either reading of the boundary. If you cannot choose, price the school line at your district's current adopted rate before you sign a build contract — it is a four-figure line that quotes routinely omit.
3. **Ask your district two questions, in writing:** Is a unit of exactly 500 square feet of interior livable space exempt here? And what is your currently adopted residential rate? The second answer changes as districts adopt the $5.38 cap on their own schedules.

One reported case shows the line working as intended: a Menlo Park owner deliberately built a 499 square foot detached unit to stay under the school-fee line, as the owner described in a published account of the project. That is a design decision made around a statute, not around a guess — and it is the kind of decision this line rewards.

The 500 square foot line is the smallest fee cliff in California ADU law, and the easiest to plan around. It costs one square foot of floor plan to avoid, or $2,690 at the cap to ignore.
