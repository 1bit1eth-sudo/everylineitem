Ten cities. Same 800 sq ft detached ADU. Same mid-grade finishes. **$12,000 apart** — Long Beach at $313,000, San Francisco at $325,000.

That spread is the entire thesis of this series in one table. Construction costs barely move between these cities; the $12,000 lives in Phase 1 — the soft costs. School fees, impact fees, permits, sewer. The ADU is identical. The fee schedules are not.

## What the spread tells you

**$12,000 is small — and that's the point.** On a $320,000 build, the city-to-city spread is under 4%. Nobody should pick a city for ADU fees alone. But $12,000 is also real money, and it concentrates in lines you can actually plan around:

- **School fees** are the most predictable spread: $1,792 (Pasadena, not yet ranked) to $4,320 (Campbell, not yet ranked). Within this table: $4,104–$4,304. District boundaries, not city boundaries, set this number.
- **Impact fees** are the most volatile: $0 (Oakland, Sacramento sewer-side) to ~$11,173 (Pasadena proportional). Within this table, Anaheim's ~$10,411 DIF is the high.
- **Permit fees** are the least predictable: $1,006 (Torrance, series low) to ~$19,500 (San Francisco). Valuation-based cities punish expensive builds; flat-fee cities don't.

**The cliff matters more than the ranking.** Every city in this table except Long Beach has a meaningful 750 sq ft cliff. Shrink the ADU to 749 sq ft and the ranking reshuffles completely — several cities drop $5,000–$11,000 in impact fees alone. The cheapest ADU in California isn't in the cheapest city; it's the 749 sq ft ADU in a high-cliff city.

## How to use this table

1. **Find your city** (or nearest published neighbor) for a planning baseline.
2. **Read that city's full post** — the total is the headline; the ledger is the tool.
3. **Check the cliff** before finalizing square footage. The 750 line is worth more than any city choice.
4. **Verify the asterisked items** with the city. Our totals are honest about what's confirmed and what isn't.
