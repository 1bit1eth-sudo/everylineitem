> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Monterey costs at least **$306,304, or about $383 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and anyone searching for Monterey ADU fees will find the shortest fee stack in this batch, because it has exactly one line in it. The city charges no development impact fees. The building permit cannot be priced from the schedule. What remains is a district charge, stated plainly on the district's own page, and that charge is the whole counted fee bill. A post that padded this stack with a guessed permit or a borrowed impact fee would be longer, and wrong.

## One fee, and it is the school fee

The school fee is the post because the city has vacated the field. Monterey Peninsula Unified School District's Developer Fees page states that the district levies the current SAB Level I fees, updated September 26, 2026, at $5.38 per square foot for residential construction and for ADUs of 500 square feet or more, payable before building-permit issuance, with a City Approval Report showing the chargeable square footage and a district Certificate of Compliance. The arithmetic is one line:

| Counted fee (Monterey, 800 sq ft) | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| MPUSD school developer fee: 800 sq ft × $5.38 | **$4,304** |
| Prior rate, for comparison: 800 sq ft × $5.17 | $4,136 |
| **Increase from the rate change** | **+$168** |
| City development impact fees | $0 (none levied) |
| **Counted fees, total** | **$4,304** |

Two boundaries on that line. First, the district's page instructs applicants to confirm that their address falls within the district before paying: no evidence was found of any City of Monterey parcel falling in another district — the district lists Monterey among the communities in its attendance area, and the neighboring districts cover Pacific Grove, Carmel, and their surroundings — but the address check remains the correct procedure for a specific parcel. Second, the district's July 8, 2026 board item was a developer-fee increase, which corroborates the update, but the resolution document itself was not retrievable in this round: the rate is confirmed from the district's live page, while the resolution number and exact adoption date are flagged for confirmation rather than asserted.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. In Monterey that rule has nothing to bite on at the city level, because the city levies no impact fees in the first place — and the one counted fee, the school fee, is not a covered impact fee, so it is unchanged by SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5, effective January 1, 2027) either. The cliff still matters to a Monterey reader in one practical way: it is the reason the valuation-based permit, not an impact stack, is the number to pin down next. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The permit valuation.** This is the largest uncounted line and the only one that can be resolved with a single document: a contractor's valuation approved by the Building Official. At a $300,000 valuation the band formulas give $14,463.95 before hourly charges — but that illustration is not a quote, and a different valuation gives a different total by $38.08 per $1,000 of valuation ($20.50 permit + $14.50 plan check + 15% of the permit increment, $3.08, on the General Plan Fee). Get the valuation first; the permit follows from it mechanically.

**2. The district paperwork.** The $5.38 rate is confirmed on the district's page, but the resolution number and exact adoption date are not yet pinned down, and the district requires an address check before payment. Both are small confirmations, and both should happen before the fee is paid — not because the rate is in doubt, but because the payment procedure turns on them.

**3. The utility and plan lines.** No utility connection or capacity figure for a Monterey ADU could be verified from the schedule, and no city pre-approved plan exists to save design cost. [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) covers why a statewide cap cannot stand in for a district rate — and in Monterey's case, why the district rate is the only rate that matters. When the city answers on utilities, those lines join the floor by addition.

## Monterey vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monterey (verified floor) | **$306,304** | $4,304 school fee only; no city impact fees; permit excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Monterey sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: Monterey's figure is missing its entire permit bill, while the LA County figures include theirs. Add only the $300,000-valuation illustration and Monterey reaches $320,768 — above the range, because the illustration is not a quote and the utility lines are still missing. The honest reading is not that Monterey is cheap or expensive; it is that Monterey's countable fee is one line, and its permit is the open question.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $306,304 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, obtain a contractor valuation and run it through the schedule's band formulas for the permit, confirm the parcel's district address check with MPUSD, and carry separate, explicit allowances for the permit, the plan check, the General Plan Fee, and the utility charges. When the city and district answer, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision. That is the point of keeping guesses out of the total in the first place.
