> **DRAFT — not published.** This post is a floor estimate built only from verified fee lines. Unverified fees are excluded, not estimated. Last verified 27 Oct 2026.

Mountain View is unusually transparent about its permit range and unusually quiet about a single permit number. The city tells you a new ADU's building permit runs **$7,000–$13,000** — and that honesty is exactly why this post counts no permit line at all. A range is not a figure, and this series does not guess a point inside one. What is countable is the school fee: **$4,192**, split across two districts, and it carries the whole fee stack in this floor.

The school split works like this. Mountain View sits in two districts: Mountain View Whisman (elementary) and Mountain View–Los Altos Union High (MVLA). The combined verified rate is **$5.24 per square foot**, or $4,192 on 800 square feet. The city requires the fee on new construction over 500 square feet, with proof of payment before the permit issues. Watch the $112 gap: MVLA's July 2026 developer fee study sets its share at $1.79 and describes the combined fee as $5.38 — the current state maximum. If the elementary district adopted the 2026 maximum on the same split, the combined rate is $5.38 and this line becomes $4,304. That adoption was not confirmed in this round, so $5.24 stands and the $112 is flagged, not counted.

## The ADU law box

Three state rules frame every number above. Under Government Code §65852.2(f)(3)(A), a local agency may not impose impact fees on an ADU under 750 square feet; at 750 or above, fees are charged proportionately to the primary dwelling's size. Under §66311.5 (SB 1117, effective January 1, 2027), per-square-foot impact fees on two or fewer ADUs are charged only on the area above 750 square feet — 50 square feet on this 800 sq ft benchmark — and connection and capacity charges are excluded from that rule. School fees are a separate statute: under SB 543, ADUs under 500 square feet pay $0 in school fees, and at 800 square feet the full district rate applies. SB 1117 does not reduce school fees, so Mountain View's $4,192 does not change under SB 1117.

## Three things that can still move the number

1. **The actual permit bill.** The $7,000–$13,000 range spans $6,000. Where a specific project's valuation lands in that band decides thousands of dollars, and plan check treatment on top of it is unstated.
2. **The $112 school-fee gap.** If the elementary district adopted the 2026 maximum, the school line is $4,304, not $4,192.
3. **New-construction impact and utility treatment.** The conversion exemption is documented; the new-detached-unit treatment is not. An excavation permit alone can add up to $12,000 where street work is required.

## How Mountain View compares

Only published posts are used here. In the Bay Area, published totals are Palo Alto **~$303,000**, Fremont **$314,000**, Campbell **~$318,000**, and Oakland **$322,000**. Mountain View's $306,192 floor sits just above Palo Alto's and well below Fremont's — but it counts only the school fee, while the published posts count fuller stacks. The honest read is that Mountain View's real total, once the permit bill lands, will sit somewhere in the Fremont–Campbell band, not below Palo Alto.
