> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in San Luis Obispo costs at least **$313,360, or about $392 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and anyone searching for San Luis Obispo ADU fees will notice two things at once: the building fee is unusually transparent, and the impact-fee picture is unusually blank. The FY 2026-27 schedule prices a New ADU line by the square foot, states its surcharge, and lets the reader check the city's announced increase to the cent. The same schedule then publishes five impact-fee tables without ever saying whether a new ADU pays any of them. This post counts the transparent line, counts the school fee the district still posts, and refuses to price the blank half.

## The per-foot permit, checked against the city's own increase

The building fee is the post's anchor, because it can be verified twice: once from the schedule, once from the city's announcement. The FY 2026-27 Comprehensive Fee Schedule, effective July 1, 2026, prices Fee #6 "New ADU" (500 square feet minimum) in its per-square-foot building table:

| New ADU building line (FY 2026-27, Fee #6) | Per sq ft |
| --- | --- |
| Plan review | $4.15 |
| Inspection | $4.61 |
| IT surcharge (3.05% of $8.76) | $0.27 |
| **Total** | **$9.03** |
| **800 sq ft × $9.03, counted** | **$7,224** |
| School fee, SLCUSD (counted) | $4,136 (800 × $5.17) |
| **Counted fees, total** | **$11,360** |

The cross-check matters. The prior year's line was $6,968, or $8.71 per square foot, and the city announced a 3.68% user-fee increase from July 1, 2026. $8.71 × 1.0368 = $9.03, and $6,968 × 1.0368 = $7,224.42 — the published rounded rate gives $7,224. A fee that survives its own city's press release is a fee worth counting. One correction to the prior framing: neither year's figure is a printed lump sum. Both are per-square-foot lines, and $7,224 is the computed 800 sq ft figure, not a line the schedule prints.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. That rule is exactly why the single-family impact tables above cannot be pasted into an 800 sq ft ADU budget: even if the city confirms the tables apply, the charge would be a proportion, not the full single-family figure. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) changes the treatment for lots with two or fewer ADUs: only the area in excess of 750 square feet is charged, so an 800 sq ft unit would be charged on 50 sq ft. That future rule is not in this post's 2026 total, and it does not change the school fee, which is not a covered impact fee. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. Whether any impact table applies at all.** This is the largest open question in the post, and it is binary before it is numerical: if the city's current position is still that ADUs pay no impact fees, the counted total stands; if the tables apply proportionally, the floor rises by a proportion that still needs a primary-dwelling size. Until the city answers, any total that includes these tables is a guess with a schedule attached. The city, not the schedule, has to settle it.

**2. The small lines around the building fee.** Planning review ($242.34), the fire alteration line ($1,616 on 800 square feet), and the construction tax ($194.78) are each small beside the impact tables, but together they could move the floor by about $2,000. None of them is ADU-specific in the schedule, so none is counted — and the city should confirm whether any of them is subsumed in the $9.03 per-square-foot building line or charged on top of it.

**3. The school fee's next move.** SLCUSD is the one district in this batch that has not adopted the $5.38 cap, and the $168 gap between $4,136 and $4,304 is a board action away. [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) covers why a statewide cap cannot stand in for a district rate. Watch the district's Developer Fees page, not the SAB announcement, for the change.

## San Luis Obispo vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| San Luis Obispo (verified floor) | **$313,360** | $7,224 building fee + $4,136 school fee; impact tables excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

San Luis Obispo sits at the bottom of the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: San Luis Obispo's figure is missing every impact line, while the LA County figures include theirs. If even one impact table applied in full, San Luis Obispo would pass the top of the range — which is precisely why the tables are not counted until the city confirms they apply. The honest reading is not that San Luis Obispo is cheap; it is that its countable permit and school bill is $11,360 and its impact position is still open.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $313,360 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, ask the city in one message whether the transportation, parkland, water, wastewater, and emergency tables apply to a new 800 sq ft ADU and on what basis, confirm whether the small planning, fire, and tax lines sit inside or outside the $9.03 building rate, and carry separate, explicit allowances for any table the city confirms. When the city answers, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision. That is the point of keeping guesses out of the total in the first place.

## What this post does not claim

No transportation, parkland, water, wastewater, or emergency-services impact fee is claimed, because the schedule does not state that any of them applies to a new ADU. No planning review, fire alteration, construction tax, or inclusionary fee is claimed, because none of them is ADU-specific in the schedule. No school fee at $5.38 is claimed, because the district's current posted rate is $5.17. No pre-approved plan saving is claimed, because the county's free plans do not cover city parcels and no city program was found. No 2,000 sq ft home total is claimed. The $7,224 building fee is claimed as a computed figure from the per-square-foot New ADU line, not as a printed lump sum, and the $4,136 school fee is claimed at the district's posted rate. Readers who want the background on plan programs generally should read [California's free ADU plans](/posts/free-adu-plans-california/) and then confirm availability and price with the city before assuming any saving.
