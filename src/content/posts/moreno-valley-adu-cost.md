Moreno Valley is the first city in this series where the two most common cost drivers both point the same way. The regional transportation fee, TUMF, is **$0 for an ADU** — the adopted schedule lists TUMF as Exempt for an ADU, where a single-family home pays $10,104, and the TUMF ordinance exempts ADUs outright. The schedule's other impact-fee columns are proportional for ADUs 750 sq ft and larger, so they apply to this 800 sq ft unit; their amounts depend on the primary dwelling, so they sit outside the $310,000 total rather than at $0 (see the footnote below). And the city hands you the plans for free: seven city-owned pre-approved detached ADU designs, from a 306 sq ft studio to a 988 sq ft three-bedroom, under the "ADU for You" program launched in December 2024. The permit package is **$3,746 all-in** — plan check $1,385, inspection $2,310, filing $51 — from a dedicated ADU category in the FY 2026-27 fee schedule. Our six-phase total lands at **$310,000, or $388 per square foot**.

The rest of the ledger is just as clean. The school fee is the state maximum $5.38 per square foot from Moreno Valley Unified, $4,304 — effective June 29, 2026. Planning is $0: ADUs are processed ministerially, straight to Building. What is not in the total is the water and sewer side — no verified connection or capacity row exists for an ADU, so those charges are excluded rather than guessed at — and the proportional impact-fee lines, whose amounts depend on the primary dwelling (see below). Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Moreno Valley does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## Seven free plans, and what they do not cover

The "ADU for You" program is the most generous design offer in this series so far: seven free, city-owned, pre-approved detached ADU plans, each with multiple architectural styles, from a 306 sq ft studio to a 988 sq ft three-bedroom. The plans have already been reviewed by Building & Safety, which can speed up permitting. To qualify, the lot must be zoned residential with an existing single-family home or duplex, and it cannot sit in a geohazard, landslide, flood or Wildland-Urban Interface zone.

What the free plans do not do is erase the design line. A homeowner still needs a site plan drawn for the lot, and site-specific engineering and Title 24 work still apply. So this ledger keeps the $14,000 design and engineering baseline in, and treats the free plans as upside: use one, and the real bill comes in under the $310,000 shown here. That is the honest way to carry a free plan in a ledger — as a saving you can actually claim, not as a number we subtracted on your behalf.

## A $0 TUMF — with proportional lines attached

The $0 in this ledger is the TUMF line only, and it deserves a careful footnote — because the schedule is easy to misread. The adopted DIF schedule (effective July 1, 2023) has no separate "City DIF" column: its fee columns are TUMF, DIF[2] (the city's own impact fee, split into Subdivision and In-Fill), Public Art, Workforce Development, SKR mitigation, and MSHCP. For an ADU, the TUMF column reads Exempt — a single-family home pays $10,104 in TUMF — and the TUMF ordinance (Riverside County Ord. 824.20, §§M and T) confirms that ADUs and junior ADUs are exempt from TUMF. That is the $0 in this ledger. The city's own fee, DIF[2], is "Based on Square footage" for an ADU, not exempt; SKR mitigation is $500 per acre; MSHCP follows footnote [5] — the single-family $4,236 fee calculated proportionately to the primary dwelling for ADUs 750 sq ft and larger; and Workforce Development is $0.15 per sq ft of living space. So an 800 sq ft ADU crosses the cliff and can owe those fees — the amounts depend on the primary dwelling's size (and the lot, for SKR and MSHCP), so they are disclosed in the table above rather than totalled. The city's own payment registers confirm ADUs pay MSHCP in practice (August 2025 collections for residential single-family & ADU, $35,862.26).

The older city ADU handout says the same thing in plain words — "DIF fees will apply to ADUs that are 750 square feet or larger" — so the handout and the schedule agree on the DIF program lines (DIF[2], MSHCP). The schedule's Exempt cell is TUMF, a separate regional fee paid directly to WRCOG, which the handout does not discuss — and the TUMF ordinance exempts ADUs, so there is no conflict to resolve. The schedule governs either way. One wrinkle from the December 1 re-verification list is now closed: the Workforce Development rate for an ADU is $0.15 per sq ft, not the 2023 schedule row's $0.13. The fee is set by council resolution (Moreno Valley Municipal Code §§3.38.145 and 3.42.115), and Resolution No. 2024-16 (adopted April 2, 2024, effective June 1, 2024) moved the residential rate from $0.13 to $0.15 per sq ft — the $0.13 was the pre-update rate, shown as the "Current Fee Schedule" column in the 2024 exhibit. The 2022 Workforce nexus study's ADU rule still governs how it applies: an ADU over 750 sq ft pays the single-family fee in proportion to the primary dwelling (ADU sq ft ÷ primary sq ft), which works out to $0.15 per sq ft of ADU living space, or $120 for this 800 sq ft unit. The FY 2026-27 Schedule of City Fees (revised June 30, 2026) confirms the ADU's development impact fee is calculated per square foot under the current schedule, whose total is $13.30 per sq ft. The 2024 schedule's automatic increases were then deferred: on April 15, 2025 the council adopted Resolution No. 2025-22 (5–0), moving the automatic increases from January 1, 2026 and January 1, 2028 to January 1, 2028 and January 1, 2030 — which is why the city's current phase-in schedule runs 2024 → 2028 → 2030. If any of this changes, this post will be corrected.

Two smaller footnotes sit on the school fee. The $5.38 rate is the Moreno Valley Unified rate; slices of the city in the southwest fall in Val Verde Unified, whose rate is also $5.38 per sq ft, effective July 13, 2026 — a homeowner there should still confirm which district covers the lot. And lots in a Community Facilities District pay higher CFD rates instead of the standard schedule. Neither changes the $4,304 in this ledger, which uses the MVUSD rate for the standard case.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. TUMF is already $0 (exempt), so SB 1117 changes nothing we can compute for that line. The proportional lines are a different story: from January 2027 they shrink to the 50 sq ft above 750 — but their 2026 amounts depend on the primary dwelling, so no dollar saving can be stated. See the full comparison at [Moreno Valley: 2026 vs 2027, line by line](/sb-1117/moreno-valley/) (verified rows only). School fees, permit fees and water/sewer charges are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and do not change.
