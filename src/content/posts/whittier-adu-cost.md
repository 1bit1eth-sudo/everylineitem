> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Whittier costs at least **$302,000, or about $378 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and that sentence is honest precisely because it contains no city fee at all. Anyone searching for Whittier ADU fees will find a school-fee map, not a price list: four elementary districts, two high-school districts, a city FAQ showing one set of rates, and district resolutions showing a newer set for the best-documented pair. What they will not find is a single number they can add to a budget without first answering which districts cover their parcel. This post works that question in full, counts nothing it cannot defend, and lets the floor stand at the construction baseline until the districts answer.

Whittier is the batch's proof that a thin page is sometimes thin for a structural reason, not a research failure. The city does not publish a building permit schedule that could be located, and the school fee cannot be one number by construction. A post that invented either — a borrowed permit figure, an averaged school fee — would look more complete and be less true. The useful post is the one that maps the districts, dates the discrepancy, and shows the one pair arithmetic that a reader in that territory can check.

One scope note, because readers often ask for the companion figure: this post does not publish a verified total for a new 2,000 sq ft single-family home in Whittier. Two details in that table deserve emphasis. First, the worked pair is not a guess dressed as a calculation: the elementary share comes from the district's own adopted resolutions, and the high-school share follows from the sharing agreement those same resolutions describe. What is missing is Whittier Union's own 2026 adoption document, and that single missing document is the difference between a worked figure and a counted one. Second, the FAQ discrepancy is itself information: a city page showing $3.36 where the district resolutions show $3.59 tells a reader that at least one official page is stale, and the FAQ's own advice — verify with the district — resolves which source to trust for the pair.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. Our 800 sq ft example therefore crosses the cliff in principle for impact fees. School fees are different: they are governed by Education Code section 17620, they apply from 500 square feet, and they are not impact fees for SB 1117 purposes — so from January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) changes the impact-fee treatment for lots with two or fewer ADUs (only the area in excess of 750 square feet charged, so 50 sq ft on an 800 sq ft unit) without changing the school fee. That future rule is not in this post's 2026 total. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. Whittier Union's own 2026 adoption.** If the high-school district confirms the $1.79 share, the worked pair becomes a counted fee and the floor becomes $306,304 by addition, with no other line changing. If it adopted a different rate, the pair reprices to that rate. Either way, the elementary $2,872 already stands on district resolutions, so this question has a bounded answer — it is the closest thing in this post to a fee waiting to be counted.

**2. The building permit schedule.** The city code points to a council-adopted schedule, but no current schedule with valuation bands could be located. When the city confirms the table and valuation rate, the permit and plan check join the floor by addition. Until then, do not borrow another city's permit table: Whittier's permit bill is Whittier's, and the gap is stated rather than filled.

**3. The other district pairs and the FAQ update.** Parcels outside the Whittier City and Whittier Union territory pay different pairs entirely — East Whittier City, Los Nietos, or South Whittier with Whittier Union or Fullerton Union — and none of those current rates were confirmed in this round. [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) covers how to read a split-district pair generally, but the rates themselves must come from the districts. Separately, the city FAQ should be updated to the 2026 figures, since it currently understates the Whittier City pair by $0.23 per square foot and invites exactly the under-budgeting this series exists to prevent.

## Whittier vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |

| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Whittier sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is more uneven here than anywhere else in this batch: Whittier's figure contains no fee at all, while the LA County figures contain theirs. Add only the worked school pair and Whittier reaches $306,304 — still below the range, because the permit bill is still missing. The honest reading is not that Whittier is cheap; it is that Whittier is unpriced, and unpriced is not the same as inexpensive.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $302,000 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, identify the parcel's elementary and high-school districts first, and carry separate, explicit allowances for the school pair, the building permit and plan check, and any connection charge. When the districts and the city answer, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision. That is the point of keeping guesses out of the total in the first place.
