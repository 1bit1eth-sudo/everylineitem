> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Piedmont costs at least **$310,293, or about $388 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and anyone searching for Piedmont ADU fees will find the rarest combination in this series: a city that charges no impact fee whatsoever, paired with the largest permit bill this batch counts. The $8,293 permit total is not a flat fee the schedule states outright; it is eight lines, each derived from a valuation, a percentage, or a flat charge, that add up to a number no single schedule cell contains. This post shows every line, so the total can be checked rather than trusted.

## The permit bill, line by line

Piedmont's 2026-27 fee schedule, effective January 1, 2026, does not list an "800 sq ft ADU permit" price. What it lists is a valuation method and a set of rates, and the verified record applies them to an 800 sq ft detached unit. The valuation is the starting point: 800 square feet at $175.92 per square foot, the ICC valuation figure for R-3 Type VB construction, gives $140,736. Every percentage line below hangs off that valuation or off the permit fee itself, which is why a different valuation method would move several lines at once.

| Permit component (2026-27 schedule) | Calculation | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building permit and inspection | $2,858 + 40.736 × $10.06 | $3,267.80 |
| Plan check | $1,141 + 40.736 × $1.17 | $1,188.66 |
| General plan maintenance fee | 1.50% × $140,736 valuation | $2,111.04 |
| Records management fee | 6% × $3,267.80 permit fee | $196.07 |
| Seismic (SMIP) fee | $0.50 + ($140,736 − $3,850) × $0.0001 | $14.19 |
| SB 1473 fee | Schedule rate, about | $5.63 |
| Title 24 new-structure fee | Flat, schedule rate | $581.00 |
| ADU planning permit | Flat, schedule rate | $929.00 |
| **Permit total, counted** | **Sum of the eight lines** | **$8,293.39** |

Two lines deserve a second look. The general plan maintenance fee, at 1.50% of valuation, is the second-largest line in the whole permit bill — larger than the plan check itself — and it is the line most likely to be missed by a reader who adds only "permit plus plan check" and stops. The records fee, at 6% of the permit fee, is small in dollars but easy to miss for the same reason: it is a percentage of a derived figure, not a number printed in the schedule. Together, the surcharges (general plan, records, seismic, SB 1473, Title 24) are $2,907.93, or about 35% of the counted total. A Piedmont permit quote that omits them understates the bill by more than a third.

One scope note, because readers often ask for the companion figure: this post does not publish a verified total for a new single-family home in Piedmont. The schedule prices permits by valuation but never prices construction, so no honest home total can be derived from it. The $140,736 figure appears here only as the permit valuation for the ADU, not as a construction cost.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. In Piedmont the rule changes nothing in this post's total, because there is no impact-fee program for it to act on, and permit fees are not impact fees. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) likewise changes nothing here: with no impact fee to flatten and a permit bill that is not an impact fee, the counted total is the same before and after the new law. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The valuation basis.** Every percentage line in the permit bill hangs off the $140,736 valuation, which itself hangs off the $175.92 ICC figure for R-3 Type VB construction. If the city values the actual unit differently — a different construction type, a different valuation edition — the permit, plan check, general plan, and records lines all move together. Confirm the valuation basis for the actual plans before treating $8,293 as final.

**2. The trade permits.** The retrieved formula does not state whether electrical, plumbing, and mechanical permits are inside the $8,293 or billed separately. In many cities they are separate lines; here they are simply unstated. That is the largest known unknown in this post, and it should be the first question to the city after the valuation basis.

**3. The school fee.** No district rate was verified, and [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) shows why the statewide ceiling cannot stand in for it. The school fee joins the floor by addition once the district confirms its rate, and it is the only remaining line large enough to move the total by thousands on its own.

## Piedmont vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Piedmont (verified floor) | **$310,293** | $8,293 permit bill; $0 impact fees; school fee excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Piedmont sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: its figure is missing its school fee and any trade permits, while the LA County figures include theirs. Add a school fee alone and Piedmont likely enters the range. What Piedmont genuinely demonstrates is that "no impact fees" and "cheap fees" are different claims: a city can charge nothing in impact fees and still present the batch's largest permit bill.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $310,293 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, confirm the valuation basis and the trade-permit treatment with the city, and carry a separate, explicit allowance for the school fee. When the city and district answer, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision.
