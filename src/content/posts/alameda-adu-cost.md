Alameda is the rare California city where the most dramatic fee line in the whole series is a zero. An 800 sq ft detached ADU here prices at a verified floor of **$306,304, or $383 per square foot** — and anyone researching Alameda ADU fees should start from that zero, because it reshapes everything around it. The city levies no development impact fee on ADUs at any size, which removes the 750 sq ft cliff that dominates the math in most other cities. What fills the gap is less forgiving: a school fee at the full state cap, a permit priced from a banded table with no flat ADU figure, and a utility layer governed by a regional water district whose charges turn on whether you add a meter. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Alameda does not publish phase-level construction costs.

The soft-costs phase is where Alameda's profile shows up most clearly. Design and engineering stay at the $14,000 baseline because the city's pre-approved plans are designer-owned rather than free. The school fee adds $4,304. The impact fee adds nothing. And the permit — the line that would normally sit in this phase — is deliberately left out of the total, because the city has not published a figure an 800 sq ft applicant can rely on. A floor that hides an unverified number inside it is not a floor.

A note on the denominator: the series sometimes frames an 800 sq ft ADU against a 2,000 sq ft main house, since 800 is 40% of 2,000. Alameda's fee schedule does not price construction itself, so no verified total for a 2,000 sq ft new house can be stated here, and none is estimated. The 40% ratio matters only as the proportion the state would use for impact fees on a larger ADU — and in Alameda, where the city charges ADUs nothing, that proportion multiplies zero.

## The $0 that changes the shape of the project

Most cities in this series make you do cliff arithmetic: a fee that is zero below 750 sq ft and suddenly real above it. Alameda skips the exercise entirely. Its development impact fee schedule, published as page 49 of the July 2026 Master Fee Schedule, carries a row for accessory dwelling units and junior ADUs with a zero in every column. The same schedule charges a new one-family dwelling $17,340. That contrast is the whole story in one line: the city treats the ADU as an accessory use of an existing home, not as a new house, and prices it accordingly.

The city's own ADU materials say the same thing without the table, and the policy predates the current schedule: the council's 2019 DIF ordinance amendments established the ADU exemption, so this is a settled position, not a one-year quirk. One open item: a standalone AB 602 web posting tying the schedule to its nexus study was not located during this review. The schedule itself is published and current; the nexus-study link is a verification item for the January review, not a reason to doubt the $0.

> **The state backstop.** State law bars an impact fee on an ADU under 750 sq ft, and requires any fee on a larger ADU to scale with the size of the main house. Alameda already goes further than that floor by charging nothing at any size, so the state rule is a backstop here, not the binding constraint. Note the recodification: the rule quoted in this series as Government Code §65852.2(f)(3)(A) now sits in the recodified ADU statutes, and SB 1117 (effective January 1, 2027) will tax only the area above 750 sq ft for a homeowner's first two ADUs. Neither change moves Alameda's number, because there is no city impact fee for either law to shrink — and school fees are not impact fees, so SB 1117 leaves the $4,304 untouched. For the general 750 sq ft cliff mechanics, see [750 sq ft cliff explained](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## The $4,304 school fee at the full state cap

If the impact fee is Alameda's gift, the school fee is its counterweight. Alameda Unified's developer-fee page publishes the State Allocation Board's January 28, 2026 Level 1 residential rate of $5.38 per square foot, adopted by the district board on March 24, 2026. On 800 sq ft that is $4,304 — the maximum the state allows at Level 1. This is the district's own current page, not a city's copy of it, a distinction that matters in a series where city fee tables have repeatedly lagged district rates. The fee is collected through the city's Planning and Building Department when the construction permit issues, so it lands on the applicant's invoice even though the money belongs to the district. For how school fees work across California, see [ADU school fees explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/).

Two boundaries worth knowing. First, the under-500 sq ft school-fee exemption does not help an 800 sq ft unit; it would matter only for a small conversion or junior unit. Second, the school fee is per square foot of the ADU itself, not proportional to the main house — unlike impact fees, there is no main-house ratio to soften it.

## The permit with no flat price

Alameda's building permit is the least quotable line in the post, and that is worth saying plainly rather than papering over with a false precision. The July 2026 Master Fee Schedule prices plan check and inspection for single-family dwellings in size bands, and the smallest band begins at 1,000 sq ft: $1,986 for plan check, $3,724 for inspection. An 800 sq ft ADU is smaller than the smallest band. Fee schedules normally treat a band's base as its floor, which would make the core $5,710 — but the schedule does not say that in words, and a homeowner should not budget on an inference. Add the $124 processing fee, the 6% technology fee, and the 0.5% community planning fee on construction valuation, and the arithmetic lands near $7,700 on a $302,000 valuation, before small state fees. That is the estimate, labeled as such, and it stays out of the floor total until the Permit Center confirms the band treatment.

Two adjacent charges deserve the same caution. The schedule lists design review for a new accessory building at $2,292.41 all-in, but the city's ADU handout describes ADU applications as eligible for concurrent review of a ministerial design review permit and building permit — not the same as confirming that the accessory-building rate applies to every detached ADU. And fire, public works, and Alameda Municipal Power charges sit outside the building tables entirely, so the electrical side is another verify item, not a number.

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. A new water meter.** This is the largest single swing in the post. Alameda's water comes from the East Bay Municipal Utility District, and EBMUD's FY2027 schedule prices a system capacity charge at $19,527 for a residential meter of 1 inch or smaller. The same schedule says ADU capacity charges apply only as authorized by state law — and state law says an ADU is not a new residential use for connection-fee purposes unless it was built with a new single-family dwelling. The reasonable expectation is that an ADU sharing the main house's existing service pays no new capacity charge, while an ADU on a new separate meter could face one. That is a $19,527 fork in the road hanging on a plumbing decision, and it is why the sewer/water line carries no number here. Verify with EBMUD before choosing a meter configuration.

**2. The permit band question.** If the Permit Center prices an 800 sq ft ADU at the 1,000 sq ft band minimum, the permit lands near the $7,700 estimate and the true all-in total rises to roughly $314,000. If it prices below the band, or if design review applies on top, the figure moves again. Either way the swing is a few thousand dollars, not tens of thousands — but it is the difference between a floor and a finished budget, and it should be resolved with one call before financing.

**3. Site conditions on an island lot.** Alameda's handout caps detached ADUs at 1,200 sq ft and 18 feet in height (20 feet where the roof pitch matches the main house), requires 4-foot side and rear setbacks, and allows a single ADU plus one junior ADU per single-family lot. The flood-zone footnote matters more here than in most cities: on lots in the special flood hazard area, the height limit can rise by the minimum needed to put finished floor a foot above base flood elevation. Fill, drainage, and flood compliance are site-work variables the $38,000 baseline may not cover on a low-lying lot. The handout also confirms no additional parking is required and no owner-occupancy for the ADU itself, while short-term rental under 30 days is prohibited — a revenue-case constraint, not a build-cost one.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026 and effective January 2027, SB 1117 changes impact-fee math for a homeowner's first two ADUs so that only square footage above 750 is taxed. Alameda is the null case for that reform: with a $0 city impact fee, there is nothing to recalculate, and the January 2027 review will confirm the zero holds rather than recompute it. School fees are outside SB 1117's definition and stay at $4,304.
