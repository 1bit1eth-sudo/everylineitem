Napa is the most expensive ledger in this series, and one line is the reason. The NapaSan sewer capacity charge for a detached 800 sq ft ADU is **$10,032.80** — 0.8 EDU at $12,541 per EDU (FY26/27, effective July 1, 2026) — the largest single verified ADU fee we have published. It is 44% of all verified fees in this ledger and more than the entire fee column in most cities we have covered. Our six-phase total lands at **$325,000, or $406 per square foot** — the first in the series above the $320,000 Los Angeles County baseline.

The rest of the ledger is itemised and ordinary. The building permit is a flat **$2,125**, including plan review, inspection, and fire and planning plan check. The school fee is **$4,304** from Napa Valley Unified at the $5.38 state maximum. Impact fees are $6,093: a $2,293 park dedication fee and $4.75 per square foot in affordable housing fees ($3,800). School and permit fees are both verified, so this total is not a floor on the series test — but two confirmations are pending for December 1: the park fee's July 2026 adjusted figure, and the building permit line under the council's revised schedule (Res. R2026-014, adopted February 3, 2026). Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Napa does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## The $10,033 sewer line

NapaSan prices sewer capacity in EDUs — equivalent dwelling units — and the banding is what makes this line so large. A detached ADU of 800–899 sq ft is 0.8 EDU under NapaSan Code §5.02.030.A.2, and the FY26/27 capacity charge is $12,541 per EDU, effective July 1, 2026. Multiply: 0.8 × $12,541 = $10,032.80. That is 44% of the $22,555 in verified fees in this ledger, and it is larger than the building permit and the school fee combined ($2,125 + $4,304 = $6,429).

The banding matters for anyone choosing a size. A JADU or an attached ADU is 0.0 EDU — no sewer capacity charge at all. A detached ADU in a lower band pays less. And the charge is a connection and capacity charge, not an impact fee, so SB 1117 does not touch it: the 2027 rule changes the impact-fee column only, and this $10,032.80 stands exactly as it is.

## The rest of the ledger

With the sewer line set aside, Napa's ledger is the most ordinary in the series — which is itself the point. The building permit is a single flat $2,125 that includes plan review, inspection, and fire and planning plan check: one number, no percentage of valuation, no add-ons to chase. The school fee is the state maximum $5.38 from a single unified district, $4,304, with no split to track. The impact fees are two lines: a flat $2,293 park dedication fee and $4.75 per square foot in affordable housing fees, $3,800. The affordable housing fee is the one line SB 1117 can move, and it moves cleanly — see below.

Two confirmations are pending for December 1, and both are flagged rather than assumed. The park dedication fee is adjusted annually by Parks & Recreation, and the July 1, 2026 adjusted figure is unverified — this ledger uses the $2,293 from the schedule effective February 3, 2025. And the council adopted a revised fee schedule on February 3, 2026 (Res. R2026-014), under which the $2,125 ADU permit line still needs confirmation. If either figure moves, this post will be corrected.

One side note for a different unit: the City of Napa runs a Junior Unit Initiative Program offering forgivable loans of up to $50,000 for JADUs — units inside the existing footprint, no more than 500 sq ft. That is a different unit from the 800 sq ft detached ADU in this ledger, and none of it is counted here. It is worth knowing about if the lot suits a junior unit instead.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Napa's affordable housing fee is $4.75 per square foot, so the 2027 charge on that line is 50 × $4.75 = $238 instead of $3,800 — a saving of 750 × $4.75, about **$3,562**. The flat park dedication fee of $2,293 has no published 2027 treatment, so the total saving is at least $3,562 and could be larger; this post does not guess at it. See the full comparison at [Napa: 2026 vs 2027, line by line](/sb-1117/napa/). School fees, permit fees and the sewer capacity charge are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and do not change.
