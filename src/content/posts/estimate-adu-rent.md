Every ROI spreadsheet starts with one invented number: the rent. This post replaces the invention with a method — comparable ADUs, sampled properly, adjusted honestly, and reality-checked against the 1% rule. The worked example at the end uses **labeled hypothetical assumptions**, not scraped listings. Your job is to rerun it with real comps from your neighborhood.

## The short answer

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Define your unit** | Beds, baths, sq ft, parking, who pays utilities, pet policy, furnished or not |
| **2. Sample ADU comps** | 6–10 recent listings of backyard cottages / in-law units / guest houses within ~3 miles, same bedroom count |
| **3. Adjust** | Normalize for utilities included, parking, furnishings, laundry, outdoor space |
| **4. Converge** | Throw out outliers, weight the closest matches, land on a range — not a point |
| **5. Reality-check** | 1% rule, vacancy haircut, and the series' LA County planning band ($2,600–$3,800/mo) |

## Why ADU comps — not apartments

It's tempting to pull the rent of a nearby 2-bed apartment and call it done. Don't. Apartments and ADUs sell different bundles:

| Factor | Apartment | Detached ADU |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Privacy** | Shared walls, halls, neighbors above | Standalone structure, own entrance |
| **Outdoor space** | Courtyard if you're lucky | Often a private yard or patio |
| **Parking** | Assigned stall, sometimes garaged | Driveway or street — varies by lot |
| **Pets** | Breed and weight restrictions common | Landlord's call — often more flexible |
| **Utilities** | Frequently bundled or submetered | Often tenant-paid, sometimes split with main house |
| **Management** | Professional, on-site | You, from your kitchen table |

An apartment comp can serve as a **ceiling sanity check** — if your ADU estimate lands far above nearby 2-bed apartments of similar size, something's off — but ADU-to-ADU is the honest comparison. Search terms that surface them: "in-law," "guest house," "back house," "granny flat," "ADU," "casita."

## Step 1: Define the unit you're pricing

Before sampling, write down the exact product — because every adjustment later hangs off this spec:

- Bedrooms, bathrooms, square footage, layout (open plan vs. closed bedrooms)
- **Parking:** dedicated spot, driveway sharing, or street only
- **Utilities:** tenant-paid, owner-paid, or split — and which ones (electric, gas, water, trash, internet)
- **Laundry:** in-unit, shared with main house, or none
- **Furnished or unfurnished** (furnished commands a premium but adds turnover cost and vacancy risk)
- **Pet policy** — pet-friendly units rent faster and often higher in ADU markets
- **Outdoor space:** private yard, shared yard, patio, none

Two identical 800 sq ft units can sit $400+/mo apart on utilities and parking alone. Define first, compare second.

## Step 2: Sample 6–10 comparable listings

Sources: **Zillow, Craigslist, Apartments.com, Facebook Marketplace.** Filters: within ~3 miles of your parcel (tighter in dense LA neighborhoods, wider in spread-out markets), same bedroom count, ADU-type keywords, listed or leased within the last 90 days.

Record for each comp:

| Field | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Asking rent and date listed | Stale listings are priced wrong or already gone |
| Beds / baths / sq ft | Match your spec; note deviations |
| Utilities included | The single biggest adjustment |
| Parking situation | Second-biggest |
| Furnished? Pets? Laundry? | Premiums and discounts live here |
| ADU type (detached, attached, garage, JADU) | Detached commands the premium; don't compare your detached cottage to someone's garage |

**Aim for 6–10 comps.** Fewer than five and one outlier owns your estimate. More than ten and you're sampling a different submarket.

**Advertised rent is a ceiling, not a result.** Listings show asking rents; leases close below them, and the listings that linger are the overpriced ones (survivorship bias). Mentally haircut the sample — or better, note days-on-market and weight fresh, fast-leased comps highest.

## Step 3: Adjust to your spec

Line up each comp against your defined unit and adjust. Direction matters more than precision — you're converging on a range:

| Comp differs from yours by… | Adjustment direction |
| --- | --- |
| Utilities included (yours are tenant-paid) | Adjust comp **down** |
| Dedicated parking (yours is street-only) | Adjust comp **down** |
| Furnished (yours is unfurnished) | Adjust comp **down** |
| Extra bedroom or 150+ sq ft larger | Adjust comp **down** |
| No laundry (yours has in-unit) | Adjust comp **up** |
| No-pet policy (yours allows pets) | Adjust comp **up** |
| Older finishes / awkward layout vs. your new build | Adjust comp **up** |

After adjusting, discard the top and bottom outliers, then weight what's left by similarity. The output is a **range**, e.g. "$3,100–$3,400/mo" — never a point estimate. Point estimates are how spreadsheets lie.

## Step 4: The 1% rule reality check

The **1% rule** is a crude investment screen: monthly rent should be roughly **1% of total project cost**. Run it against the series baseline:

| Project cost | 1% monthly rent target |
| --- | --- |
| $320,000 (series 800 sq ft build) | **$3,200/mo** |
| $381,000 (all-in year-one, from our [all-in post](/posts/adu-all-in-cost-california/)) | **$3,810/mo** |

Note that $3,200/mo sits at the **middle of the series' LA County planning band ($2,600–$3,800/mo)** used in our [payback post](/posts/adu-rent-payback-roi-california/) — the 1% rule and the comp-based band agree on the construction-cost basis. They diverge on the all-in basis, which is the honest signal: **clearing 1% on construction cost is achievable in LA County; clearing it on all-in cost requires top-of-band rents.** That's exactly the kind of tension the rule is meant to surface.

Use the 1% rule as a **filter, not a pricing method.** If your comp-derived range clears 1% of project cost, proceed to full underwriting. If it doesn't, don't round up the rent — rerun the deal with the [payback tables](/posts/adu-rent-payback-roi-california/) and decide with eyes open.

## Worked example (hypothetical — rerun with your comps)

**Assumed unit:** 800 sq ft detached ADU, 2 bed / 1 bath, new construction, dedicated driveway spot, tenant pays electric/gas, owner pays water/trash, in-unit laundry, pets allowed, unfurnished. Location: hypothetical LA County neighborhood.

**Six hypothetical comps** (labeled assumed — these are illustrations, not scraped listings):

| # | Comp | Asking | Key differences vs. our spec | Adjusted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2/1 detached, 750 sq ft, utils included | $3,500 | Utils included → down; slightly smaller → up | ~$3,300 |
| 2 | 2/1 detached, 820 sq ft, tenant-paid utils, street parking | $3,050 | Street parking → up | ~$3,200 |
| 3 | 2/2 detached, 900 sq ft, utils included, no pets | $3,650 | Bigger + 2nd bath + utils → down; no pets → up | ~$3,250 |
| 4 | 2/1 attached JADU-style, 600 sq ft | $2,950 | Attached, much smaller → up | ~$3,300 |
| 5 | 1/1 detached, 550 sq ft, furnished | $3,100 | Smaller + furnished → mixed; weak comp | discarded |
| 6 | 2/1 detached, 780 sq ft, tenant-paid utils, pets OK | $3,200 | Near-identical spec | ~$3,200 |

**Converged range: $3,150–$3,300/mo** (weighting comps 2 and 6 highest as closest matches).

**Reality checks:**
- 1% rule on $320,000 → $3,200/mo target: **clears**, mid-range.
- Series planning band $2,600–$3,800/mo: **inside**, slightly above middle.
- Next step: run $3,150–$3,300 through the [payback tables](/posts/adu-rent-payback-roi-california/) with a 5% vacancy haircut before treating it as income.

If your real comp set converges *below* $2,600 or *above* $3,800 for this spec, your submarket differs from the series baseline — trust your comps, not our band.

## What breaks rent estimates

| Breaker | How it shows up |
| --- | --- |
| **Apartment comps** | Overstates or understates by the amenity-bundle gap; always the wrong tool first |
| **Asking-rent bias** | Lingering listings are the overpriced ones; weight fresh leases |
| **Ignoring utilities** | A "$3,400 utils-included" comp is not a $3,400 comp for your tenant-paid unit |
| **Point estimates** | False precision; always publish a range |
| **Seasonality** | Summer listings lease faster and higher; winter comps in a summer pro forma flatter the deal |
| **Regulatory surprises** | Local rental-registry, licensing, or inspection requirements change the economics — underwrite locally |

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I hire a property manager to set the rent?**

A local manager who leases ADUs (not just apartments) will have real leased-not-listed data — the single most valuable input. Even a one-time rent survey engagement can pay for itself against a $300,000 build decision.

**How much does separate utility metering matter for rent?**

It matters more for *your* net than for the asking rent: separately metered tenants pay their own usage, removing the utility-cost risk from your side. In the comp set, normalize ruthlessly — utils-included vs. tenant-paid is the largest single adjustment.

**Does rent history help when I sell?**

Yes — a signed lease at market rent is evidence for the appraiser's income approach and a marketing asset for investor buyers. See [Selling a House With an ADU](/posts/selling-house-with-adu/) and [Does an ADU Raise Your Home's Appraised Value?](/posts/adu-appraised-value/).

**My comps are all over the place. What now?**

Widen the bedroom-count match before widening the radius — a 1-bed comp pool in your neighborhood beats a 2-bed pool three cities away. And if the range stays wide, that's information: your submarket prices ADUs inconsistently, so underwrite the low end.
