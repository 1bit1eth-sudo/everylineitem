A garage conversion is not a discounted detached ADU. It is a different project with a different phase mix: you reuse a shell, then spend hard on insulation, egress, fire separation, wet rooms, and — often — replacing the parking you just eliminated.

We modeled a **typical two-car garage conversion** in Los Angeles County: roughly **450 square feet of living area**, mid-grade finishes, existing slab retained where sound, flat lot. The total came to **$190,000, or about $422 per square foot** of living area.

That is less cash than a new [400 sq ft detached ADU](/posts/400-sq-ft-adu-cost-california/) at $215,000, and far less than an [800 sq ft detached](/posts/800-sq-ft-adu-cost-california/) at $320,000. It is not automatically the better deal — parking replacement, HOA rules, and local ordinances can erase the advantage or stop the project entirely.

## What you are actually buying

### Soft costs — $24,000

Still real. You need architectural drawings for the conversion, structural engineering for new openings and any loft or roof work, Title 24 energy documentation, plan check, and building permits. Fees are often lower than a full new detached valuation, but not free. See the [soft costs deep dive](/posts/adu-soft-costs-permits-california/) for line-item ranges.

### Site work + foundation — $14,000

This is where conversions save. You are not pouring a new stem wall and slab for a whole building. Typical work: moisture barrier or slab coating, limited saw-cutting for plumbing trenches, leveling or patching, and waterproofing at the garage door opening once it becomes a wall or glazed facade. If the existing slab is cracked, heaving, or below flood requirements, this line climbs fast — sometimes enough to erase the conversion advantage.

### Framing + structure — $22,000

Interior partitions for bath and kitchen, headers over new windows and the former garage door opening, possible ceiling or roof framing if you need height or a loft, and shear or hold-down work if you punch new openings in braced walls. Cheaper than framing an entire 400–800 sq ft shell. Not a paint-and-carpet job.

### Building envelope — $28,000

This phase **grows** relative to what homeowners expect. Uninsulated garage walls and roofs become conditioned dwelling envelope. That means insulation to current code, air sealing, new or enlarged windows for light and egress, exterior finishes where the garage door was, and fire-resistance detailing between the ADU and the main dwelling when attached or close. Skip this phase and you get a cold, illegal, or unpermitted unit.

### Systems + interior — $85,000

Still about 45% of the budget. Full kitchen, full bath, electrical panel or subpanel work, plumbing supply and waste from the main house or new lateral, heat pump or equivalent, drywall, flooring, paint, fixtures, appliances. Absolute dollars sit below the 400 detached model's $102,000 because you are not building as much new shell around the wet rooms — but the wet rooms themselves do not get cheaper.

### Landscaping & hardscape — $17,000

On many lots this is where conversions hurt. California ADU rules generally protect the right to convert, but **replacing required parking** — or satisfying an HOA that still thinks in pre-state-law terms — can mean a new driveway apron, uncovered stalls, or alley access work. Budget it. If parking replacement is impossible on your lot, the project may be dead regardless of the construction number.

## When the conversion wins

- The garage is already in the right place relative to setbacks and utilities
- The slab and roof structure are sound
- You need a studio or compact one-bed, not 800 feet
- Absolute cash or loan size is the constraint
- You accept that the unit will feel like a conversion (ceiling height, window placement) unless you spend to change it

## When the conversion loses — or dies

**Parking replacement.** If local practice or an HOA still demands replacement stalls you cannot fit, stop before you pay for drawings.

**HOA and CC&Rs.** State ADU law has narrowed what associations can ban, but architectural review, rental restrictions, and parking covenants still create delay and cost. Get written clarity early.

**Fire and egress.** Bedrooms need compliant egress. Attached configurations need fire separation. These are not optional upgrades.

**Bad slab or low ceiling.** Replacing a slab or raising a roof can push a "cheap conversion" past new-build studio pricing.

**Utility capacity.** Panel upgrades and sewer tie-ins still apply. Distance and upsizing do not care that the walls already exist.

**Local rules.** Always verify with the current municipal ADU ordinance and the California HCD ADU Handbook. Do not rely on a neighbor's 2019 conversion story.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does a garage conversion ADU cost in California?**

This model lands at **$190,000** for a typical two-car garage conversion (~450 sq ft living) in Los Angeles County with mid-grade finishes. Sound structures with easy utilities can come in lower; slab replacement, roof raises, and parking work push higher.

**Is a garage conversion cheaper than building new?**

Usually cheaper in absolute dollars than a new detached unit of similar living area — this model is $25,000 below our 400 sq ft detached and $130,000 below our 800. Per-square-foot can still look high if living area is small, and parking/HOA issues can wipe out the savings.

**Do I still need permits for a garage conversion?**

Yes. A conversion to dwelling space requires permits, energy compliance, and inspections. Unpermitted conversions create rental and resale problems the same way unpermitted new ADUs do.

**What about parking?**

Budget for replacement parking hardscape in the landscaping phase, and verify local and HOA rules before design. Parking is one of the most common reasons garage conversions stall.

**How long does a garage conversion take?**

Often shorter in pure construction than a new detached build — commonly on the order of a few months of construction once permits are in hand — but permitting and HOA review can still dominate the calendar.

**Should I convert or build detached?**

Convert when the garage is sound, parking is solvable, and you want lower absolute cost. Build detached when you need more space, the garage is poor quality, or conversion rules/parking make the retrofit a dead end.
