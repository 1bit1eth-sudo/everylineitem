The 750 sq ft cliff gets the headlines, but a quieter trend is running underneath it: cities **waiving ADU fees outright**, no cliff required. Two California cities in our data have now zeroed out major fee categories for ADUs — each through a different legal mechanism, each with different durability. A third, Oceanside, looked like the strongest waiver of all: our September 2025 capture of the city's ADU page said all development impact fees under the city's control had been waived. That language is no longer on the live page, and the city says information relying on the ADU ordinance is no longer applicable while it certifies the ordinance with HCD — so Oceanside's waiver is unconfirmed, not $0.

## The three cases

### Salinas: the full DIF waiver

Salinas is the first city in our series with a **complete development impact fee waiver for ADUs** — not a reduction, not a cliff, a waiver. The fee schedule simply doesn't apply the DIF to ADU permits. For an 800 sq ft unit, that's the entire impact-fee line — potentially $5,000–$10,000+ in peer cities — at $0.

Legal footing: fee schedule / council action. Durability: medium — a future council can reintroduce the fee, but there's no pending study or draft suggesting one.

### Oceanside: the waiver we could not confirm

Oceanside looked like the strongest waiver of the three: our September 2025 capture of the city's ADU page stated that all development impact fees under the city's control had been waived. That language is no longer on the live page. The city is certifying Article 30, Section 3006 of the Zoning Ordinance (the ADU ordinance) with HCD, and says any information previously provided that relied on or referenced those ADU regulations is no longer applicable. Until the certification lands, the waiver is **not confirmed** — treat Oceanside as $0 unproven, not $0.

Separately, a **2025 draft parks nexus study** (Economic & Planning Systems, August 22, 2025) led to a new parks fee schedule adopted November 19, 2025 (single-family $6.03/sq ft, effective January 20, 2026). Whether that schedule applies to ADUs is not confirmed — the table is headed "Residential Only" and does not mention ADUs. State law still backstops the smallest units: under Gov. Code §66311.5, impact fees are $0 for ADUs of 750 sq ft or less. If you're building in Oceanside, verify the current ordinance before you budget, and don't assume the waiver is untouched.

### Oakland: the documented exemption

Oakland's ADU guidebook states ADUs are **exempt from impact fees** — the clearest single-document record of the three. Unlike Salinas (fee schedule) or Oceanside (waiver unconfirmed), Oakland's exemption reads as settled policy: the city has considered the question and answered it.

## Why cities waive

The logic is straightforward: impact fees exist to make new development pay for the infrastructure load it creates. ADUs — particularly sub-750 sq ft units — create marginal load on infrastructure sized for the primary dwelling. Several cities have concluded the administrative cost of calculating, billing, and defending small ADU fees exceeds the revenue.

There's also a housing-policy motive. California's ADU laws (SB 13, AB 68, and their successors) steadily stripped cities' power to block ADUs; fee waivers are the carrot cities offer voluntarily, often tied to housing-element compliance pressure from HCD.

## The durability ranking

Not all $0s are equal:

1. **Oakland** — documented exemption in the city's ADU guidebook. Strongest signal of settled policy.
2. **Salinas** — fee-schedule waiver, no pending threat identified. Solid until a council decides otherwise.
3. **Oceanside** — waiver unconfirmed: the city's ADU page no longer carries the waiver language, and the ordinance is under HCD certification. Re-check before you budget.

And the statewide backstop: **SB 1117** (effective January 1, 2027) reshapes the cliff everywhere — fees only on square footage above 750, for the first two ADUs. In high-fee cities, that's a bigger saving than any local waiver. In waiver cities, it's belt and suspenders.

## What this means for your build

1. **Check the waiver before you check the fee.** If your city waives, the entire impact-fee research step collapses to one confirmation.
2. **Confirm the legal instrument.** Ordinance > resolution > fee schedule, in amendment difficulty. Ask the planning counter which one your waiver lives in.
3. **Watch for studies.** A nexus study is the leading indicator that a waiver is about to narrow. Oceanside's 2025 parks study — adopted as a new fee schedule months later, while the waiver language itself disappeared from the city's page — is the template: if your city commissions one, budget as if the waiver has an expiration date.
4. **Waivers don't cover everything.** School district fees are set by school districts, not cities — no city waiver touches them. Plan-check, permit, and utility charges usually survive too.

## The bottom line

Two confirmed $0s, one unconfirmed, three different shelf lives. The waiver trend is real and growing — but a waived fee is a policy choice, not a physical law. Verify it exists on the day you pull the permit, not the day you read this post.
