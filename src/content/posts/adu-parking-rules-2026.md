A parking space is concrete, grading, drainage, and sometimes a retaining wall. It is also one of the few ADU costs you can erase entirely — if your project fits one of five exemptions written into state law.

This guide covers when cities cannot require ADU parking, the one-space cap that applies when they can, and the garage-conversion trap owners still fall into.

> This is a general guide to California ADU law, not legal advice. Local ordinances vary; confirm your city's specific standards before designing.

## The five exemptions

Under Gov. Code **§66314(d)(10)-(11)**, a city may not impose any parking standards for an ADU when any one of these is true:

1. **The ADU is within one-half mile of public transit.** This is the big one. In most California cities, half a mile from a bus stop covers a large share of residential lots. Measure it — many owners are exempt and do not know it.
2. **The ADU is within an architecturally and historically significant historic district.** Note the qualifier: the district must be designated as architecturally or historically significant, not merely old.
3. **The ADU is part of the existing primary residence or an existing accessory structure.** Converting a garage, a portion of the home, or an existing pool house? No parking requirement.
4. **On-street parking permits are required but not offered to the ADU occupant.** If the city runs a permit district and excludes your tenant from it, the city cannot demand you build them a space.
5. **A car share vehicle is located within one block of the ADU.** One block — not one mile. Check actual car share placement, not just service-area maps.

If any single exemption applies, the city's parking requirement is zero. You do not need to stack them.

## The one-space maximum

Where none of the five exemptions applies, state law still caps what a city can demand: **one parking space per ADU, maximum**. Additional rules that make the one space cheap to provide:

- **Tandem parking is allowed** — two cars nose-to-tail in one driveway-length strip.
- **The space may be in a driveway** — it does not have to be a dedicated pad or a new structure.
- **Covered, uncovered, or mechanical lift** configurations are all permitted for replacement situations.

A city cannot require two spaces, cannot require a covered garage, and cannot reject a compliant tandem-in-driveway layout.

## The garage-conversion parking trap

This is the scenario that still costs owners money: you demolish or convert your garage to build the ADU, and the city says you must replace the lost parking.

State law allows the replacement — but **the replacement spaces may go anywhere on the lot**, in any of these forms:

- Covered or uncovered,
- Tandem,
- Mechanical lift.

The old expectation — replace the garage in kind, with a new enclosed structure — is gone. A simple uncovered tandem pad on an existing driveway often satisfies the requirement. Do not let anyone sell you a $30,000 garage rebuild when the law permits a fraction of that.

## The cost angle: what each space is worth

Every required parking space is real construction: demolition, grading, base rock, concrete or pavers, drainage tie-in, and sometimes retaining or a curb cut. Owners typically see **$5,000–$15,000+ per space** — a range, because a flat existing driveway pad costs far less than a sloped lot needing a retaining wall and new drainage.

Two implications:

- **Check exemptions before you design.** If the half-mile transit exemption applies, a space you were planning to pour is $5,000–$15,000+ you keep.
- **Price the space honestly in your budget.** If you do need one, get it in the site-work estimate early — it is one of the most commonly forgotten line items in owner-managed builds.

## Check your city's specific standards

State law sets the ceiling — zero spaces in the five exemption cases, one space maximum otherwise, flexible replacement for garage conversions — but cities write the implementing details: how they measure the half-mile, which districts count as historic, and how they verify car share locations. Before finalizing your site plan:

1. Measure your lot against each of the five exemptions — transit distance first, since it exempts the most lots.
2. If you are converting a garage or existing structure, confirm the exemption in writing with the planning counter.
3. If replacement parking is required, propose the cheapest compliant layout (uncovered, tandem, in the existing driveway) and get it approved on paper.
4. Budget any required space as site work with a range, not a single optimistic figure.

Parking is one of the few ADU costs governed almost entirely by a short list of clear rules. Learn the five exemptions, and you may find your cheapest line item is the one you never build.
