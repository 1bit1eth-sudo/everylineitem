Region is the variable you cannot redesign away. The same 800 sq ft detached plan that models at **$320,000 ($400 / sq ft)** as a Los Angeles County median in this series does not cost the same on a Westside coastal lot as it does on a flat Inland Empire parcel.

We held the drawings constant and priced two Southern California bands: a **LA coastal** setting and an **Inland Empire** setting. Coastal landed near **$380,000 ($475 / sq ft)**. Inland landed near **$300,000 ($375 / sq ft)**. That is an **$80,000** gap — about **21%** — inside the broader **$375–$600 / sq ft** Southern California site-built band this series has used from the start.

Three patterns jump out:

1. **Soft costs** nearly jump from inland to coastal ($26k → $42k). Fees and coastal review live here.
2. **Site work** rises with coastal access, soils, and drainage expectations ($34k → $48k).
3. **Systems + interior** still dominate every column — but coastal finish labor and inspection cycles push the largest absolute dollars highest on the coast ($162k).

Framing and envelope move with regional labor rates. Landscaping on the coastal model includes a heavier hardscape / drainage / access restoration allowance — coastal lots punish temporary construction damage.

## What drives the swing

### 1. Labor

Southern California is not one labor market. Coastal and near-coastal crews often price higher for the same rough carpentry, MEP, and finish work. Inland framing and concrete can clear calendars at lower fully burdened rates. In this model, framing + envelope + systems together run **$227,000** inland vs **$268,000** coastal — a **$41,000** labor-and-productivity gap on the same quantities.

### 2. Fees and agency path

[Soft costs](/posts/adu-soft-costs-permits-california/) already vary more by jurisdiction than by square footage. Coastal cities layer:

- Higher plan-check and permit fee schedules in many cases
- Separate **coastal development** or local coastal program review where the Coastal Act applies
- More frequent design-review or discretionary touchpoints even when state ADU law limits denial grounds
- Utility districts with different capacity charges

Inland cities are not "fee-free," but the median path from first drawing to issued permit is often shorter and cheaper on paper. Our soft-cost line: **$26,000 inland vs $42,000 coastal**.

### 3. Coastal review (when it applies)

Not every "coastal" ZIP requires a Coastal Development Permit for an ADU — exemptions and local implementations matter. When review **does** apply, expect:

- Extra documentation and findings
- Longer schedule (which feeds [loan interest in the all-in stack](/posts/adu-all-in-cost-california/))
- Occasional design concessions that add structure or shrink openings

Budget time and soft cost even when your architect is optimistic. Optimism is not a fee schedule.

### 4. Site access

Coastal lots in the LA band are more often:

- Narrower street frontage
- Steeper or more constrained rear yards
- Tighter neighbor setbacks and staging rules
- Harder crane and concrete-pump logistics

That shows up in site work ($48k coastal vs $34k inland) and in whether [modular delivery](/posts/modular-vs-site-built-adu-cost-california/) is even viable. Inland flat lots with wide streets are where factory-built savings are easiest to keep.

If your coastal lot is flat, wide-open, and outside coastal permit jurisdiction, you may sit closer to the series **$320k** median than to $380k. If your inland lot is hillside with long utility runs, you may sit closer to coastal numbers than the Inland Empire median implies. **Region sets the band; the lot picks the seat.**

## How this maps to the $375–$600 / sq ft band

| Band | Approx. $/sq ft | What it usually means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inland / easier SoCal sites | **$375–$425** | Flat access, competitive labor, simpler fees |
| LA County median (this series) | **~$400** | Modeled mid-grade detached baseline |
| Coastal / constrained SoCal | **$450–$550** | Labor premium + fees + access |
| Coastal custom / hillside / luxury finishes | **$550–$600+** | Outside these mid-grade models |

The [400 sq ft post](/posts/400-sq-ft-adu-cost-california/) noted that coastal vs inland can swing **$75–$150 per square foot**. On 800 feet, that is roughly **$60,000–$120,000** — and our $80,000 coastal-vs-inland delta sits comfortably inside that range.

## What does not change with ZIP code

- One kitchen and one bathroom still dominate systems
- Halving the unit still fails to halve the price ([400 vs 800](/posts/400-sq-ft-adu-cost-california/))
- Contingency still belongs in the budget ([all-in cost](/posts/adu-all-in-cost-california/))
- Prop 13 still adds the ADU only, not a full home reassessment

Geography multiplies the same structure. It does not invent a new one.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much more does a coastal ADU cost in Southern California?**

In this same-plan model, the LA coastal band runs about **$80,000 more** than an Inland Empire build ($380k vs $300k), or roughly **$75 more per square foot**. Extreme coastal custom and hillside work can exceed that.

**Is the Inland Empire always cheaper?**

Usually on labor and often on fees — not always on soils, utilities, or HOA constraints. Compare phase-by-phase for your lot.

**Does coastal review always apply near the ocean?**

No. Coastal Act jurisdiction and local coastal programs are mapped. Ask your planner or architect whether your parcel needs a Coastal Development Permit or qualifies for an exemption / exclusion.

**Should I use the $320k series number if I am coastal?**

Use $320k as the **LA County median** anchor, then stress-test toward **$450–$500+ / sq ft** if you are truly coastal and constrained. Do not average yourself into a false comfort.
