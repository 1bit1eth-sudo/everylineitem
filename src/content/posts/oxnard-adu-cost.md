Oxnard is the first coastal city in this series, and its fee profile reads like one: a standard 750 sq ft cliff written into ordinance, a valuation-based permit schedule the city declines to price out per square foot, and a school fee split across two districts because the city's children are educated by committee. Nothing here is exotic. That is the point — Oxnard is the control case for what a mid-size California coastal city charges when it follows the state playbook exactly.

Our six-phase total lands at **$308,000, or $385 per square foot** — a floor, not a total, marked with the asterisk where it appears. The building permit dollar figure could not be derived (the schedule is confirmed; the city's ADU valuation method is not), and the proportional DIF amount is excluded because the base dwelling fees are unpublished. The one fee we can count that other cities leave out: the **$1,705 ADU planning review**. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Oxnard does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## The permit you can't price from home

Oxnard's Master Fee Schedule is public, current (FY 2026-27), and mathematically complete — the valuation tiers run from $31.50 on the first $750 all the way to 0.30% above $1.5 million. What it cannot do is tell a homeowner what an 800 sq ft ADU costs to permit, because the city does not publish the one input the whole schedule needs: how it derives construction valuation per square foot for an ADU. Whether it uses the ICC Building Valuation Data, its own table, or the Building Official's judgment is not stated in the public documents. So the schedule is transparent and the answer is opaque — the exact inverse of Chula Vista's flat $3,951, and the reason the permit line carries the largest asterisk in this post. The planning review is the consolation prize: **$1,705** for the ADU review, published line-item, counted in the total.

## The 750 sq ft cliff in Ordinance Sec. 15-183

The cliff mechanics are standard-issue California: under 750 sq ft, no fees; at 750 and above, proportional to the primary dwelling by area ratio — written into Municipal Code Sec. 15-183 under Ordinance No. 2979. What makes Oxnard's entry worth reading is the footnote. The code text available online still shows the older ordinance language, and the City Council adopted an ADU ordinance revision (PZ 23-5383, Chapter 16) on December 16, 2025 that had not yet been reflected in the public code at research time. Whether the fee provisions changed in that revision is unconfirmed — so treat the cliff as confirmed by ordinance and the current wording as worth a second look at the January 2027 review.

## A school fee paid to two districts

Oxnard's $4,136 school fee arrives in two envelopes. The Oxnard School District (K–8) charges **$3.41 per square foot** — $2,728 on 800 — from its 2024 developer fee study, representing 66% of the combined rate. The Oxnard Union High School District (9–12) charges roughly **$1.76** — $1,408 on 800 — within its 2024 justification study's $1.50–$2.07 range. The OUHSD side has a clean trigger: new construction, ADUs included, above 500 sq ft pays — 800 sq ft is in. The unconfirmed piece is the 2026 update: the state ceiling moved to $5.38, and we found no adopted 2026 resolution from either district. Two more caveats for address-checking: some Oxnard addresses fall in the Rio, Hueneme, or Pleasant Valley elementary districts instead of Oxnard SD, and the split ratio follows inter-district agreements. $4,136 is the confirmed combined rate for the standard case.

## Sewer and water: only if you touch the connection

Oxnard's utility posture is the friendliest reading of state law in the series. Municipal Code Sec. 15-197 limits planned wastewater facility fees to **new connections or meter upsizings** — an ADU that shares the existing lateral and meter should not trigger capacity charges at all. The physical plumbing is separate from the capacity question: sewer tie-in installation runs $470 on an existing lateral to $1,175–$1,485 on a new main connection, but that is pipe, not privilege. The city ordinance has no ADU-specific utility sentence we could find, so the new-connection-only reading is confirmed as policy but not as ADU language. Both lines stay excluded and marked "verify with city."

## Pre-approved plans, borrowed from two counties

Oxnard does not maintain its own pre-approved ADU plan library — it **accepts pre-approved plans from the Ventura County and San Diego County programs**, consistent with AB 1332, per the city's Building & Engineering division. The benefit is speed: total review compresses to 30 days. There is no fee discount attached — plan check costs what plan check costs. For homeowners, the practical move is shopping the county libraries for a design under 750 sq ft, where the cliff exemption makes the DIF question moot.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Oxnard's Sec. 15-183 proportional formula cannot survive that math unchanged — an 800 sq ft ADU would owe fees on 50 sq ft, not 800. The open question is whether the December 2025 ordinance revision (PZ 23-5383) already moved the city toward marginal math; the January 2027 quarterly review will check the current code text. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and stay at $4,136 pending the districts' 2026 resolutions.
