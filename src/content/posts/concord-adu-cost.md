Concord's impact fees come in a band, not a line. The Off-Site Street Improvement Program (OSIP) and the Park Land fee are flat amounts for ADUs of 750–1,200 sq ft, and each has two tiers: OSIP is $1,073 or $1,626, Park Land is $5,508 or $8,345, depending on whether the ADU exceeds 40% of the primary dwelling. For an 800 sq ft ADU, the boundary is a 2,000 sq ft primary home — and the size of the house already on the lot is the one thing this ledger cannot know. That single unknown moves the total by $3,000, so the ledger is a range: **$313,000–$316,000, or $391–$395 per square foot**. And it is a floor: the building permit fee is not yet verified, so it is not in the total at all.

The offset is real. Concord offers **six free city pre-approved ADU plans**, from a 205 sq ft efficiency studio to an 874 sq ft two-bedroom, with typical plan-check fees waived and permits possible within five working days for a complete submittal. The school fee is $4,136 from Mt. Diablo Unified at $5.17 per square foot — the 2024-25 study rate, with no 2026 increase published. Planning is $0: ADUs are reviewed ministerially. The sewer application fee is $157; the sewer capacity fee itself is unverified and not in the total. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Concord does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## The 40% line

The tier rule is the whole story of this ledger, and it is worth reading slowly. Both impact fees are flat amounts for ADUs of 750–1,200 sq ft — not per-square-foot, not a percentage of valuation. Each fee has two tiers, and the tier is decided by one ratio: the ADU's size against the primary dwelling. If the ADU exceeds 40% of the primary dwelling, OSIP is $1,626 and Park Land is $8,345. If it is 40% or smaller, OSIP is $1,073 and Park Land is $5,508. For an 800 sq ft ADU, the boundary is a 2,000 sq ft primary home: a 1,900 sq ft house puts you in the high tier, a 2,100 sq ft house puts you in the low tier, and the difference is $3,390 across the two fees.

Both tiers are recorded as confirmed figures because the fee schedule does not fix the primary dwelling's size — the schedule gives the rule, not the answer for a particular lot. And both fees are $0 below 750 sq ft, which is the cliff doing its work: build at 750 and the impact-fee total is $0; build at 800 and it is $6,581–$9,971. The cliff is written into the band itself.

## Six free plans, and the rebate that just closed

Concord's free-plan program is the most complete in this series so far. Six construction-level designs the city already approved — a 205 sq ft efficiency studio, a 394 sq ft studio, three one-bedroom designs at 627, 650 and 709 sq ft, and an 874 sq ft two-bedroom — in multiple architectural styles (Bungalow/Craftsman, Spanish-inspired, post-war suburban rancher, modern). There is no cost for design fees and typical plan-check fees are waived; the applicant provides a basic site plan, and hourly plan-check fees apply to the review of that. A complete site-specific submittal can be issued within five working days.

The same program used to come with cash. Concord ran an ADU rebate program, funded by a $277,226 California Housing Finance Agency grant, offering up to $15,000 for income-restricted units or up to $7,500 for market-rate units — but applications closed on September 30, 2026, and the funding was first-come, first-served. This ledger does not count the rebate, because the window has closed. Ask the city whether any new incentives are available before budgeting around one.

The one gap in this ledger sits next to the free plans. The building permit and plan-check fee is not verified — no rate table, no flat fee, nothing to price. The free plans waive typical plan-check fees, which softens the gap, but the permit fee itself is a real cost a homeowner will pay, and it is not in the $313,000–$316,000. When it is verified, this post will be updated and the total will rise.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Concord's impact fees are flat amounts in a 750–1,200 sq ft band, and the city has not published how a flat band converts to a marginal charge on the 50 sq ft above 750 — so no 2027 saving is computed here. The 2027 treatment of flat-band fees is uncertain, and this post will not guess at it. See the full comparison at [Concord: 2026 vs 2027, line by line](/sb-1117/concord/). School fees, permit fees and sewer charges are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and do not change.
