> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Richmond costs at least **$307,602, or about $385 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and anyone searching for Richmond ADU fees should read the headline of this post the way it is written, not the way it is easy to misread. The $1,466 in the title is a planning review, not a permit. It is flat, it is verified, and it is only one line. Paired with a school fee that is equally flat in its arithmetic — $5.17 times 800 square feet — Richmond's counted stack is two lines that do not pretend to be a complete permit bill. This post counts those two lines, names the missing building permit plainly, and refuses to let a tidy total pass for a finished budget.

## A flat planning line, and what it is not

Richmond's FY 2026-27 Master Fee Schedule, adopted June 2026, prices its planning permit fees as a list, and the ADU entry in that list is unusually clean: "ADU (attached/detached) zoning compliance review, $1,466.00 flat." Flat matters here. Most permit-adjacent charges in this series scale with valuation or floor area, which means they must be recomputed for every design. This one does not scale: the review costs the same whether the ADU is 800 square feet or a different size, because the work being priced is a compliance review, not a valuation-based permit.

That clarity cuts both ways. A flat planning line is easy to verify and easy to quote — and easy to mistake for the whole cost of permission. It is not. The building permit, the plan check, and inspections sit in a different part of the schedule, and none of them was converted into a verified ADU figure in this round. The honest statement of Richmond's permission cost today is therefore two-part: $1,466 verified for planning, building permit unknown. Any quote that merges those two parts into one number without citing a schedule edition should be treated as unverified.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. Neither counted line in this post is changed by that rule: the planning review is a fee-schedule charge, not an impact fee, and the school fee is a separate district charge that applies to an 800 sq ft ADU in full. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) likewise leaves both counted lines unchanged; it flattens impact fees for lots with two or fewer ADUs, and no impact fee is counted here. The size rule that does matter for this post is the school-fee exemption: an ADU smaller than 500 square feet pays no school fee, while this 800 sq ft unit pays the full $4,136. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The building permit, whole.** This is the same warning as in the planning section, stated as a budget line: the building permit and plan check are entirely missing from this floor, and in comparable cities they run into the thousands. When the city prices the actual unit, that figure joins the floor by addition, and it is likely to be the largest single addition this post will ever receive. Do not budget Richmond as if $1,466 were the permission cost.

**2. The uncounted impact position and the sewer charges.** No other city impact fee and no sewer or water charge was verified, and both categories are capable of adding thousands. Confirm them with the city and the district before budgeting beyond the floor; a floor with two verified lines is useful precisely because it makes the unverified lines visible instead of hiding them inside a rounded total.

**3. The school fee's district, not the ceiling.** The $4,136 is the district's $5.17 rate, not the statewide $5.38 ceiling, and the difference — $168 on an 800 sq ft unit — is the size of the error a reader makes by substituting the ceiling for the rate. [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) covers why that substitution fails generally; here the district document settles it specifically, under Resolution 2425-58.

## Richmond vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Richmond (verified floor) | **$307,602** | $1,466 planning review + $4,136 school fee; building permit excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Richmond sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: its figure is missing its building permit, its other impact fees, and its sewer charges, while the LA County figures include theirs. Once the building permit alone joins the total, expect Richmond to move into or past the range. What Richmond genuinely offers is a planning line that can be quoted exactly, without valuation arithmetic — a small certainty in a post that is otherwise explicit about its unknowns.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $307,602 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, carry separate, explicit allowances for the building permit and plan check, other impact fees, and sewer charges, and do not let the $1,466 planning line stand in for the permission cost. When the city and district answer, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision.
