Thirty-six days. That is all that stands between today's 750 sq ft cliff and the new marginal rule.

On January 1, 2027, [SB 1117](/posts/sb-1117-explained-plain-english/) takes effect, and impact fees on the first two ADUs per lot may be charged only on the livable square footage above 750 sq ft — not the whole unit. An 800 sq ft ADU in Encinitas goes from a $22,040 impact-fee bill to roughly $1,378. That is ~$20,663 in savings, created by a calendar date.

So the question every homeowner with plans in the drawer is asking this week: **do I pull my permit now, or wait for January?**

This is a decision framework, not a prescription. Your city, your ADU's size, and your schedule all matter. Work through it honestly.

## First, know where you stand: the calendar

It is Thanksgiving. City halls are closed tomorrow (Friday). The remaining business days of 2026 are ticking down — and that is what actually matters for a permit, not the calendar days.

If your drawings are not submitted yet, be realistic: many California cities take weeks to months to issue a building permit after submittal. A permit *submitted* in December but *issued* in January may land on either side of the rule change depending on when your city assesses fees — which is typically at permit issuance, but practice varies by jurisdiction. **Call your city's building division and ask: on what date are impact fees assessed?**

That single question decides everything below.

## The framework: who should pull in December vs wait for January

| Your situation | Pull in December | Wait for January |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ADU over 750 sq ft, steep-cliff city (Encinitas, Ontario, Palo Alto — five-figure cliff) | | **Wait** — the marginal rule saves real money |
| ADU over 750 sq ft, moderate-cliff city | Either — weigh savings vs schedule | **Lean wait** if savings exceed a month of carry |
| ADU over 750 sq ft, $0 DIF city (Salinas, Oakland, Fremont, Santa Monica, Oceanside) | **No timing advantage** — pull when ready | |
| ADU at or under 750 sq ft | **No timing advantage** — pull when ready | |
| Construction loan already closed with a draw deadline | **Pull** — loan terms beat fee savings | |
| Contractor mobilized and scheduled | **Pull** — idle-contractor costs beat fee savings | |

The core logic: **the law only helps, it never hurts.** A December permit does not get penalized — it simply gets 2026 rules, the same rules that applied yesterday. The only question is whether the January savings are worth the wait.

## The steep-cliff cities: waiting pays

Three cities in our research show cliffs steep enough that waiting is a five-figure decision:

- **Encinitas:** $27.55/sq ft impact fee on the full 800 sq ft today = **$22,040**. Under SB 1117's marginal rule, 50 billable sq ft x $27.55 = **$1,377.50**. Savings: **~$20,663**. ([Full research](/posts/encinitas-adu-cost/) — pending 2027 schedule verification.)
- **Ontario:** an 800 sq ft ADU behind a 1,600 sq ft primary in General City pays **$18,797.50** today (prorated against the primary dwelling). Marginal treatment brings it to roughly **$1,175**. Savings: **~$17,623**.
- **Palo Alto:** current proportional DIF of roughly **$8,000–$20,000** on an 800 sq ft ADU; marginal treatment drops it to roughly **$500–$1,250**. Savings: up to **~$18,750**.

These are illustrative figures from 2026 rate schedules — exact 2027 numbers depend on each city's published schedules and implementation, which we will verify in the December 1 quarterly re-verification. But the order of magnitude is what matters for a timing decision: five figures is worth a month.

## The zero-cliff cities: timing does not matter

If your city charges no impact fees on ADUs, there is nothing for SB 1117 to reduce, and the December-vs-January question is moot:

- **Salinas:** no city DIF or traffic impact fees on ADUs (city policy).
- **Oakland:** no impact fees on ADUs.
- **Fremont, Santa Monica, Oceanside:** $0 ADU DIF in our fee-matrix research.

In these cities, pull your permit when the project is ready — holidays or not.

## The costs of waiting (be honest about them)

Waiting for January is not free. Put these on the other side of the ledger:

- **Carrying costs.** If your construction loan is closed, every month of delay costs interest on the drawn amount. If you are paying rent while planning to move into the ADU, that is a monthly number too.
- **Contractor scheduling.** A contractor booked for a December start who sits idle into January may reprice the job — or take another job. An idle crew is the most expensive kind.
- **Rate and price drift.** Material and labor prices do not wait for the calendar. Neither do your quotes' expiration dates.
- **Implementation lag.** How each city implements the marginal rule — new fee schedules, updated billing systems — is still pending verification. A January permit in a city that has not yet published its 2027 schedule could face administrative friction. Ask the building division now.
- **The holiday crunch.** Building divisions run on skeleton staffing through the holidays. Plan checks submitted in mid-December often sit until January — which, conveniently, is exactly when you want them issued.

A rough rule of thumb: if the expected SB 1117 savings are larger than one to two months of your project's carrying costs, waiting pencils out. If they are smaller, pull now.

## The risks of rushing

Rushing a December permit has its own hazards:

- **Paying the cliff for nothing.** The most expensive mistake: pulling in December to "get it done" and paying $22,040 in Encinitas when a January permit would have cost $1,378. That is a $20,663 impatience tax.
- **Half-baked submittals.** Rushed plans come back with corrections, and a correction cycle easily pushes issuance into January anyway — at which point you rushed for no benefit and paid for the privilege in revision fees.
- **No retroactive benefit.** A permit assessed under 2026 rules does not get re-assessed under 2027 rules. The date of assessment is (nearly always) final. Confirm it with your city.

## Your Thanksgiving-week action plan

1. **Monday–Wednesday (if your city is open):** call the building division. Two questions: (a) on what date are impact fees assessed — submittal or issuance? (b) has the city published its 2027 fee schedule with the SB 1117 marginal treatment?
2. **Run the arithmetic** from our [SB 1117 explainer](/posts/sb-1117-explained-plain-english/) using your city's per-sq-ft impact-fee rate and your ADU's size. Compare the savings against your monthly carrying costs.
3. **If you wait:** use December productively. Finalize plans, get contractor bids, start the utility conversation early (the [SB 1196 backlog](/posts/sb-1196-utility-hookup-backlog/) is the other calendar killer). January-you will thank December-you.
4. **If you pull:** pull a *complete* submittal. The fastest permit is the one that does not come back.

## The bottom line

Thirty-six days. For most homeowners the answer is simple: **if your ADU is over 750 sq ft and your city has a real impact-fee cliff, January's marginal rates are worth the wait** — and the holiday slowdown may put you there whether you plan it or not. If your city charges no DIF, or your ADU is under 750 sq ft, the calendar is irrelevant: pull when ready.

Either way, do not let the decision be made by inertia. Run the numbers this week, call the building division, and pick your date on purpose.
