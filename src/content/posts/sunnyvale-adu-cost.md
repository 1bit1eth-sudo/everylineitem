Sunnyvale is the most computable city in this batch. Its FY 2026-27 Master Fee Schedule publishes both halves of the permit math — the valuation table and the permit table — so the permit stack can be derived rather than quoted, and the derivation is shown in full below. The result is a **$311,604** floor, the highest in this batch, and it still excludes the transportation impact fee, because the schedule never says what an ADU pays for it.

The permit math, step by step. Attachment B (Construction Valuation Data) prices new dwellings, Type V wood frame, at **$171 per square foot**. An 800 sq ft detached ADU therefore values at **$136,800**. Attachment A (Building Permit Fee Schedule) prices the $100,001–$500,000 valuation band at **$2,445 for the first $100,000 plus $13.50 per additional $1,000 or fraction thereof**: $36,800 of additional valuation is 37 units, so $2,445 + 37 × $13.50 = **$2,944.50**. Plan check is **70%** of the permit fee ($2,061.15) and energy plan check is **10%** ($294.45). Stack total: **$5,300.10**. That is the permitFee in this post — not a range, not a quote, a table computation.

The school fee is the state maximum, split two ways. Sunnyvale School District collects **$3.32** per square foot (61.8% of the $5.38 statutory rate under its sharing agreement with Fremont Union High School District), and Fremont Union collects **$2.06** per square foot in the Sunnyvale attendance area. Combined: **$5.38 × 800 = $4,304**. The caveat is procedural, not arithmetic: both 2026 rates were documented at the hearing-notice stage (Sunnyvale's March 26, 2026 hearing notice) and the proposed-schedule stage (Fremont Union's schedule, effective on or after May 9, 2026). The adoption resolutions were not sighted in this round, so the split is flagged for confirmation even though the two shares sum exactly to the statutory maximum.

## The ADU law box

Three state rules frame every number above. Under Government Code §65852.2(f)(3)(A), a local agency may not impose impact fees on an ADU under 750 square feet; at 750 or above, fees are charged proportionately to the primary dwelling's size. Under §66311.5 (SB 1117, effective January 1, 2027), per-square-foot impact fees on two or fewer ADUs are charged only on the area above 750 square feet — 50 square feet on this 800 sq ft benchmark — and connection and capacity charges are excluded from that rule. School fees are a separate statute: under SB 543, ADUs under 500 square feet pay $0 in school fees, and at 800 square feet the full district rate applies. SB 1117 does not reduce school fees. Note that Sunnyvale's transportation impact fee is charged per dwelling unit, not per square foot, so SB 1117's per-square-foot proration does not convert it — and since the ADU classification is unstated, no SB 1117 delta is quantified for Sunnyvale at all.

## Three things that can still move the number

1. **The transportation impact fee.** The schedule's two candidate rates are $5,095 (single-family detached) and $3,158 (multifamily attached) per unit. Whichever classification the city applies to an ADU — or whether it exempts ADUs — moves this total by thousands.
2. **The surcharge and fire stack.** The 5% technology surcharge and the 70% fire fee are both stated as percentages of the permit fee, but their ADU applicability and the surcharge's base are unstated. Together they could add roughly $2,300–$2,500.
3. **The school-fee adoption paperwork.** The $3.32 + $2.06 split sums exactly to the statutory maximum, but the adoption resolutions were not sighted. If either district's 2026 rate differs from its published proposal, the $4,304 moves.
