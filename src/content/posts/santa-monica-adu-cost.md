Santa Monica exempts ADUs of **every size** from **all four** of its city impact-fee programs — the first city in this series to waive the entire development impact fee stack by local ordinance, with no 750 sq ft cliff anywhere in it. On paper, that is roughly $21,800 per unit the city declines to collect. In practice, the coast is still not cheap: our six-phase total lands at **$305,000, or $381 per square foot** — and that number carries the largest asterisk of any coastal post so far, because it is a floor, not the answer.

Two things sit above the floor. The building permit's current rates are unpublished — the city prices permits per square foot and adjusts them annually by CPI, but the latest published table is from 2019, so we could not compute an 800 sq ft figure. And the city's own preapproved-ADU page warns that units over 750 square feet "may be subject to Public Works impact fees" — a de facto 750 sq ft threshold for water and wastewater capital facility fees whose amounts we could not verify from any current official source. What we *can* count is the line that dominates Santa Monica's soft costs: the school fee at **$3,368**, below the state maximum and the single largest fee on the ledger. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Santa Monica does not publish phase-level construction costs. HCD logged 396 ADU permits here in 2022–2024, 38th statewide.

## $0: the boldest DIF exemption in the series

Read Santa Monica Municipal Code Section 9.31.025, subsection O, and the impact-fee conversation ends there: "ADUs and JADUs established pursuant to this Section shall be exempt from the impact fees specified in Chapter 9.64, Affordable Housing Production Program, Chapter 9.65, Child care Linkage Program, Chapter 9.66, Transportation Impact Fee Program, and Chapter 9.67, Parks and Recreation Development Impact Fee Program." No size threshold. No proportionate math. No cliff. Every Batch 1–3 city runs its exemption through the state's 750 sq ft threshold; Santa Monica is the first to erase the threshold by ordinance. The FY 2026-27 Development Impact Fee handout shows what that erasure costs the city: the transportation impact fee alone runs **$11,097–$11,389 per single-family unit**, and the parks and recreation fee is **$10,702.89 per unit** — among the highest per-unit residential impact fees anywhere in our matrix. Roughly $21,800 per unit, waived by one subsection. No other city in the series has forgone more per ADU.

## The permit you can't price from the schedule

Most cities in this series price building permits on construction valuation — Santa Monica prices them per square foot. The only published rate table we found, Exhibit B (Building & Safety), is built on 7/1/19 base fees: one- and two-family dwellings at $1.7863 per square foot, residential remodels and additions including accessory buildings at $1.8410 per square foot, a $1,160.44 minimum plan review fee, and plan check not-otherwise-classified at $267.08 per hour. At those 2019 rates, an 800 sq ft ADU's permit math would start around $1,473 — but 2019 is not 2026, and the city adjusts these fees annually by CPI. The current per-square-foot rates are not published on any official source we could find. So the pricing *model* is confirmed (per square foot, not valuation) and the price is not — the exact inverse of Oxnard's fully published schedule that hides its valuation method. The permit line is excluded from the total and marked "verify with city."

## $3,368: the school fee with an adoption gap

With the city's four impact-fee programs at zero, the school fee is the largest fee on a Santa Monica ADU by default — and it is not a small line. At **$4.21 per square foot**, Santa Monica-Malibu Unified's rate is below the 2026 state maximum of $5.38 (the district did not seek the max), totaling **$3,368** on 800 sq ft. The asterisk is about process, not math: $4.21 is the rate the district's June 8, 2023 "Development School Fee Justification Study" proposed, and the district noticed a July 20, 2023 public hearing for the Board to consider the updating resolution. We searched for the adoption minutes and could not find them on any official district source. So $3,368 is proposed-and-noticed by the district — official material — but not board-confirmed. If the Board adopted a different number, the true figure differs. It is the honest ceiling of what we can say, and it is the single largest fee we can count in Santa Monica.

## The Public Works caveat at 750 sq ft

Here is the one place a 750 sq ft threshold appears in Santa Monica's materials: the Preapproved ADU Program page warns that "ADUs larger than 750 square feet may be subject to Public Works impact fees." The water capital facility fee (SMMC Ch. 7.12) and the wastewater capital facility fee (SMMC Ch. 7.04) both exist as code authority — "the owner or developer of a building in the City shall pay" — with amounts set by council resolution. We searched for those amounts and could not find a current resolution or fee schedule listing them. Our 800 sq ft example sits above the warning's threshold, so some Public Works charge may apply — the only cliff-adjacent line in this post, and the only one whose dollar impact is entirely unknown. It is excluded and on the verify list.

Both totals are floors, and the comparison is really a comparison of what each floor excludes: Santa Monica leaves out the permit and the Public Works fees; Oxnard leaves out the permit and the proportional DIF. The structural difference is the cliff: Oxnard keeps the state threshold in ordinance (Day 34's control case), while Santa Monica abolished it for impact fees — and replaced it, quietly, with a Public Works warning whose price tag nobody can find.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Santa Monica has nothing to recalculate — its ADU impact fees are already $0 at every size, and the SB 1117 story here is that the state is catching up to what the city's ordinance did years ago. The open question is the Public Works side: if the water and wastewater capital facility fees do apply to the >750 square feet of an 800 sq ft ADU, SB 1117's marginal math would govern how they're calculated. We found no mention of SB 1117 in city materials; the January 2027 quarterly review will check whether the city has published current Public Works fee amounts. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and stay at $3,368 pending adoption confirmation.
