Santa Monica exempts ADUs of **every size** from **all four** of its city impact-fee programs — the first city in this series to waive the entire development impact fee stack by local ordinance, with no 750 sq ft cliff anywhere in it. On paper, that is roughly $21,800 per unit the city declines to collect. In practice, the coast is still not cheap: our six-phase total lands at **$309,000, or $386 per square foot** — and that number still carries an asterisk, because it is a floor, not the answer.

One thing sits above the floor. The building permit is now priced — $3,669 from the FY 2026-27 schedule (see below) — but the city's own preapproved-ADU page warns that units over 750 square feet "may be subject to Public Works impact fees" — a de facto 750 sq ft threshold for water and wastewater capital facility fees whose amounts we could not verify from any current official source. What we *can* count is now two lines: the building permit at **$3,669** and the school fee at **$3,368**, below the state maximum — the two largest fee lines on the ledger. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Santa Monica does not publish phase-level construction costs. HCD logged 396 ADU permits here in 2022–2024, 38th statewide.

## $0: the boldest DIF exemption in the series

Read Santa Monica Municipal Code Section 9.31.025, subsection O, and the impact-fee conversation ends there: "ADUs and JADUs established pursuant to this Section shall be exempt from the impact fees specified in Chapter 9.64, Affordable Housing Production Program, Chapter 9.65, Child care Linkage Program, Chapter 9.66, Transportation Impact Fee Program, and Chapter 9.67, Parks and Recreation Development Impact Fee Program." No size threshold. No proportionate math. No cliff. Every Batch 1–3 city runs its exemption through the state's 750 sq ft threshold; Santa Monica is the first to erase the threshold by ordinance. The FY 2026-27 Development Impact Fee handout shows what that erasure costs the city: the transportation impact fee alone runs **$11,097–$11,389 per single-family unit**, and the parks and recreation fee is **$10,702.89 per unit** — among the highest per-unit residential impact fees anywhere in our matrix. Roughly $21,800 per unit, waived by one subsection. No other city in the series has forgone more per ADU.

## The permit, priced at $3,669

Most cities in this series price building permits on construction valuation — Santa Monica prices them per square foot. The city's FY 2026-27 Fee Schedule, Exhibit B (Building & Safety), effective July 1, 2026, now publishes the current rates: formal plan review (704.03, one- and two-family dwellings including accessory buildings) at $777.20 plus $1.73 per square foot, and the combination building permit (704.37) at $163.80 plus $1.68 per square foot. On 800 sq ft that is **$2,161.20 + $1,507.80 = $3,669**. The schedule's $1,486.25 minimum plan review fee does not bind at this size. This supersedes the 7/1/19 table this post previously cited ($1.8410/sq ft, $1,160.44 minimum) — the pricing model was always per square foot; now the price is published too. The permit line is counted in the total.

## $3,368: the school fee with an adoption gap

With the city's four impact-fee programs at zero, the school fee is the second-largest fee on a Santa Monica ADU — behind only the $3,669 building permit — and it is not a small line. At **$4.21 per square foot**, Santa Monica-Malibu Unified's rate is below the 2026 state maximum of $5.38 (the district did not seek the max), totaling **$3,368** on 800 sq ft. The asterisk is about process, not math: $4.21 is the rate the district's June 8, 2023 "Development School Fee Justification Study" proposed, and the district noticed a July 20, 2023 public hearing for the Board to consider the updating resolution. We searched for the adoption minutes and could not find them on any official district source. So $3,368 is proposed-and-noticed by the district — official material — but not board-confirmed. If the Board adopted a different number, the true figure differs. It is the honest ceiling of what we can say, and it is the single largest fee we can count in Santa Monica.

## The Public Works caveat at 750 sq ft

Here is the one place a 750 sq ft threshold appears in Santa Monica's materials: the Preapproved ADU Program page warns that "ADUs larger than 750 square feet may be subject to Public Works impact fees." The water capital facility fee (SMMC Ch. 7.12) and the wastewater capital facility fee (SMMC Ch. 7.04) both exist as code authority — "the owner or developer of a building in the City shall pay" — with amounts set by council resolution. We searched for those amounts and could not find a current resolution or fee schedule listing them. Our 800 sq ft example sits above the warning's threshold, so some Public Works charge may apply — the only cliff-adjacent line in this post, and the only one whose dollar impact is entirely unknown. It is excluded and on the verify list.

Both totals are floors, and the comparison is really a comparison of what each floor excludes: Santa Monica now leaves out only the Public Works fees above 750 sq ft; Oxnard still leaves out the permit and the proportional DIF. The structural difference is the cliff: Oxnard keeps the state threshold in ordinance (Day 34's control case), while Santa Monica abolished it for impact fees — and replaced it, quietly, with a Public Works warning whose price tag nobody can find.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Santa Monica has nothing to recalculate — its ADU impact fees are already $0 at every size, and the SB 1117 story here is that the state is catching up to what the city's ordinance did years ago. The open question is the Public Works side: if the water and wastewater capital facility fees do apply to the >750 square feet of an 800 sq ft ADU, SB 1117's marginal math would govern how they're calculated. We found no mention of SB 1117 in city materials; the January 2027 quarterly review will check whether the city has published current Public Works fee amounts. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and stay at $3,368 pending adoption confirmation.
