Before a single nail goes in, the ADU has a price. Permits, plan check, school fees, impact fees, utility capacity charges — the paper cost. We have ranked [building permits alone](/posts/adu-permit-fees-ranked/) and [school fees alone](/posts/adu-school-fees-ranked-california/). This is the combined leaderboard: every paper-cost line for the same 800 sq ft detached ADU, construction excluded.

The definition: permit + plan check + school + impact/DIF + water/sewer. The $14,000 design line carried in every city post is subtracted — it is a series-wide assumption, not a city difference. Floors and ranges are marked where the source post marks them: a floor means real paper costs are at least this high.

## The ranking

**1. Lancaster — ~$3,000.** The series' only outright DIF waiver. No impact-fee cliff, no proportional math — the single largest paper-cost line most cities carry simply does not exist here. The catch: the school figure covers only one of two districts, so this is slightly understated.

**2. Santa Maria — ~$4,000 (floor\*).** Design subtracted from the published floor — and the floor excludes the building permit, plan check, DIF, and water/sewer entirely, all unverified. This number will only go up.

**3. Westminster — ~$4,802 (floor\*).** Floor again: the building permit and the school fee are both "verify with city" and excluded. Real paper costs are higher by an unknown margin.

**4. Pasadena — ~$6,000–$12,000\*.** A band, not a point: the building permit is a city-published $7,000–$13,000 range (valuation-based, project-specific), and sewer and water charges are unverified. The school fee ($1,792, the series low) is the anchor; everything else is a range.

**5–7. Chula Vista, Oxnard, Santa Ana — ~$6,000 each.** The efficient middle: flat or proportional fee structures with no cliff drama. Chula Vista's flat $3,951 ADU permit is the series standout for pricing transparency.

**8. Riverside — ~$7,000.** The $1,006 permit (series low) and free 800 sq ft pre-approved plan keep paper costs down. One of the cheapest cities to permit, full stop.

**9. Campbell — ~$8,649 (floor\*).** Before sewer and DIF — both carry asterisks (West Valley Sanitation's per-fixture-unit math runs up to ~$7,750; impact-fee rates are unpublished). The series-high school fee ($4,320) is doing most of the lifting here.

**10. Downey — ~$9,000.**

**11–12. Long Beach, Oceanside — ~$11,000 each.**

**13. Alhambra — ~$13,501.** The $0 DIF is doing real work — offset by the steepest water-meter step-up in the series ($448 vs. $7,222, ranked on the no-new-meter assumption).

**14–16. Sacramento, Salinas, San Bernardino — ~$14,000 each.** The middle of the pack, to the dollar.

**17. Torrance — ~$15,000.** The $2,243.47 flat DIF is the series' cheapest impact-fee line — and its SB 1117 treatment is the series' biggest open question, since a flat fee has no per-foot rate to marginalize.

**18–20. Fullerton, Garden Grove, Glendale — ~$16,000 each.** Three different philosophies, one paper cost: Fullerton's cheap permit ($1,223) against Garden Grove's dual cliffs and Glendale's $4,700 50-sq-ft step.

**21. Fontana — ~$17,000.**

**22. Palo Alto — ~$17,569\*.** Sewer, water, and plan check all unverified. The proportional DIF math (~$7,947 on an assumed 2,000 sq ft house) is the series' most assumption-sensitive paper line — and the one SB 1117 will reshape most dramatically (~$7,500–$18,750 in estimated savings).

**23–24. Anaheim, Burbank — ~$18,000 each.** Anaheim's ~$10,411 DIF (series high) and Burbank's $3,692 permit-plus-plan-check (series high at the time) arrive at the same paper cost from opposite directions.

**25. Oakland — ~$20,000.** A $12,800–$19,200 valuation-based permit carrying what other cities spread across impact-fee lines — against $0 in ADU impact fees. The permit is the paper cost.

**26. Ontario — ~$26,003.** The top of the leaderboard by a clear margin: roughly nine times Lancaster's paper cost. Impact-fee lines other cities waive, reduce, or never had.

**Unranked — Lakewood.** No soft-cost summary was published (county-run permits; the permit line itself is the softest number in the series), so it cannot be ranked honestly. Verify with the county before budgeting.

## The uncomfortable finding

Paper cost and construction cost do not move together. A city can be cheap to build in and expensive to permit — Ontario's $26,003 paper cost sits inside a total that is unremarkable, and Oakland's $20,000 paper cost is almost entirely one permit line. Conversely, Lancaster's $3,000 paper cost is not a construction bargain; it is a policy choice — waived impact fees.

That is the point of this leaderboard: **the build is priced by the market, but the paper is priced by the city.** Two ADUs with identical lumber can be $23,000 apart before either one breaks ground — and the difference is entirely a stack of PDFs.

Two caveats before you budget from this table. First, floors are floors: Santa Maria, Westminster, and Campbell are ranked on confirmed lines only, and their true paper costs are higher. Second, the January 1, 2027 clock: SB 1117 compresses the impact-fee component of every cliff city on this list. Re-rank this table in February and the middle will look different — which is exactly why the [December re-verification](/posts/december-2026-fee-reverification/) exists.
