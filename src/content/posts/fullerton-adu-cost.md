Fullerton has the cheapest building permit in this series — and one of the most expensive park fees. That tension is the whole story.

The FY 2025–2026 fee schedule prices a $250,000-valuation building permit at **$1,223**, about 39% of Garden Grove's $3,137 for the same valuation. But the permit is only the cover charge: plan review runs **87%** of the permit fee, energy plan check another **20%**, and the Park Dwelling Fee — **$12,020 per unit** — is the single largest impact-fee line we have seen in any city so far. Our six-phase total lands at **$318,000, or $398 per square foot** — the same headline number as Garden Grove, built from completely different parts.

Three assumptions sit underneath that total, each marked where it appears: the valuation behind the building permit, the ADU's share of park impact fees, and whether the regional sewer charge applies to an ADU the ordinance says is "not a new residential use." We have not yet contacted the city about these items; this post will be updated when we do. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Fullerton does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## The cheapest permit in the series

Fullerton's building-permit tiers are strikingly low. At a $250,000 valuation the permit is $623 for the first $100,000 plus $4.00 per additional $1,000 — **$1,223 total**. Garden Grove charges $3,137 for the same valuation. But Fullerton stacks percentage-based reviews on top: 87% plan review ($1,064), 20% energy check ($245), 5% NPDES ($61). The permit-plus-reviews bundle is ~$2,593 — still the lowest in the series, just less dramatically so.

One more wrinkle: the fee schedule lists "Residential Addition $88.00/sq ft" as a valuation shortcut, and the ordinance says valuation follows the ICC Building Valuation Data. Which one the city actually applies to a detached ADU is unconfirmed — at $88/sq ft, an 800 sq ft ADU values at just $70,400, which would cut the permit roughly in half.

## The 750 cliff

Ordinance No. 3343 (June 2025) draws the familiar line: no impact fees under 750 sq ft, proportional fees at 750 and above. The ordinance cites Government Code §66324 only for the *definition* of "impact fee" — the ADU rules themselves live in §66311.5, which the ordinance follows.

The cliff matters more here than in most cities because the Park Dwelling Fee is so large: $12,020 per unit. Our pro-rata example (800 sq ft ADU on a 1,500 sq ft house) puts the ADU's share at ~$6,411 — a 749 sq ft design would pay $0. And the city's own in-development pre-approved plan at **842 sq ft** sails right past the cliff. If the city ever publishes those plans, the 440 sq ft one will be the fee-optimized pick.

## Pre-approved plans: coming soon, maybe

Fullerton's planning department announced three pre-approved ADU designs (440, 842, and 1,000 sq ft) with RRM Design Group, targeting a spring 2026 public release. As of October 2026, the city's ADU page shows no trace of them. The ordinance sweetens the deal for whenever they arrive: using a pre-approved plan (or a plan already approved in the same code cycle) cuts the city's review clock from 60 days to **30 days**.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Fullerton's current pro-rata-on-the-primary-dwelling structure will need a rewrite. We found no mention of SB 1117 in city materials; the January 2027 quarterly review will check for an updated ordinance.
