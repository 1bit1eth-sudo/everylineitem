Santa Ana's official answer to "how much does a permit cost" is a phone number. The city's FAQ page, under the heading "How Much Does a Permit Cost?", does not give a number — it gives the Permit Counter at **(714) 647-5800**. No valuation table, no per-square-foot schedule, no worked example. It is the most explicit fee opacity in the series: not a missing document, but a published refusal to publish.

What the city does publish is substantial. A three-tier ADU planning review ($1,865 / $571 / $504). A per-square-foot DIF structure that would make an 800 sq ft ADU expensive if it applied as printed. A **$4,304** school fee at the state ceiling. And a pre-approved plan program that has been running since 2021. Santa Ana hides the permit and shows everything else — which makes this post the series' study in selective transparency.

Our six-phase total lands at **$308,000, or $385 per square foot** — a floor, not a total, marked with the asterisk where it appears. The building permit is excluded entirely (no published table exists), the DIF amount is excluded (no ADU formula is published), and sewer/water capacity charges are excluded (the OC San ADU rate interpretation needs the district's confirmation). Counted: design, the **$1,865** standard ADU review, and the **$4,304** school fee. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Santa Ana does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## "Call the Permit Counter"

There is a difference between a fee table that is hard to find and a fee table that does not exist, and Santa Ana makes the difference explicit. The FAQ page's "How Much Does a Permit Cost?" entry is the series' only published shrug: call (714) 647-5800. Lancaster's permit table is missing from an index; Santa Ana's was never put online. The practical consequence is identical — no 800 sq ft example can be computed, and the permit line is a complete blank in the fee autopsy — but the posture is different. Lancaster looks disorganized; Santa Ana looks decided.

The planning side is the counterweight. The Planning Division's FY 2025-2026 schedule publishes three ADU review tiers with unusual granularity: **$1,865** for the standard review, **$571** over the counter, and **$504** for pre-approved plans. That is more published review structure than most cities in the series offer, sitting one page away from a permit FAQ that publishes nothing. The $1,865 standard review is counted in our total; the permit behind it is not.

## $4,304: the series-maximum school fee

If the permit line is the post's biggest blank, the school fee is its biggest certainty. SAUSD charges **$5.38 per square foot** — the state ceiling — on residential new construction and explicitly on ADUs of 500 square feet and above, from the district's official Facilities page and its 2026 justification study under Ed. Code §17620. On 800 sq ft: **$4,304**, tying Long Beach, Glendale, Anaheim, and Burbank for the highest school fee in the series. No adoption-date ambiguity (unlike Downey's 2024 citation), no missing half (unlike Chula Vista's elementary gap or Lancaster's high-school hole). It is the single largest confirmed fee line in this post, and the one number in the fee autopsy with no asterisk on its source.

## A per-square-foot DIF machine

Santa Ana's DIF structure is the outlier of the series. Where other cities charge flat per-unit fees or waive ADU DIF entirely, Santa Ana's FY 2025-26 schedule prices transportation at **$1.80 per square foot** and parks at **$1.81–$5.53 per square foot** by zone. If those tables applied to an 800 sq ft ADU as printed, Zone C alone would be $4,424 in park fees plus $1,440 in transportation — a DIF bill in the thousands, and the largest 750-cliff penalty in the series. But the tables are labeled "for projects with 5 or more dwelling units," and no document says whether a single ADU on a single-family lot is inside that label. The city publishes no ADU-specific DIF formula at all — the state proportional rule is the only mechanics on record. So the DIF machine exists, its ADU switch is unlabeled, and the whole line is excluded pending the city's word.

## Two sewer bills

Santa Ana homeowners can get two separate sewer charges, and neither is clean. The City of Santa Ana charges **$53.00 per fixture unit** for sewer connection — an 800 sq ft fixture set works out to about $689 at 13 fixture units — but the fixture-count math is our example, and the city states no ADU application rule. Separately, the Orange County Sanitation District (OC San, which serves Santa Ana) has an ADU-specific rate in ordinance OC SAN-59: ADUs pay the **average demand rate of $1,710 per 1,000 sq ft** (2026-27, effective July 1, 2026), with the $6,588 minimum charge explicitly not applying to ADUs. The interpretation question is whether 800 sq ft pays the full $1,710 ("per 1,000 sq ft or portion thereof" reads as rounding up) or a prorated $1,368 — our reading is $1,710, but OC San should confirm. Both lines stay excluded until the agencies confirm the ADU application.

## The mature plan program

Santa Ana's pre-approved ADU program predates the current ADU boom cycle: RFP 21-001 in 2021, a $60,000 contract with Design Path Studio approved July 6, 2021, producing studio-through-3-bedroom plans in Ranch, Spanish, Traditional, Contemporary, and Craftsman styles — downloadable from the city's website with a dedicated submittal checklist. The financial hook is the planning review: **$504** with a pre-approved plan versus **$1,865** standard, a $1,361 savings before the building permit (whatever it is) even enters the picture. It is the series' most developed answer to the permit-opacity problem: the city cannot tell you the permit price, but it will sell you a plan that makes the review around it cheap and fast.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Santa Ana is where that matters most in the series. If the per-square-foot DIF tables (transportation $1.80, parks up to $5.53) apply to ADUs as printed, the current 750 cliff creates the series' largest penalty at 800 sq ft — and SB 1117's marginal math would collapse the fee base from 800 sq ft to 50. The catch is the same as the rest of this post: until the city publishes how the tables apply to ADUs, neither the current bill nor the SB 1117 revision can be computed. We found no mention of SB 1117 in city materials; the January 2027 quarterly review will check for a revised impact-fee schedule. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and stay at $4,304.
