> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Lynwood costs at least **$303,929, or about $380 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and the most interesting line in that total is the one that gets smaller when the main house gets bigger. Anyone searching for Lynwood ADU fees will find a city whose impact-fee bundle is fully itemized and whose ADU summary states the state rules in plain language: no impact fee under 750 square feet, proportional fees at 750 and above, and no connection fee or capacity charge for a standard ADU. What they will not find is a building permit they can price, a plan check base rate, a school-fee rate, or a clear answer on the art fee. This post counts what the city documents price, prorates it at a stated primary-home size, and leaves the rest as named blanks.

Lynwood is the batch's study in a small fee with a long shadow. The counted $1,929 is the smallest impact fee in this batch, yet the uncounted items around it — permit, plan check, school fee, art fee, and two further bundle rows under review — could each exceed it. A floor built on one small verified fee is still a floor, but readers should understand how much of the eventual budget is still unpriced, and this post says so in the table rather than in a footnote.

The soft-costs phase is $14,000 of design from the series baseline plus the $1,929 prorated impact fee, with the $0 standard-case connection treatment adding nothing. Everything else that usually lives in that phase — the building permit, plan check, school fee, and art fee — is excluded, not estimated. A floor that quietly absorbed guesses would stop being a floor.

One scope note, because readers often ask for the companion figure: this post does not publish a verified total for a new 2,000 sq ft single-family home in Lynwood. The fee schedule prices permits by band and impact fees by bundle but never prices construction, so no honest total can be derived from it. The 2,000 sq ft figure appears here only as the denominator that makes the example legible — an 800 sq ft ADU is 40% of a 2,000 sq ft primary home, which is the proportion the state's impact-fee rule uses once the 750 sq ft cliff is crossed.

Two details in that table deserve emphasis. First, the bundle's two largest components — parks and recreation at $2,270.10 and water facilities at $1,381.25 — together make up three-quarters of the stack, so Lynwood's impact fee is mostly a parks-and-water story, not a traffic or police story. Second, the $1,297 construction-permit band is the post's most tempting trap: it looks countable, and for many valuations it probably would be the band, but "probably the band" is not a verified fee, and the valuation method the city actually uses was not in the retrieved text. Counting it would have added a plausible number and removed a visible blank; leaving it out keeps the blank visible, which is the point.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. The city's ADU summary states the same rule in its own words, and our 800 sq ft example therefore crosses the cliff in principle, with the $1,929 above as the prorated result. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) changes the treatment for lots with two or fewer ADUs: only the area in excess of 750 square feet is charged, so an 800 sq ft unit beside a 2,000 sq ft primary home would be charged on 50 sq ft — about $121 on this bundle. That future rule is not in this post's 2026 total. Connection and capacity charges are a separate category from impact fees, which is why the $0 connection treatment sits in its own row. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The permit and plan check, together.** The construction-permit band and the plan check base rate are the largest unpriced city items, and they arrive together at permit issuance. A third-party write-up puts Lynwood plan check near $3,240, but that is not the city's schedule and is not counted here. When the city confirms the valuation basis and the plan check rate, both lines join the floor by addition — and they may jointly exceed the counted impact fee several times over.

**2. The art fee and the two extra bundle rows.** The ADU form's 1.5% art charge, if it applied to a detached ADU, would be calculated on project cost over $100,000 — a large base, and therefore a potentially large fee. Separately, if the two single-family rows under review apply to ADUs, the bundle rises to $5,159.33 and the prorated fee to $2,064, adding $135 to this floor. Both questions are cheap to ask the city and expensive to discover at permit issuance.

**3. The school fee and the connection exception.** The school fee needs a district rate that was not located, and [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) covers why a statewide cap cannot stand in for it. Separately, the $0 connection treatment holds only for the standard case: an ADU built with a new single-family dwelling can face a proportionate charge and a new or separate connection, so confirm the connection route before relying on $0.

## Lynwood vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lynwood (verified floor) | **$303,929** | $1,929 prorated impact fee + $0 standard-case connection; permit, plan check, school fee, and art fee excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Lynwood sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: Lynwood's figure is missing its permit bill, its plan check, and its school fee, while the LA County figures include theirs. Once those blanks fill in, expect the gap to narrow. What Lynwood genuinely leads on is the connection rule stated plainly in its own summary — a $0 standard case with a named exception beats a schedule that leaves the question unasked.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $303,929 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, re-price the impact fee at the actual primary-home size, and carry separate, explicit allowances for the permit and plan check, the school fee, the art fee, and the two bundle rows under review. When the city and district answer those questions, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision. That is the point of keeping guesses out of the total in the first place.
