In most California cities, the school fee is the boring line: $5.38 a square foot, the state maximum, take it or leave it. Folsom is where that line eats the budget.

Folsom Cordova Unified doesn't charge the Level I rate at all. It charges **Level II** — a rate built from its own facilities needs analysis, split by improvement district. In SFID 2, which the district defines as the City of Folsom, the residential rate is **$10.26 per square foot**, effective April 16, 2026. On an 800 sq ft ADU that is **$8,208** — the highest school fee we have verified in our 134-jurisdiction research matrix, roughly double what the same unit pays in a $5.38-max district ($4,304), and more than many cities' *entire* government fee stack.

## A school fee with no state ceiling

Level I developer fees are capped by the State Allocation Board — $5.38/sq ft for 2026, the number behind most rows in our [school-fee ranking](/posts/adu-school-fees-ranked-california/). Level II is a different statute: a district that meets the eligibility conditions may charge what its School Facilities Needs Analysis justifies. FCUSD's board approved its 2026 justification study and needs analysis on April 16, 2026, and its official fee page lists the result — SFID 2 (Folsom) at $10.26, SFID 1 (Rancho Cordova) at $9.01, commercial at $0.87.

Two consequences follow. First, the [750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/) doesn't protect you here: school fees answer to the state's **500 sq ft** assessable-space threshold. Shrink the ADU to 749 sq ft to dodge the city's impact fees and the school bill is still **$7,684.74** (749 × $10.26). Only at 500 sq ft or less does it reach $0. Second, no pending law trims it: SB 1117 covers city impact fees, not school developer fees, so the $10.26 rate survives January 1, 2027 untouched (see below).

Collection is the standard California ritual: get the Certificate of Compliance form from the building department, pay the district, bring the certified form back before the permit issues.

## The city's stack — heavy, but proportional

Folsom's own impact fees are per-unit, not per-square-foot (Resolution No. 11601, effective July 1, 2026). For a new single-family house the stack is **$26,193** — Transportation ($9,903) and Park Construction ($8,532) alone are 70% of it. But an ADU never pays full freight. Under FMC §17.105.140(L):

- **Under 750 sq ft:** $0 in city-imposed impact fees.
- **750 sq ft or more:** the stack is charged *in proportion to the primary dwelling* — stack × (ADU sq ft ÷ main-house sq ft).

So the honest dollar figure depends on your house. On a 2,000 sq ft primary, an 800 sq ft ADU's ratio is 0.40 and the bill is **$10,477.20** (illustrative arithmetic on confirmed rates — the city publishes no worked ADU example, which is why this row is labeled the way it is). On a 1,500 sq ft primary the same ADU would owe about $13,969. One boundary flag: the $1,194 Water Impact Fee sits inside the schedule but may be a capacity charge rather than a covered impact fee; the city hasn't published which way it treats the line for ADUs. Excluding it, the stack is $24,999.

## The cleanest permit in the matrix

After the school fee, the building permit is a relief: **$4,709 flat for a detached or attached ADU** (Junior ADUs: $2,260), plan review and inspections included, no valuation math (Resolution No. 11481, effective January 1, 2026). Riding on it: a $79 processing fee, a 5% General Plan/Zoning update charge whose application to the flat ADU fee the city hasn't spelled out (it would be $235.45 if applied), and fixed lines of $25 + $3.60 + $0.40, plus valuation-based state surcharges that can't be dollarized in advance. Notably absent: the 5% technology fee the 2024 schedule carried is gone.

A design note: Folsom **cannot impose design standards on ADUs of 800 sq ft or less and 16 ft or less**, so our spec unit is design-review exempt — $0 planning-side. Taller or larger units face director-level review ($400–$864 on the published schedule).

## Utilities: two providers, no published ADU total

Water is the city's own (its own treatment plant): connection **$4,075/unit**, plus the $1,194 impact line discussed above. Sewer splits — the city collects, **SacSewer** treats — with a city connection rate of **$1,301/unit** and a separate SacSewer regional treatment impact fee behind it (a per-ESD charge; the figure in a neighboring city's official finance plan is $6,479 per ESD, but the Folsom tier, current rate, and ADU application are unconfirmed — we are not publishing it as a Folsom ADU number).

What the city does *not* publish is what an ADU actually pays these lines: connection and capacity charges sit outside the ADU impact-fee rule, state law requires them to be proportionate to the ADU's burden, and no official Folsom document we found prices the spec case. Rates confirmed; ADU totals open — all of it is on our December re-verification list.

## Pre-approved plans, marketplace style

Folsom's ADU Master Plan Program is the anti-Chico: instead of a free city plan library, designers submit plans for city pre-review, the approved plans are listed on the city website, and **homeowners buy the plan from the designer**. The streamlined review is real; the plan is not free, and the $4,709 permit doesn't change. Whether any listed plan is sized at 749 sq ft — which would zero the city impact fees by size alone — is unverified.

[SB 1117](/posts/sb-1117-kills-750-sq-ft-cliff/) guts Folsom's city DIF and leaves the total's center of gravity exactly where it already was: the school district. Post-2027, the school line alone exceeds the city's covered impact fees by two orders of magnitude. If you take one number from this post into a Folsom budget meeting, take $10.26 — and ask who at the district office can show you the needs analysis behind it.
