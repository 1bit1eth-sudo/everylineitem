> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

For a homeowner pricing an 800 sq ft detached ADU in Davis, the verified floor is **$306,304, or about $383 per square foot**, and the shape of that number tells the story before any table does: a reader working through Davis ADU fees will find exactly one charge that can be priced for this unit from official documents, a district school fee, with the city's own permit and impact lines left unpriced because the documents that set them do not contain an 800 sq ft answer. Nothing else has been added to make the total look more complete, because a floor padded with estimates stops being a floor.

## The 500 sq ft school-fee threshold comes first

Before the permit, before the phases, the threshold decides whether the one counted fee exists at all. Davis Joint Unified School District states its residential developer rate as $5.38 per square foot and states that it does not charge on square footage of 500 square feet or less, with a fee-exemption application filed alongside the certificate of compliance form. The district's 2026 fee justification study, presented at its May 21, 2026 board meeting, supports collecting the maximum residential rate: it finds a facilities cost impact of $12.04 per square foot, above the $5.38 statutory maximum set by the State Allocation Board on January 28, 2026, and concludes the district is justified in charging up to that maximum. The arithmetic for this post is then one line:

| Davis school fee, 800 sq ft | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| DJUSD residential rate × conditioned space: 800 sq ft × $5.38 | **$4,304** |
| Unit of 500 sq ft or less, per the district page | $0 (exemption application required) |
| **Counted fees, total** | **$4,304** |

Three details keep that line honest. First, the fee runs on conditioned space as determined by the city Building Inspection Division, and garage and porch space is exempt, so the 800 sq ft in this post means 800 sq ft of conditioned space, not 800 sq ft of structure including a garage. Second, the district page covers permits issued within Davis city limits through its Business Services office, and an applicant pays with the school district fee form plus a cashier's check or money order — a procedure worth confirming for a specific parcel before budgeting the payment date. Third, the boundary itself has a wording wrinkle at exactly 500 square feet: the district page exempts square footage of 500 or less, while the statewide statute phrases the exemption as units containing less than 500 square feet of interior livable space. At 800 square feet both wordings point the same way, the fee applies, so the total is unaffected — but a homeowner planning a unit at exactly 500 square feet should get the district's answer in writing rather than relying on either paraphrase, and this post flags that as a confirmation request rather than resolving it.

> **The ADU fee box, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. An 800 sq ft Davis unit is therefore above the cliff, not below it — but because the proportional charge needs the primary dwelling's size and the city's current rates, this post counts $0 of city impact fee for now and marks the line for verification rather than guessing it. SB 1117, codified at Government Code section 66311.5 and effective January 1, 2027, narrows that proportional calculation for up to two ADUs on a lot to only the square footage above 750, but it is a future rule only and is not reflected in this 2026 total. School fees are a separate regime under Education Code section 17620, levied by the district rather than the city, and SB 1117 does not change them. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The permit valuation.** This is the largest uncounted line, and it cannot be resolved from the schedule alone: the valuation comes from the Building Safety Magazine figure the schedule points to, and the plan check follows as 65 percent of the resulting basic fee. The city's own FAQ answer on permit cost is that costs vary and applicants should contact the division. Until a valuation is set for a specific project, any permit total attached to this post would be a guess dressed as a line item.

**2. The proportional city impact charge.** An 800 sq ft unit crosses the 750 sq ft threshold, so the question is not whether the proportional rule applies but what it produces for a particular lot: the primary dwelling's square footage and the city's current impact rates for an ADU. Neither was verified in this round, and Davis's published fee schedule contains no ADU impact line to compute from. [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) covers why a district rate and a city impact charge are different regimes — and in Davis, why only the district rate can be stated today.

**3. The two small confirmations before payment.** The district's exact-500 wording differs from the statute's less-than-500 wording, and the schedule's 3 percent school impact fee admin charge does not say whether an ADU applicant pays it on top of the district fee. Both are small, both should be settled with the district and the city before money moves, and neither changes the counted $4,304 unless the district answers otherwise — in which case the floor is corrected by addition or subtraction, not quietly rewritten.

## Davis vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Davis (verified floor) | **$306,304** | $4,304 school fee only; permit, city impact fees, and utilities excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Torrance (LA County) | $317,000 | Flat impact-fee structure in the LA County set |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Davis sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: the Davis figure is missing its entire permit bill and its proportional impact charge, while the LA County figures include theirs. The honest reading is not that Davis is cheap; it is that Davis's countable fee is one line, and its permit and impact lines are the open questions. A Davis homeowner who wants a budget rather than a floor should start by getting a valuation from the Building Division, the primary-dwelling proportion from the city, and the district's payment confirmation — then add those lines to $306,304.

## What this post does not claim

No building permit, plan check, or master/stock plan figure is claimed, because the schedule prices permits by valuation and does not print the valuation figure. No city impact fee is claimed, because the proportional charge needs a primary dwelling size and current rates that were not verified. No utility figure is claimed, because no ADU-specific sewer or water figure was located. No pre-approved plan saving is claimed, because the free city plans top out at 740 sq ft and this unit is 800 sq ft. No district resolution number is claimed, because the rate is confirmed from the district's live page and its 2026 fee justification study, not from a separately retrieved resolution. Readers who want the background on plan programs generally should read [California's free ADU plans](/posts/free-adu-plans-california/) and then confirm availability and price with the city before assuming any saving.
