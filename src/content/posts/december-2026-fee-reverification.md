This site made a public promise: **cost figures are re-checked against official records quarterly.** The December 2026 pass is now underway across 23 published city posts. This post is the running log — the methodology, the checklist, and confirmed findings as they land.

**Re-verification results will be posted to this page as they are confirmed.** Nothing below asserts a changed number that has not been verified.

## Why December, not January

Two clocks are running. First, California cities adopt new fee schedules throughout the fiscal year — some mid-year — so a figure verified in October can be stale by December. Second, [SB 1117](/posts/sb-1117-explained-plain-english/) takes effect **January 1, 2027**: cities are publishing implementation language now, and we want the January review to be a confirmation, not a surprise.

## The methodology

Every city post was built the same way: official-source fee numbers, captured at a point in time, with every unverified line labeled. The December pass repeats the build for all 23 cities:

1. **Re-pull the fee schedule.** Each city's current master fee schedule (or building, planning, and utility schedules where separate) is pulled fresh and compared against the archived snapshot from October. The October archive holds 37 fee-schedule PDFs captured directly from city sites — the comparison baseline.
2. **Re-confirm school-district resolutions.** School fees are the most volatile line in the series: districts adopt new developer-fee resolutions on their own calendars, and stale city tables (see [Alhambra's $3.79](/posts/alhambra-adu-cost/)) are a known failure mode. Each ranked district's current board-adopted rate is checked.
3. **Re-check city replies.** Several posts carry open questions with the city — Torrance's seven-question inquiry, Garden Grove's and Fullerton's pending items. Outstanding "verify with city" lines get a second attempt, and any reply updates the post it belongs to.
4. **Review SB 1117 implementation language.** With 31 days to the effective date, cities are publishing how they will apply the new marginal math. We are watching for three specific treatments: flat-fee schedules (Torrance's $2,243.47 has no published per-foot rate to marginalize), per-unit fees, and the first-two-ADUs definition.
5. **Re-check the leginfo full text.** Our October 7 attempt to pull the enrolled bill text from leginfo was blocked; the re-check is on this pass's list. The chaptered record (SB 1117, Chapter 809, Statutes of 2026, amending Gov. Code §66311.5) is not in doubt — the full text confirms the details, not the outcome.

## The SB 1117 readiness checklist

Ahead of January 1, these are the specific items under review — the same checklist we would hand any homeowner:

- [ ] **Marginal-rate tables.** Which cities have published 2027 fee language applying impact fees only above 750 sq ft for a homeowner's first two ADUs?
- [ ] **Flat-fee treatment.** How does a flat impact fee (Torrance's $2,243.47) become marginal when there is no per-square-foot rate? Unconfirmed — pending city language or our January review.
- [ ] **Per-unit fee treatment.** Per-unit charges are not per-square-foot charges; their treatment under the new law is unconfirmed.
- [ ] **School fees.** Confirmed unaffected — Education Code regime, outside SB 1117. Every district rate in the [school fee ranking](/posts/adu-school-fees-ranked-california/) stands.
- [ ] **Building permit fees.** Confirmed unaffected — not impact fees under the statute.
- [ ] **Utility timelines.** SB 1196 (hookup deadlines, effective the same day) is tracked separately in [the utility backlog post](/posts/sb-1196-utility-hookup-backlog/).

## What changes, and where

When a re-verified figure differs from the published post, the sequence is fixed: the city post is updated, the change is logged in [Corrections](/corrections/), and the affected post's `lastVerified` date moves. No figure changes on the basis of this post's existence — only on a confirmed official source.

**What this page will show when the pass completes:** a per-city table — confirmed unchanged, updated with new figure, or still pending city reply. Until then, this page is the methodology and the checklist, published so the promise is visible while the work runs.

The quarterly promise only works if the checking is visible. This is the visible part.
