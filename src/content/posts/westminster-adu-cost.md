Westminster passed a new fee schedule that took effect September 1, 2025 — and then, as far as the public web can tell, never showed anyone what is in it. The Application Forms and Fees page announces the new schedule; the actual PDF with the numbers cannot be verified online. That is the honesty-forward story of this post: we publish what is confirmed from official sources, and we label everything else "verify with city" instead of estimating.

What is confirmed is genuinely interesting. The park development impact fee runs a proportional calculation against the primary dwelling — and then caps the result at **$4,000 flat**, the only cap structure in the series. The sewer district publishes a rare ADU-specific capacity rate: $1,002 per 1,000 square feet, or **$801.60** for our 800 sq ft example. And the city's six pre-approved plans include a Plan 1 netting 748 square feet — two square feet under the cliff, which does not look accidental.

Our six-phase total lands at **$306,802\*, or $384 per square foot** — with the asterisk doing heavy lifting. It is a floor: the building permit and the school fee could not be verified, so both are excluded. The old permit structure was valuation-based (plan check at 1.55x and inspection at 1.42x of UAC Table 3-A), which in other cities produces some of the highest permit lines in the series — so this floor may understate the true total by a meaningful margin. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Westminster does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## A new fee schedule nobody can read

Councils adopt new fee schedules routinely; what is not routine is a schedule the public cannot see. Westminster's community development page announces that a new schedule took effect September 1, 2025, but the published document's contents are not verifiable online — so the valuation bands, the permit tiers, everything the new schedule changed is a blank to us. The old published structure is worth knowing anyway: it was valuation-based with aggressive multipliers — plan check at **1.55x** and construction inspection at **1.42x** of UAC Table 3-A, plus a $65 issuance fee. That is a heavier structure than most cities in this series, and whatever the new schedule says, it matters. This is the item to settle first with the Building Division — the permit line is the largest known-unknown in the soft costs.

## The $4,000 cap: the cliff with a ceiling

Every city in this series treats the 750 sq ft cliff differently — exemptions, proportional charges, flat fees. Westminster's version is proportional-with-a-ceiling, and it is the only one like it. Under 750 square feet, the park DIF is fully exempt (confirmed in the Housing Element). At 750 and above, the ADU pays proportionally to the primary dwelling — an 800 sq ft ADU behind a 1,600 sq ft house would compute to $8,589.50 — but the city's PL-202 guide caps the proportional result at **$4,000 flat**. In practice, almost every realistic ADU-to-house ratio hits the cap: even behind a 3,200 sq ft house, the calculation lands at $4,294.75 and gets capped. The cap turns the cliff into a plateau — cross 750 and you owe $4,000, full stop, regardless of the primary dwelling's size. Note the base figure: $17,179 per dwelling (adjusted annually each July 1), not the $13,760 in the ordinance text — use the current number.

## $801.60: the ADU-specific sewer rate

Sewer capacity fees in this series usually come in per-EDU or per-dwelling-unit form, with the ADU application left ambiguous. The Midway City Sanitary District — which handles Westminster's wastewater — is one of the rare exceptions: its Wastewater Capacity Fee Table A has an explicit "Accessory Dwelling Unit" line at **$1,002 per 1,000 square feet** (84 gpd). For 800 sq ft: **$801.60**. No ambiguity about EDUs, no guessing at fixture counts. The city-side water story is the opposite — Westminster runs its own water utility, but no ADU-specific water connection fee could be found in any official source, so that line stays "verify with city."

## Six free plans, one deliberate 748

The city's Pre-Approved ADU Construction Plans Program offers six WHA designs, free through an application packet with a liability waiver. Plan 1 grosses 781 square feet — and nets **748**, two square feet under the 750 sq ft cliff. Plans 2A through 6 step up from there (791 to 1,198 net). Whether or not the 748 was deliberate, it functions as one: pick Plan 1 and the park DIF is fully exempt under the sub-750 rule, the SB 1117 marginal math never triggers, and the fee story simplifies to sewer plus whatever the school districts charge. Design and engineering stays at the $14,000 series baseline in our numbers — the free plans are the alternative path, not the assumption.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Westminster's $4,000 cap would become nearly irrelevant under that math — an 800 sq ft ADU would owe fees on 50 square feet proportionally, a figure far below the cap either way. The sub-750 exemption (already in WMC §3.62) survives; the proportional calculation at 750+ gets replaced by the marginal rule. We found no mention of SB 1117 in city materials; the January 2027 quarterly review will check for a revised park DIF guide. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition.
