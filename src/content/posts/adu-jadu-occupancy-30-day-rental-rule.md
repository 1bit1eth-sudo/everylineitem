Most ADU occupancy advice is paraphrase of paraphrase. This post is the opposite: the rules below are what the statute text actually says, with the two bills that wrote them — AB 976 for standard ADUs, AB 1154 for JADUs — and the places where the wording matters more than the summary.

## Standard ADUs: the owner-occupancy ban, in the statute's words

AB 976 (2023) amended former Government Code section 65852.2, subdivision (a)(8):

> "This subdivision establishes the maximum standards that local agencies shall use to evaluate a proposed accessory dwelling unit on a lot that includes a proposed or existing single-family dwelling. No additional standards, other than those provided in this subdivision, shall be used or imposed, **including an owner-occupant requirement**, except that a local agency may require that the property may be used for rentals of terms **30 days or longer**."

Two consequences follow from that one sentence:

- A city **may not** condition a standard ADU permit on the owner living on the property — detached, attached, or garage conversion. Rent out both the main house and the ADU and live elsewhere: the statute leaves no room for a local owner-occupancy condition on a standard ADU.
- The **only** rental condition the sentence preserves is the 30-day floor: a city may require that the property be used for rentals of terms 30 days or longer. Short-term rental conversion is the one rental restriction the statute allows a city to impose on a standard ADU.

## JADUs: AB 1154 narrowed owner-occupancy to shared bathrooms

Junior ADUs live in a different article — Government Code section 66333 — and AB 1154 (Chapter 507, Statutes of 2025, effective January 1, 2026) rewrote its owner-occupancy rule. Subsection (b) now reads:

> "If the junior accessory dwelling unit has shared sanitation facilities with the existing structure, require owner-occupancy in the single family residence in which the junior accessory dwelling unit will be permitted. The owner may reside in either the remaining portion of the structure or the newly created junior accessory dwelling unit. **Owner-occupancy shall not be required if the junior accessory dwelling unit has separate sanitation facilities, or if the owner is another governmental agency, land trust, or housing organization.**"

The test is the bathroom, not the unit type:

| JADU configuration | Owner-occupancy required? |
| --- | --- |
| Shares sanitation facilities (bathroom) with the main house | **Yes** — owner must live in the main house or the JADU |
| Separate sanitation facilities | **No** |
| Owner is a governmental agency, land trust, or housing organization | **No** |

Before AB 1154, the JADU owner-occupancy requirement applied regardless of shared facilities. The bill's change is exactly this narrowing — and it is the reason a JADU with its own bathroom now behaves, for occupancy purposes, like a standard ADU.

## The 30-day rule, worded two ways

Both unit types have a rental-term floor, but the statutes do not use the same words — and the difference is real:

- **Standard ADU (AB 976):** a city may require rentals of terms "**30 days or longer**."
- **JADU (§ 66333(g)):** the ordinance must "require that a rental of a junior accessory dwelling unit be for a term **longer than 30 days**."

A 30-day term satisfies the standard-ADU wording and does **not** satisfy the JADU wording. If you rent a JADU, treat 31 days as the safe floor. AB 1154 added the JADU floor; before it, JADUs had no statewide rental-term minimum in section 66333, which is why older JADU guidance sometimes reads as if short-term JADU rental were permitted. It no longer is, effective January 1, 2026.

## The JADU deed restriction: what it covers, and what it costs

Section 66333(c) requires "the recordation of a deed restriction, which shall run with the land, shall be filed with the permitting agency, and shall include both of the following":

1. A prohibition on the sale of the JADU separate from the sale of the single-family residence, including a statement that the restriction may be enforced against future purchasers.
2. A restriction on the size and attributes of the JADU that conforms with the article.

Note what is **not** in the list: owner-occupancy. The deed restriction does not itself impose the occupancy rule — the occupancy rule is an ordinance condition under subsection (b). A buyer doing due diligence should still ask for the recorded restriction, because it runs with the land and binds future purchasers on the two items above — but the occupancy question is answered by the bathroom test, not by the deed restriction.

Cost item, stated without an amount: recording the deed restriction is a county-recorder step, and no statewide fee figure exists in the statute. Counties set their own recording fees, so this post lists recordation as a cost item only — check the recorder for the county where the property sits, and do not budget a number from this post.

## When you sell: the rule follows the property

Because the deed restriction runs with the land and is enforceable against future purchasers, a JADU's sale restriction and size/attribute restriction bind the buyer. The shared-bathroom occupancy rule likewise follows the configuration, not the person: a buyer of a shared-bathroom JADU property inherits the same owner-occupancy requirement the seller had. A standard ADU carries no owner-occupancy requirement for the buyer either — AB 976's ban applies to the permit standards, whoever holds title.

## The bottom line

| Rule | Standard ADU | JADU |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner-occupancy may be required? | **No** — AB 976 bars it | **Only if shared sanitation** — § 66333(b) as amended by AB 1154 |
| Rental-term floor | 30 days or longer | Longer than 30 days (treat 31 days as the floor) |
| Deed restriction required? | No state-required JADU-style restriction | Yes — no separate sale + size/attributes; runs with the land |
| Deed restriction recording cost | — | Cost item only; no statewide figure — check the county recorder |
