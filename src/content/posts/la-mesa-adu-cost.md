> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in La Mesa costs at least **$303,398, or about $379 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and the most useful number in that sentence may be the one that is missing. Anyone searching for La Mesa ADU fees will find the city's generosity first: the first $2,000 of ADU permit fees waived, no sewer connection fee for ADUs. What they will not find, in any document the city currently publishes, is a current-year fee schedule. The schedule linked from the city's fee page is the FY 2024-25 edition, adopted by Resolution No. 2025-010 and effective April 1, 2025. This post prices what that schedule prices, applies the waiver exactly as written, and refuses to fill the remaining blanks with newer-looking numbers that do not exist.

La Mesa is also the first city in this batch where the school fee breaks the template. The instruction for split-district cities is strict: confirm each district's rate from that district's own documents before writing a school fee, and if the boundary splits, do not invent a representative combination. La Mesa follows that rule into a dead end, honestly reported: the elementary half is stale, the high-school half is undated and inconsistent with it, so no combined school fee appears in the total at all.

The soft-costs phase is $14,000 of design from the series baseline, $1,398 of net building permit bill, and $0 of sewer connection fee. Everything else that usually lives in that phase — plan review, school fees, RTCIP, trade-permit extras beyond the counted combination fees, and any water capacity charge — is excluded, not estimated. A floor that quietly absorbed guesses would stop being a floor.

One scope note, because readers often ask for the companion figure: this post does not publish a verified total for a new 2,000 sq ft single-family home in La Mesa. The fee schedule prices permits by valuation and never prices construction, so no honest total can be derived from it. The 2,000 sq ft figure appears here only as the denominator that makes the example legible — an 800 sq ft ADU is 40% of a 2,000 sq ft primary home, which is the proportion the state's impact-fee rule uses once the 750 sq ft cliff is crossed.

Two details in that table deserve emphasis. First, the waiver is real but narrow: it covers the first $2,000 of incurred city fees on the ADU building permit application, and the applicant remains responsible for fees due to any other agency, calculated on the full fees before the waiver. It does not waive school fees, county fees, or utility charges, and the counted combination fees are part of the permit bill the waiver applies to. Second, the $0 sewer figure is a housing-element statement plus an ordinance principle, not a line in the fee schedule — and it comes with the lot-characteristics exception described above, priced at the schedule's general rate for reference. Both qualifications are in the table rather than smoothed over.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. Our 800 sq ft example therefore crosses the cliff in principle. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) changes the treatment for lots with two or fewer ADUs: only the area in excess of 750 square feet is charged, so an 800 sq ft unit would be charged on 50 sq ft. That future rule is not in this post's 2026 total. Connection and capacity charges are a separate category from impact fees, which is why sewer and water lines stay in their own row. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The missing current fee schedule.** This is the largest structural unknown. The FY 2024-25 schedule is the latest edition linked from the city's fee page, but a FY 2025-26 or FY 2026-27 edition may exist elsewhere or may have been adopted without the fee page being updated. If the valuation rate or the permit band has moved, the gross permit bill — and therefore the net $1,398 — moves with it. Before budgeting, ask the city which schedule is in force at permit time and whether the $2,000 ADU waiver survives in it. That single answer re-prices the only city fee counted here.

**2. The school fee, both halves.** If both districts confirm current 2026 rates, the combined school fee can be summed and added to this floor by addition, not by revision: the verified lines do not change, only the blank one fills in. Until then, do not borrow the statewide $5.38 cap, the stale elementary rate, the undated high-school half, or any other city's split as a substitute. The school-fee explainer that covers how split districts work across the state is [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/).

**3. RTCIP and the separate-connection question.** The city FAQ exempts ADU-only projects from park fees but says only "maybe" for RTCIP, and no RTCIP dollar figure appears in the fee schedule. Separately, if the lot requires a new sewer or water connection rather than use of the primary dwelling's connection, proportionate connection fees at the schedule's general rate may apply. Both questions are cheap to ask the city and expensive to discover at permit issuance.

## La Mesa vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| La Mesa (verified floor) | **$303,398** | $1,398 net permit bill after waiver + $0 sewer + $0 park (FAQ); school and RTCIP excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

La Mesa sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: La Mesa's figure is missing its school fee, its plan review fee, and any RTCIP charge, while the LA County figures include theirs. Once those blanks fill in, expect the gap to narrow. What La Mesa genuinely leads on is the waiver itself — $2,000 off the top of city fees is a clearer incentive than a low rate buried in a valuation table, even if the table that contains it is an older fiscal year's edition.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $303,398 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, and carry a separate, explicit allowance for the four unpriced items — plan review, the school fee, RTCIP, and any separate-connection charge. When the city answers the schedule question, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision. That is the point of keeping guesses out of the total in the first place.
