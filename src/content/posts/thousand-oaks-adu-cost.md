> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Thousand Oaks costs at least **$302,754, or about $378 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and the most important number in this post is one that is deliberately missing. Anyone researching Thousand Oaks ADU fees will find a district rate that looks ready to multiply and a city schedule that looks ready to price a permit, and both impressions fall apart on a closer read: the district never says what square footage its rate multiplies for an ADU above 750 sq ft, and the city never says an ADU is priced under its residential tables at all. This post counts only what survives that closer read, and leaves the rest labeled rather than estimated.

## The 800 sq ft question the district page does not answer

Conejo Valley Unified School District publishes a Developer Donations page that does three things clearly and one thing not at all. It states that the district collects fees on development and construction projects exceeding 500 square feet within its boundaries. It lists the current residential rate as $3.36 per square foot, beside a $0.54 commercial and senior-citizen rate. And it states an exemption tied to Government Code sections 65852.2 and 65852.22 for ADUs at the 750 sq ft line and junior ADUs at 500 sq ft or less. What it never states is the charging basis once an ADU crosses that line, and an 800 sq ft ADU crosses it by 50 sq ft. Three readings are all consistent with the words on the page, and they produce very different bills:

| Candidate basis for an 800 sq ft ADU at $3.36/sq ft | School fee | In the counted total? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full square footage: 800 × $3.36 | $2,688 | No — basis not stated by the district |
| Excess only: 50 × $3.36 | $168 | No — basis not stated by the district |
| Proportionate to the primary dwelling: (800 / primary sq ft) × $2,688 | Depends on the primary dwelling | No — basis not stated by the district, and this is the impact-fee rule, not a stated school-fee rule |
| **Counted school fee in this post** | **$0** | **Excluded pending district confirmation** |

The gap between the first two readings alone is $2,520, which is larger than every counted fee in this post combined several times over. Picking either one would convert a floor into a guess, so neither is picked. The district page also carries no effective date for the $3.36 rate, which is a second, smaller confirmation item: the rate is stated as current on the page as retrieved on October 26, 2026, but its adoption date is not pinned down here. Both points go to the district as confirmation requests before anyone budgets the school fee, and the hypothetical totals are shown only so readers can see the stakes: adding the full-footage reading would lift the floor to $305,442, or about $382 per square foot, while the excess-only reading would lift it to $302,922, or about $379 per square foot. Neither hypothetical is the headline total.

## The ADU law box: two statutes, and what each one does not do

California's current ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged impact fees only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. That proportion rule is the source of the third candidate reading in the first section — but it is an impact-fee rule, and the district has not stated that it prices its school fee that way. From January 1, 2027, Government Code section 66311.5, as amended by SB 1117, changes the impact-fee rule for up to two ADUs on a lot: impact fees on an ADU above 750 sq ft are charged proportionately to the primary dwelling and only on the area in excess of 750 square feet. That is a future rule, not the rule in force for a 2026 permit, and it still does not decide the school fee, because school developer fees are a separate levy under the Education Code and are not SB 1117 impact fees. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The district's answer on the charging basis.** This is the largest open line and the only one with a $2,520 swing between its two simplest readings. A single sentence from Conejo Valley Unified — full square footage, excess only, or proportionate — collapses the range and moves the school fee from excluded to counted by addition. Until then, the honest floor leaves it out. [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) covers why a statewide cap cannot stand in for a district rate — and in Thousand Oaks, why even the district's own rate is not enough without its basis.

**2. The building permit's ADU treatment.** The schedule's residential band and valuation tables are both real, and neither names ADUs. One confirmation from the city's building division — which table, if either, prices a detached ADU, and what plan review attaches to it — turns an unpriced permit into a counted line. That confirmation matters more than any illustrative band arithmetic, which is why no band total is summed in this post.

**3. The utility and plan lines.** No ADU connection or capacity figure and no city plan catalog or price could be verified from official sources in this round. Readers weighing a city plan set against custom design should read [California's free ADU plans](/posts/free-adu-plans-california/) for the general picture, then confirm availability and price with the city before assuming any saving. When the city answers on utilities, those lines join the floor by addition.

## Thousand Oaks vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. The published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thousand Oaks (verified floor) | **$302,754** | $754 planning fee only; building permit and school fee excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Torrance (LA County) | $317,000 | Flat-rate ADU impact fee in the counted stack |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Thousand Oaks sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: the Thousand Oaks figure is missing its entire building permit bill and its whole school fee, while the LA County figures include theirs. Add only the full-footage school reading and Thousand Oaks reaches $305,442 — still below the range, before any permit is added. The honest reading is not that Thousand Oaks is cheap; it is that its countable fee stack is one flat planning charge, and its two largest fee questions are still open.

How to use this floor in practice: budget no lower than $302,754, ask Conejo Valley Unified for the 800 sq ft charging basis in writing before budgeting any school fee, ask the city's building division which schedule table prices a detached ADU, and hold separate, explicit allowances for the permit, the plan review, the school fee, and the utility charges. When the district and the city answer, those allowances simply join the floor — the headline total grows by addition instead of being rewritten. Keeping guesses out of the total is what makes that possible.
