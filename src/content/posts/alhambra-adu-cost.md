Search the City of Alhambra's master fee schedule for the word "impact" and you get zero hits. Not a fee that skips ADUs, not a fee schedule that buries it — no development impact fee at all. In a series where DIFs run from Salinas's full exemption to Ontario's $37,595-per-dwelling heavyweight, Alhambra's zero is its own kind of statement, and its fee profile is shaped by what fills the gap: a **$2,000 New Construction Tax** on every ADU, a valuation-based permit the city prices in official example estimates, and the steepest water-meter step-up in the series — **$448 without a new meter, $7,222 with one**.

Our six-phase total lands at **$316,000, or $395 per square foot** — just under the LA County baseline. Two items carry asterisks rather than certainties: the school fee ($3,032) is what the city's March 2026 fee schedule tells applicants, but the district's own current board-adopted rate could not be confirmed; and the water line assumes no new meter ($448) rather than the $7,222 alternative. The regional sewer layer — LA County Sanitation District fees, paid directly to the district — could not be pinned to an ADU-specific figure and is excluded. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Alhambra does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## Zero hits: the city with no DIF

Alhambra's Master Fee Schedule, effective February 23, 2026, is a long document — and it contains no impact fee entries. Full-text search: zero. The ADU informational handout (December 2025) and the ADU FAQ list fees by the dozen, and none of them is a development impact fee. That makes Alhambra the series' cleanest negative: most cities' fee schedules at least contain a DIF the ADU might be exempt from; Alhambra simply doesn't levy one. The closest equivalent is the **New Construction Tax of $2,000 per new residential unit** (AMC §5.06.030), which applies to every ADU at every size — it is a per-unit tax, not a proportional impact fee, and our 800 sq ft example pays exactly $2,000. Because no DIF exists, there is no city-level 750 sq ft cliff to write about; the state baseline (no impact fees under 750 sq ft, proportional at 750+) is the only applicable rule.

## The $6,774 water-meter decision

City-side, Alhambra's utilities line is a cliff of its own. The ADU FAQ puts the Utilities Improvement Fees at **$448 if no new water meter** versus **$7,222 with one** — a $6,774 difference hanging on a single design decision. For an 800 sq ft detached ADU sharing the main dwelling's sewer line (allowed where the applicant shows sufficient capacity and slope, per AMC §23.22.040(J)), the $448 figure is the reasonable assumption, and it's what the breakdown carries. The regional layer sits on top: the city directs applicants to the LA County Sanitation District for approval and fees paid directly to the district. We searched the LACSD connection-fee documents for an ADU-specific figure and could not confirm one — that line stays out of the total and on the verify list.

## $3,032: the school fee with a staleness warning

The $3.79/sq ft figure is what the City of Alhambra's Community Development Fee Schedule (updated March 2026) tells applicants — $3,032 on 800 sq ft, paid directly to Alhambra Unified, not to the city. The asterisk is deliberate: $3.79 exactly matches the State Allocation Board's 2022 Level 1 cap, which suggests the city's table was never updated when the SAB raised caps to $5.17 (2024) and $5.38 (2026). Multiple searches for an AUSD board resolution or developer-fee justification study surfaced only other districts' documents. So $3,032 is the city's number, not the district's verified current rate — and if the district has caught up to a later cap, the true figure is higher. Separately, Alhambra runs its own stricter threshold: school-district clearance is required for all new-construction ADUs regardless of size, and for ADU conversions of 500 sq ft or more — stricter than the state's 750 sq ft impact-fee threshold.

## The 850 sq ft proxy: the permit, estimated by the city itself

Alhambra does not price permits flat; it is valuation-based. But the city does something most cities in this series don't — it publishes official example estimates. The December 2025 ADU handout gives a Building Permit Fee of $4,276.84 plus a Building Plan Check Fee of $3,744.37 for an 850 sq ft ADU, and $5,331.81 + $4,668.00 for 1,200 sq ft. No 800 sq ft figure exists, so the 850 is the closest official proxy: **$8,021.21**. The handout's full 850 sq ft total — $11,677.00 — folds in the fixed add-ons (Fire, Public Works, Planning, Utilities plan checks, issuance), the $2,000 New Construction Tax, the percentage fees, SMIP, and technology fees, before separate mechanical, electrical, and plumbing permits. For comparison, that combined permit-plus-plan-check figure is the highest in the series — higher than Burbank's $3,692 — though Alhambra pairs it with a $0 DIF, which no other city does.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Alhambra has no DIF for the cliff to apply to — the smallest SB 1117 story in the series, because there is nothing to recalculate. The $2,000 New Construction Tax applies to every ADU regardless of size and is not an impact-fee line. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and stay at $3,032 pending verification of the district's current rate. We found no mention of SB 1117 in city materials; the January 2027 quarterly review will confirm the zero-DIF position holds.
