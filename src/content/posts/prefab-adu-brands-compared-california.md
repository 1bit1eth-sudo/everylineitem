Prefab ADU brands market the same promise: factory-built, faster, cheaper. But when you go looking for the actual price of an 800 sq ft unit, the industry splits in two. Two brands publish numbers you can check before a sales call. Three don't.

This post holds the same program constant — **800 square feet, detached, Los Angeles County** — and reports what each brand's official website actually says. Nothing here is estimated, extrapolated, or quoted from a press interview. Where a brand doesn't publish a price, we say so.

## The short answer

| Brand | 800 sq ft model | Published price | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Villa Homes** | H800 (2BR/2BA) | **From $282,000** | Complete project pricing — design, permitting, construction, installation |
| **Villa Homes** | C800 (2BR/2BA) | **From $278,000** | Complete project pricing |
| **Villa Homes** | 800 Comfort (2BR/1.5BA) | **From $261,000** | Complete project pricing |
| **Abodu** | 800 (2BR/2BA) | **From $424,800** all-in | Home ($328,800) + typical installation ($96,000) |
| **Boxabl** | Casita (studio, ~391 sq ft) | Not published | Official site shows financing "starting at $895/mo" only |
| **Dvele** | — | Not published | No pricing on official site |
| **Mighty Buildings** | — | Not published | No pricing on official site |

The gap between the two published numbers is the real story of this post: **$282,000 vs $424,800** for 800 square feet — a **$142,800 difference** — both from companies that call their pricing "transparent." They are not selling the same scope, and that is exactly what you need to see before comparing the numbers.

## What each published price actually covers

### Villa Homes — from $261,000 to $282,000 (800 sq ft)

Villa publishes a full product grid on villahomes.com with "complete project pricing" for every model. Three models hit 800 sq ft:

- **H800**: 800 sq ft, 2 bed / 2 bath — **from $282,000**
- **C800**: 800 sq ft, 2 bed / 2 bath — **from $278,000**
- **800 Comfort**: 800 sq ft, 2 bed / 1.5 bath — **from $261,000**

That works out to roughly **$326–$353 per square foot** — the closest any published prefab number gets to our modeled modular all-in of [$288,000](/posts/modular-vs-site-built-adu-cost-california/).

Villa describes this as turnkey: design, permitting, construction, and installation. Their own pricing page carries a disclaimer that estimates are subject to change and final terms are set in the project services agreement — read it as a starting figure, not a quote.

### Abodu — from $424,800 all-in (800 sq ft)

Abodu's fixed-pricing page on abodu.com splits every model into two numbers: a **home price** and a **typical installation price**. For the 800 sq ft, 2BR/2BA model:

- Home: **from $328,800** — factory construction, architectural and interior design, civil engineering, permits and plan check, fire sprinklers, delivery
- Typical installation: **$96,000** — foundation, site work, utility trenching, crane, project management
- **Expected all-in: from $424,800** — roughly **$531 per square foot**

That is **$104,800 above** our site-built series baseline of [$320,000](/posts/800-sq-ft-adu-cost-california/) for the same square footage. Abodu's premium buys a fully managed process and architect-designed product — but it is not the discount path. Note what installation excludes per their page: boundary survey, longer trenching or crane distances, demolition, tree removal, unique site engineering, and sales tax are quoted separately.

## The three brands that don't publish

**Boxabl.** The boxabl.com homepage advertises financing "starting at $895/mo" for the Casita studio (~391 sq ft — half the program we compare). The company has stated unit prices in press interviews, but **no purchase price is published on the official site**. Without an official figure, we report nothing.

**Dvele.** The dvele.com site describes net-zero modular homes with solar and battery systems standard — but publishes **no pricing**. Their positioning is premium and self-powered, which tells you the direction of the price without giving you the number.

**Mighty Buildings.** The mightybuildings.com projects page shows 3D-printed designs including a 1-story ADU — but publishes **no pricing**. The company has claimed production costs "up to 45% less than comparable homes" in press materials, which is a cost claim about their factory, not a price you can pay.

This is not a criticism. All three are legitimate companies. But "transparent pricing" is a marketing phrase until there is a number on the website — and three of the five biggest names in prefab ADU don't have one.

## Six-phase ledger: what the published prices map to

Mapping the two published brands onto our six-phase ledger (800 sq ft, LA County):

| Phase | Villa H800 ($282,000) | Abodu 800 ($424,800) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft costs (design, permits, plan check) | Included in complete price | Included in home price |
| Site work + foundation | Included in complete price | Included in installation ($96,000 covers foundation + site work) |
| Framing + structure | Included (factory) | Included (factory) |
| Building envelope | Included (factory) | Included (factory) |
| Systems + interior | Included (factory) | Included (factory) |
| Landscaping & hardscape | Confirm — scope varies | Confirm — scope varies |
| Crane, transport & staging | Included in complete price | Included in installation |
| **Total** | **From $282,000 ($353/sq ft)** | **From $424,800 ($531/sq ft)** |

Both brands wrap the factory phases (3–5) and logistics into one number. The $142,800 gap lives in design pedigree, project management depth, and brand positioning — not in square footage.

Neither published price includes a **contingency** (budget 10–15% yourself) or **landscaping** beyond basic scope. And Abodu's installation exclusions — survey, extended trenching, demolition, tree removal — are exactly the site-specific items that turn a published price into a real one.

## How to use this comparison

1. **Don't compare unit prices to all-in prices.** Villa's $282,000 is a complete project figure. Abodu's $328,800 home price is not — you need the $424,800 all-in for an apples-to-apples read.
2. **Treat unpublished prices as unknown, not as zero.** Boxabl, Dvele, and Mighty Buildings may be cheaper or more expensive than both published brands. There is no way to know from their websites.
3. **Ask for the installation exclusions in writing.** Abodu publishes theirs; ask Villa and the other three for the same list before you sign.
4. **Run any quote through the six-phase ledger.** Our [worksheet](/worksheet/) is free and takes any contractor or prefab number apart by phase.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which prefab ADU brand is cheapest in California?**

Of the brands that publish prices, Villa Homes is lowest: from $261,000 (800 Comfort) to $282,000 (H800) complete project pricing for 800 sq ft. Abodu's 800 sq ft model starts at $424,800 all-in. Boxabl, Dvele, and Mighty Buildings don't publish prices, so they can't be ranked.

**Why is Abodu so much more expensive than Villa?**

They sell different scopes and positioning. Abodu's all-in includes architect design, dedicated project management, and a premium factory product. Villa's complete project pricing covers the same phases at a lower price point. The $142,800 gap is brand, design, and service depth — not square footage.

**Does Boxabl publish ADU prices?**

Not on boxabl.com. The official site shows financing starting at $895/mo for the Casita studio. The company has discussed unit prices in press interviews, but we only report what the official site publishes.

**Are prefab ADUs cheaper than site-built?**

Sometimes. Our modeled comparison puts modular at [$288,000 vs $320,000 site-built](/posts/modular-vs-site-built-adu-cost-california/) for 800 sq ft — about 10% lower after crane and transport. Villa's published $282,000 lands close to that model. Abodu's $424,800 lands well above it.
