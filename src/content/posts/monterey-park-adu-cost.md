> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Monterey Park costs at least **$312,117, or about $390 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and it is the cleanest arithmetic in this batch. Anyone searching for Monterey Park ADU fees will find a permit table that does something rare in this series: it reproduces, to the dollar, the figure already stored in the fee database. The same schedule then splits the city four ways for school fees and leaves its impact-fee proration to state law rather than printing it. This post takes the clean number, shows its working, and refuses to pretend the split parts are equally clean.

Monterey Park is the clearest illustration in this batch of why the split-district rule exists. Four districts, four fee schedules, one building counter that will not issue a permit until the district has been paid. There is no average district, no typical parcel, and no honest single number — so there is no school fee in the total.

Six phases, the same six used on every build in this series. The soft-costs phase is $14,000 of design from the series baseline, $5,162 of permit and plan check, and $4,955 of proportional impact fees. The city's designer-owned pre-approved plan program (four sets, June 2026) is not counted as a saving because no purchase price is published. Everything else in that phase — the school fee, the county sanitation fee, and the published surcharges — is excluded, not estimated. A floor that quietly absorbed guesses would stop being a floor.

One scope note, because readers often ask for the companion figure: this post does not publish a verified total for a new 2,000 sq ft single-family home in Monterey Park. The fee schedule prices permits by valuation and never prices construction, so no honest total can be derived from it. The 2,000 sq ft figure appears here only as the denominator that makes the example legible — an 800 sq ft ADU is 40% of a 2,000 sq ft primary home, which is the proportion the state's impact-fee rule uses once the 750 sq ft cliff is crossed.

Two details in that table deserve emphasis. First, the surcharges box is the honest price of precision: archival, general plan, and strong-motion charges total about $444 and are stated to apply to all building permits, while the energy and accessibility plan checks add about $461 more if they apply. Counting all of them would raise the floor to roughly $313,000. They are listed, priced, and left out of the total because this post's counted permit figure follows the defined scope of the verified permit-plus-plan-check item, and because the schedule itself marks the energy and accessibility fees "where applicable" without defining the case. A reader who confirms with the city that all of them attach can add about $900 by addition, not by revision.

Second, the impact-fee proration deserves its caveat in full. The schedule prints a unit rate and an under-750 exemption, but it does not print the sentence "ADUs of 750 sq ft or more pay in proportion to the primary dwelling." That sentence comes from state law, and this post applies it. If the city bills an 800 sq ft ADU at the full $12,387 unit rate instead, the floor rises by $7,432. If it bills nothing, the floor falls by $4,955. The range is stated here so that no reader mistakes a derived figure for a printed one.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. Monterey Park's own schedule states the under-750 exemption in those terms, and our 800 sq ft example therefore crosses the cliff in principle. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) changes the treatment for lots with two or fewer ADUs: only the area in excess of 750 square feet is charged, so an 800 sq ft unit would be charged on 50 sq ft. That future rule is not in this post's 2026 total. Connection and capacity charges are a separate category from impact fees, which is why sanitation lines stay in their own row. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The school fee, by parcel.** Four districts is not a rounding problem; it is four different fee schedules, four different offices, and four different rates. The city's building counter will give the applicant a school-fee write-up for the specific project, and the district's office is where the fee is paid. Until that write-up exists, any school number in a Monterey Park post would be a guess wearing a district's name. The school-fee explainer that covers how split districts work across the state is [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/).

**2. The impact-fee billing treatment.** As described above, the $4,955 is a state-law derivation from a printed unit rate, not a printed ADU figure. The city's confirmation — prorated, full-unit, or waived — moves this floor by thousands in either direction. Ask it before the permit budget is fixed, and keep the answer in writing.

**3. The surcharges and the sanitation fee.** About $900 of published surcharges plus an unstated county sanitation fee sit outside the total. None of them is individually large, but together they are the difference between a floor and a budget. The water division's meter-upgrade fees and the engineering division's drainage fees are also project-specific and cannot be priced from the schedule alone.

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Monterey Park sits just below the LA County range, and unlike the other cities in this batch its floor already contains both a full permit bill and a proportional impact fee. The remaining gaps are the school fee, the sanitation fee, and the surcharges — real money, but not the kind that rewrites the comparison.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $312,117 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, and carry a separate, explicit allowance for the three unpriced items — the school fee, the county sanitation fee, and the published surcharges. When the city and the district answer those questions, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision. That is the point of keeping guesses out of the total in the first place.
