> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Vista costs at least **$314,597, or about $393 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and the most interesting thing about that number is that it is newer than the database it replaces. Anyone searching for Vista ADU fees will find two different answers depending on which document they open: the figures stored in this site's own fee database, built from the July 2025 schedule, and the figures in the July 2026 schedule that the same city URL now serves. This post uses the July 2026 schedule throughout, shows every repricing in the open, and leaves the database correction as a confirmation item rather than quietly rewriting history.

Vista is also a city of splits. Two school districts, two sanitation districts, and a conditional fee waiver that applies to some households and not to others. Each split is handled the same way: price what is general, flag what is conditional or parcel-dependent, and never let a conditional saving shrink a floor that is supposed to hold for everyone.

The soft-costs phase is $14,000 of design from the series baseline, $4,880 of permit and plan check, and $7,717 of proportional impact fees. Everything else in that phase — the school fee, sewer charges, and any fire-prevention review — is excluded, not estimated. A floor that quietly absorbed guesses would stop being a floor.

One scope note, because readers often ask for the companion figure: this post does not publish a verified total for a new 2,000 sq ft single-family home in Vista. The fee schedule prices permits by valuation and never prices construction, so no honest total can be derived from it. The 2,000 sq ft figure appears here only as the denominator that makes the example legible — an 800 sq ft ADU is 40% of a 2,000 sq ft primary home, which is the proportion the state's impact-fee rule uses once the 750 sq ft cliff is crossed.

Two details in that table deserve emphasis. First, the repricing is small but instructive: the permit and plan-check base fees did not move between the July 2025 and July 2026 schedules at all — the entire $150 increase comes from the valuation table moving from the 2021 ICC edition to the 2024 edition. Anyone who updates only the fee table and keeps the old valuation will reproduce the old answer. Second, the park fee is the only DIF component with its own future effective date in the schedule (August 14, 2026), and as of this post's verification date that date has passed, so the new rate is the current one. A reader who priced this project in July 2026 would have had to check which side of August 14 their permit fell on.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. Our 800 sq ft example therefore crosses the cliff in principle, and the $7,717 above is that proportional charge. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) changes the treatment for lots with two or fewer ADUs: only the area in excess of 750 square feet is charged, so an 800 sq ft unit would be charged on 50 sq ft rather than by the primary-dwelling proportion. That future rule is not in this post's 2026 total. Connection and capacity charges are a separate category from impact fees, which is why sewer lines stay in their own row. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The school fee, by parcel.** Vista is not a one-district city for this purpose, and the schedule's silence on rates means the school fee cannot be estimated from city documents at all. The district's own fee page is the only honest source, and which district's page to open depends on the parcel. The school-fee explainer that covers how split districts work across the state is [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/).

**2. The conditional waiver.** Third-party guides describe a waiver under which the entire $7,717 impact fee may be waivable for a qualifying household — eligible low-income occupancy or a family-member/caregiver occupant, plus a recorded covenant restricting occupancy for ten years from the certificate of occupancy — but no city source confirming that program was located in this session. If those conditions fit and the city confirms the program, the floor for that household falls to $306,880 before any other change. If they do not, the full $7,717 stands. A floor must hold for the general case, which is why the waiver is described here and not subtracted above.

**3. Sewer district and fire review.** Two sanitation districts serve Vista at different EDU rates ($6,978.94 vs. $8,391.23), and neither rate is counted because the ADU connection treatment is unstated. Likewise, the fire-prevention plan review at 19% of plan check would add about $465 if it attaches to a detached ADU permit. Both are single questions to the city, and both should be asked before the permit budget is fixed.

## Vista vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vista (verified floor) | **$314,597** | $4,880 permit + plan check + $7,717 proportional DIF; school and sewer excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Vista lands inside the LA County range, which is the honest place for it: unlike the other cities in this batch, its floor already contains both a full permit bill and a full proportional impact fee. The remaining gaps — school and sewer — are the same gaps that would move any city's number, and they move Vista's by thousands, not by tens of thousands.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $314,597 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, and carry a separate, explicit allowance for the three unpriced items — the school fee, the sewer treatment, and the fire-prevention review. When the city and the district answer those questions, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision. That is the point of keeping guesses out of the total in the first place.
