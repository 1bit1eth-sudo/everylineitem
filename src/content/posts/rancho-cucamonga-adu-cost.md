An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Rancho Cucamonga costs at least **$308,258, or about $385 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and anyone searching for Rancho Cucamonga ADU fees will find a permit line that answers its own most common follow-up question. The city's FY 2026-2027 schedule does not just price an R3 accessory dwelling unit permit at $1,954 for 800 square feet; it states that mechanical, electrical, and plumbing are included in that price. Paired with a school fee stated as a complete two-district total, this is the batch's cleanest two-line stack: a permit that needs no trade-permit footnote, and a school fee that needs no missing-half footnote.

## Two lines, both complete

The counted total here is unusually short, and that shortness is the story. Most posts in this series count a permit line and then spend a paragraph explaining which surcharges or trade permits sit outside it, or count a school rate and then explain which district half is missing. Rancho Cucamonga's two lines arrive complete:

| Counted line (FY 2026-2027) | Amount | Why it is complete |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R3 accessory dwelling unit permit, 800 sq ft | $1,954 | Schedule states MEPs included |
| School fee, $5.38 × 800 sq ft | $4,304 | Justification report states both district halves |
| **Counted fees** | **$6,258** | |

The school split is worth stating in full because split-district school fees are where this series most often goes wrong. Cucamonga School District's May 14, 2026 justification report prices the combined residential rate at $5.38 per square foot, split $3.71 to the TK-8 district and $1.67 to Chaffey Joint Union High School District. At 800 square feet that is $2,968 plus $1,336, totaling $4,304. A post that published only the $3.71 elementary half would understate the school line by $1,336; a post that substituted the statewide $5.38 ceiling without a district document behind it would reach the same total for the wrong reason. Here the total and the document agree, so the full $4,304 is counted.

One scope note, because readers often ask for the companion figure: this post does not publish a verified total for a new single-family home in Rancho Cucamonga. The schedule prices permits by class and the justification report prices school fees per square foot, but neither prices construction, so no honest home total can be derived from them. > **The 750 sq ft rule, in plain terms.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. Neither counted line in this post is changed by that rule: the R3 permit is a fee-schedule charge, not an impact fee, and the school fee is a separate district charge that applies to an 800 sq ft ADU in full. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5) likewise leaves both counted lines unchanged; it flattens impact fees for lots with two or fewer ADUs, and no impact fee is counted here. The school-fee rule that does matter for this post is the size exemption: an ADU smaller than 500 square feet pays no school fee, while this 800 sq ft unit pays the full $4,304. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The uncounted impact position.** The two counted lines are complete, but they are not the whole fee picture: any other city impact fee that applies to an ADU would join the floor by addition, and this round did not verify that position either way. That is the first question to the city, precisely because the counted lines look so finished that a reader might mistake completeness of the lines for completeness of the picture.

**2. The sewer and water charges.** No ADU-specific connection or capacity charge was verified, and sewer and water in this part of the Inland Empire run through district systems whose charges do not appear in the city schedule. Confirm the connection route and the district's charge with the city and the district before budgeting this line; [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) is not the source for it, but it illustrates the same discipline — a charge is counted only from the body that levies it.

**3. The permit's scope for the actual plans.** The $1,954 line is stated for 800 square feet with MEPs included, and it should be re-confirmed for the actual plans: a larger unit, a different occupancy class, or additional inspections could price differently. The line is complete as stated, not as a guarantee for every design.

Rancho Cucamonga sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: its figure is missing any other city impact fee and its sewer charges, while the LA County figures include theirs. What Rancho Cucamonga genuinely offers is a permit line that can be quoted without a footnote — a rarer quality in this series than a low number.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $308,258 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, confirm the full city impact position and the sewer and water charges with the city and district, and carry separate, explicit allowances for both. When those answers arrive, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision.
