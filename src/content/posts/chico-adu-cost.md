Chico's ledger is built on the school fee. Chico Unified's Level I rate is the **$5.38** state maximum, effective July 19, 2026 — **$4,304** on 800 sq ft, the largest single verified fee line in the post. The rest of the stack is small and specific: a flat **$1,460** ADU permit for a new detached structure, a **$282** energy plan-check minimum, and a seismic surcharge of 0.013% of valuation — **$41.60** on the $320,000 series-model valuation. The city water connection is **$0** because Cal Water serves Chico and no new meter is required. Our six-phase total lands at **$308,000, or $385 per square foot** — but it is a floor, not a finished figure. The building permit fee and impact fees are not yet verified, so they are not in the total at all.

The $1,460 ADU permit is a planning fee, not the building permit — the building permit itself has no verified row on file, and neither does any impact fee. Those are real costs a homeowner will pay; this ledger names them rather than guessing at them. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Chico does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## The school fee carries the ledger

The school fee is 70% of the verified fee stack — $4,304 of $6,087.60 — which makes Chico the rare post where the district, not the city, sets the price. The rate is the $5.38 state maximum, adopted by Chico Unified and effective July 19, 2026, and it applies in full at 800 sq ft because the assessable space is over 500 sq ft. There is nothing proportional or banded about it: $5.38 times 800, verified from the district's own page.

The small lines are worth naming because two of them are unusual. The seismic surcharge is computed on valuation, not on square footage — 0.013% of established construction valuation under Public Resources Code §2705 — so it moves with the project's valuation rather than its size. The $41.60 here uses the $320,000 series-model valuation; the city's own established valuation for a specific project could move this line by a few dollars either way. And the $282 energy plan check is a minimum, not a computed figure: the schedule prices it for up to two residential units, and this ADU is one.

## Two gaps that matter

This total is a floor, and the two gaps are the reason. We have no verified building permit or plan-check fee for Chico — the $1,460 ADU permit is a planning fee, and the building permit itself is a separate, unpriced line. We have no verified impact fee either — no row on file at all, so this post claims nothing about Chico's impact fees, $0 or otherwise. Both are real costs a homeowner will pay; neither is in the $308,000. When they are verified, this post will be updated and the total will rise.

The $308,000 also carries one soft footnote. The seismic surcharge is computed on the series-model valuation of $320,000. If the city's established valuation for a real project differs, the $41.60 moves with it — by dollars, not by thousands — and the total barely notices.

## The ADU law box

Three state rules frame every number above. Under Government Code §65852.2(f)(3)(A), a local agency may not impose impact fees on an ADU under 750 square feet; at 750 or above, fees are charged proportionately to the primary dwelling's size. Under §66311.5 (SB 1117, effective January 1, 2027), per-square-foot impact fees on two or fewer ADUs are charged only on the area above 750 square feet — 50 square feet on this 800 sq ft benchmark — and connection and capacity charges are excluded from that rule. School fees are a separate statute: under SB 543, ADUs under 500 square feet pay $0 in school fees, and at 800 square feet the full district rate applies. SB 1117 does not reduce school fees, and it does not change any line counted in this post: no impact fee is counted, and the school fee is untouched by SB 1117.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. No impact fee is counted in this ledger, so SB 1117 changes nothing we can compute here — there is nothing to reduce. See the full comparison at [Chico: 2026 vs 2027, line by line](/sb-1117/chico/). School fees, permit fees and the water connection are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and do not change.
