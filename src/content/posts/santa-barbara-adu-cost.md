Santa Barbara's building permit for an 800 sq ft ADU costs **$22,566**. Fullerton charges $1,223 for the same piece of paper. That is not a typo — it is roughly an 18x multiple, the highest permit bill this series has published, and it single-handedly makes Santa Barbara the most expensive fee environment we have priced.

The city's fee schedule (Resolution No. 2026-059, FY 2027) prices new R-3 dwellings by square footage with unusual transparency: a **$23,704 base for 1,000 sq ft** ($17,383 permit + $6,321 plan check), scaled proportionally — 800/1,000 × $23,704 = **$18,963** ($13,906 permit + $5,057 plan check). Then come the mandatory add-ons: an **8% technology fee** ($1,517) and an **11% growth-management / general plan update fee** ($2,086), both levied on projects creating new square footage. Total: $22,566. This is the schedule's own methodology, not our estimate.

The counterweight is equally stark: Santa Barbara levies **no development impact fees at all**. The full fee schedule contains zero DIF line items. The 750 sq ft cliff — the defining feature of most coastal California ADU fee structures — is moot here. And when the ADU shares the lot's existing water meter (the standard case under state law), sewer and water capacity charges are **$0**. The city's fee philosophy is concentrated, not diffuse: one enormous permit bill, nothing else.

The Preapproved ADU Program (May 2025, implementing AB 1332) is the city's answer to its own permit pricing. Ready-to-use designs get a **25% plan-check discount** — a flat $2,369 for 501–800 sq ft versus the standard $5,057 — plus review in half the standard time and staff review within 10 business days. On an $18,963 standard permit path, the program saves roughly $2,700 in plan check alone.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Santa Barbara has no impact fees, so SB 1117 changes nothing on the DIF line — there is nothing to prorate. The permit fee, which is the actual story here, is outside SB 1117's scope entirely. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and stay at $4,136.
