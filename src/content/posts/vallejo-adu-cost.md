In Vallejo, the sewer district sets the price. The Vallejo Flood & Wastewater District charges a **$2,865** sanitary sewer capacity charge for a detached ADU of 800–899 sq ft on the Mainland — a size-banded charge, not a flat rate — and then adds **$580** for plan review, **$400** for inspection, and **$61.94** for permit processing. The four district lines total **$3,906.94**, more than 80% of the verified fee stack. The city's own planning fee is **$968**, itemized down to its surcharges. Our six-phase total lands at **$307,000, or $383 per square foot** — but it is a floor, not a finished figure. The school district fee and the building permit fee are not yet verified, so they are not in the total at all.

The $968 planning fee is the most itemized line in the post: an $866 base plus 5% General Plan Update ($43), 4% Technology ($34), and 3% Permit Streamlining ($25). The capacity charge is banded — the district's schedule prices detached ADUs by size, 800–899 sq ft on the Mainland at $2,865, with bands starting at 501 sq ft and contained ADUs never charged. What is not in the total is the school fee and the building permit — no verified rows exist for either, so they are excluded rather than guessed at. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Vallejo does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## A banded sewer charge

The $2,865 capacity charge is the quirk, and the band is the point. The Vallejo Flood & Wastewater District does not charge a flat sewer fee: it prices detached ADUs by size band, and 800–899 sq ft on the Mainland is $2,865. A smaller ADU would fall in a lower band; a contained ADU — one inside the existing structure — is never charged at all. The band is where the money is: the capacity charge alone is more than half the verified fee stack, and the district's four lines together are over 80% of it.

The planning fee is the other end of the same story. Where the district prices by band, the city prices by surcharge: $866 base, then 5% for the General Plan Update, 4% for Technology, and 3% for Permit Streamlining, each computed and shown. Nothing in this post is a single unexplained number — the two priced lines are both shown down to their components.

## Two gaps that matter

This total is a floor, and the two gaps are the reason. We have no verified school district fee for Vallejo — at the $5.38 state maximum it would add $4,304. We have no verified building permit or plan-check fee either — the $968 ADU planning fee is a planning line, and the building permit itself is a separate, unpriced line. We have no verified impact fee at all, so this post claims nothing about Vallejo's impact fees, $0 or otherwise. All three are real costs a homeowner will pay; none is in the $307,000. When they are verified, this post will be updated and the total will rise.

## The ADU law box

Three state rules frame every number above. Under Government Code §65852.2(f)(3)(A), a local agency may not impose impact fees on an ADU under 750 square feet; at 750 or above, fees are charged proportionately to the primary dwelling's size. Under §66311.5 (SB 1117, effective January 1, 2027), per-square-foot impact fees on two or fewer ADUs are charged only on the area above 750 square feet — 50 square feet on this 800 sq ft benchmark — and connection and capacity charges are excluded from that rule. School fees are a separate statute: under SB 543, ADUs under 500 square feet pay $0 in school fees, and at 800 square feet the full district rate applies. SB 1117 does not reduce school fees, and it does not change any line counted in this post: no impact fee is counted, the sewer capacity charge is a connection/capacity charge outside SB 1117, and the school fee is untouched by SB 1117.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. No impact fee is counted in this ledger, and the sewer capacity charge is a connection/capacity charge outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition, so SB 1117 changes nothing we can compute here. See the full comparison at [Vallejo: 2026 vs 2027, line by line](/sb-1117/vallejo/). School fees and permit fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and do not change.
