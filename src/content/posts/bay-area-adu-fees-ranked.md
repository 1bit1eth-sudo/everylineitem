"Bay Area expensive" is true about the houses. It is not reliably true about the ADU fees — and the ranking below is the proof. Four cities, one 800 sq ft detached ADU, four entirely different philosophies about what an ADU owes the city it sits in.

## The ranking

| | Palo Alto | Oakland | San Francisco | San Jose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Total (800 sq ft)** | **~$303,000** | **$322,000** | **$325,000** | **~$400,000** |
| Per sq ft | ~$379 | $403 | $406 | ~$500 |
| Building permit | ~$5,790 (1.76%) | $12,800–$19,200 | ~$19,500 | $5,103 |
| School fee | $3,832 ($4.79) | $4,136 ($5.17) | $3,032 | varies by district |
| Impact / DIF | ~$8,000–$20,000* | **$0 (exempt)** | ~$0 (minimal) | ~$17,000–$24,500 fees+taxes |
| Sewer / water | TBD | $0 | $0 | varies |
| 750 sq ft cliff | **Steepest per-foot** | None (exempt) | None | Tiered |
| Free plans | In development | Pre-approved | None (0 approved) | Private vendors |

*Palo Alto DIF range reflects proportionality to the primary dwelling; see correction note below.

The nearly $100,000 spread between Palo Alto and San Jose — fifteen miles apart — is the whole argument for reading fee schedules before architects' portfolios.

## Four philosophies

**Palo Alto: the steep cliff, the proportional bill.** The per-square-foot rates are the highest we have published anywhere — $18.65/sq ft for parks alone. But the ordinance makes 750+ sq ft ADUs pay proportionally to the house, so the actual 800 sq ft bill lands around $8,000–$20,000, not the six figures the raw rates suggest. The cliff itself is the story: 749 sq ft pays $0, 750 pays thousands. And from January 2027, SB 1117 collapses it toward ~$500–$1,250 — the most dramatic single-law swing in the series.

**Oakland: the exemption.** Two city documents state it plainly: no impact fees on ADUs, the opposite of the cliff. Oakland's soft costs come from elsewhere — a valuation-based building permit of $12,800–$19,200 (4–6% of construction value, the series high) and the $4,136 school fee. The policy logic is legible: Oakland wants ADUs and priced the fee schedule accordingly.

**San Francisco: the small-lot carve-out.** The city's State ADU Program exempts qualifying ADUs on small lots from impact fees; the permit bill (~$19,500) and $3,032 school fee carry the soft costs. Zero pre-approved ADU plans — the only Bay Area city in this ranking without a program.

**San Jose: the stack.** The building permit itself is modest ($5,103 at 800 sq ft), but the stack above it is the heaviest in the Bay Area: a 13% citywide planning fee, construction taxes at 3.96% of valuation (~$5,573 on the city's own numbers), school fees, and parkland fees of $1,700–$8,900 that switch on above 750 sq ft. Fees and taxes we could source total roughly $17,000–$24,500 — more than some cities' entire soft-cost budget.

## A correction we owe you

Our Batch 3 research matrix originally listed Palo Alto's DIF at $76,385 and the permit at 5.42% (~$17,347). Both numbers failed verification against the city's official documents — the DIF figure appears to have confused an in-lieu fee that does not apply to ADUs, and the permit rate is 1.76%, not 5.42%. The corrected total (~$303,000) makes Palo Alto a series-low candidate, not the series high we first reported. We corrected the matrix, kept the wrong numbers visible with a banner explaining the error, and wrote the full post from the verified documents. This site's core value is honesty; that includes about our own mistakes.

## What changes January 1, 2027

SB 1117's marginal rule (fees only above 750 sq ft) rewrites the Palo Alto row more than any other in this ranking — ~$8,000–$20,000 toward ~$500–$1,250. San Jose's parkland fee faces the same math. Oakland and San Francisco barely move: exemptions don't need rewriting. The Bay Area ranking will look different in 2027, and the January quarterly re-verification will republish it.

## Disclaimer

Model estimates, not quotes. Bay Area construction costs run above the Southern California baseline used in some rows; San Jose's total is explicitly our estimate. Always verify current fees with each city before budgeting.
