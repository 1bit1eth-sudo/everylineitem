> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

Costa Mesa is the thinnest file in this series so far, and that thinness is itself the finding. The verified Costa Mesa ADU fees add up to just $5,846 on an 800 sq ft detached unit — a school fee and a sewer charge, both confirmed from the agencies that levy them — while the city's own building permit and impact fees sit behind a fee schedule whose next edition is still waiting on a council hearing. The floor total is **$307,846, or about $385 per square foot**, built from the $302,000 series baseline plus those two confirmed charges. Everything the city itself has not yet priced is marked "verify with city" below, not folded into the total as a guess.

That discipline matters more here than in most cities in the series, because Costa Mesa's unpriced lines are plausibly large. The city's park fee for a single-family home is $13,572 per unit, and an 800 sq ft ADU crosses the 750 sq ft cliff, so some share of it — plus traffic, drainage, and fire charges — will eventually land on the bill. The $307,846 figure is the ground the number stands on, not the ceiling it stops at.

## The 750 cliff, and why an 800 sq ft design pays for crossing it

> **The state rule.** Under Government Code §65852.2(f)(3)(A), a city, special district, or water provider may not levy any impact fee on an ADU below 750 sq ft. At 750 sq ft and above, any impact fee must be scaled to the size of the main home on the lot. Costa Mesa's own zoning code for ADUs, §13-35, was updated in 2025 to track current state law.

An 800 sq ft unit is 50 sq ft past that line, and in Costa Mesa the crossing is expensive in principle. The only ADU-specific computation the city has published is a 2020 staff report: the park fee for an ADU was scaled to the primary dwelling's size and capped at the $5,000 rental-unit rate. Applied to the familiar series example — an 800 sq ft ADU beside a 2,000 sq ft house, where the ADU is 40% of the main home — that old method would put the park share at about $5,429 (40% of $13,572), then cap it at $5,000. We do not put that $5,000 in the total, because the method is six years old and the current schedule carries no ADU line to confirm it against. But it shows the order of magnitude at stake: the cliff is not a rounding error here, it is potentially the largest city fee on the project.

Two related wrinkles deserve attention. First, the 800 sq ft figure is 40% of a 2,000 sq ft main home only as a proportional denominator for that park-fee math; the fee schedule prices building permits by valuation, not by home size, so no verified total for a 2,000 sq ft new home can be derived from it, and none is published here. Second, SB 1117, effective January 2027, will change the cliff's shape for a homeowner's first two ADUs: impact fees will apply only to the square footage above 750, measured marginally. On an 800 sq ft unit that means fees on 50 sq ft rather than on a share of the whole. School fees and sewer capacity charges are outside SB 1117's scope, so the two verified lines in this post are unaffected. The January 2027 quarterly review will check whether Costa Mesa has rewritten its ADU fee method to match.

## Pre-approved plans: a program without plans

Costa Mesa's council approved a preapproved ADU design program in March 2025, alongside an ordinance update aligning local ADU rules with state law. That is a real step — a published plan set would cut both the $14,000 design line and review time. But as of October 2026, no plan set could be located on the city's ADU pages, and the city's pre-engineered ADU page itself was unreachable during this review. Until plans are actually published, treat the program as pending: the design budget stays at $14,000, and the [free plans roundup](/posts/free-adu-plans-california/) does not yet count Costa Mesa among its cities. For context on how the 750 sq ft line works everywhere, see [the cliff explainer](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/), and for how school fees work across districts, see [the school-fee explainer](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The building permit, once the new schedule lands.** The published valuation tiers run from $40.12 at the bottom to nearly $10,000 at $1,000,000 of valuation, with plan check at 65% and document retention at 5% on top — but those rates are dated July 2022, the schedule carries an annual inflator, and the FY 2026–27 consolidated schedule has been continued repeatedly, most recently to November 17, 2026. Until that hearing produces an adopted schedule, any permit estimate is arithmetic on stale rates. This is the first line to re-check after the hearing.

**2. The 2026 storm drainage nexus study.** In April 2026 the council adopted a Harris & Associates nexus study and Resolution 2026-14, setting new storm drainage impact fees of $95,015 per new impervious acre in the Newport Bay watershed and $109,809 in the Santa Ana watershed, effective June 6, 2026. Whether an ADU's footprint counts as new impervious acreage, and how the 750 sq ft proportionality rule interacts with a per-acre charge, is nowhere stated in the published material. If the city applies it, a detached ADU's footprint could add a four-figure line; if it does not, the line is zero. Either way it belongs in the pre-application questions, not in a guessed total.

**3. The water connection and the parcel's school district.** Retail water in Costa Mesa comes from Mesa Water District, not the city, and no ADU-specific connection charge was found in the district's published material — a separate meter or an upsized meter could carry a charge that is invisible in every city document. Separately, school fees follow the parcel's district: the verified $4,136 is Newport-Mesa Unified's rate, and the district's own page directs Costa Mesa applicants to the city's Development Services Department. If a parcel's assignment ever differed, that district's rate would govern instead. Both checks cost a phone call; both can move the total by thousands.
