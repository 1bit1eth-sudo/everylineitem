Factory-built ADUs are marketed as cheaper and faster. Sometimes they are. Sometimes the crane, the trucking, and a narrow street erase the savings before the modules leave the yard.

We held the same program constant — **800 square feet, detached, single story, mid-grade finishes, Los Angeles County** — and priced it two ways: conventional **site-built** (the [series baseline at $320,000](/posts/800-sq-ft-adu-cost-california/)) and a typical **modular / panelized** path. The modular all-in came to **$288,000, or $360 per square foot** — about **10% lower**, inside the common 10–25% hard-cost savings band after you add what the brochure leaves out.

## Phase-by-phase comparison

Same six phases as every post in this series, plus an explicit **crane / transport / staging** row for modular so the savings are not hiding a six-figure trucking bill.

| Phase | Site-built | Modular | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Soft costs (design, permits, plan check) | $32,000 | $29,000 | Factory drawings can trim architecture; site adaptation, Title 24, and local fees remain |
| Site work + foundation | $38,000 | $36,000 | Still need a foundation engineered for modules; slight savings, not elimination |
| Framing + structure | $48,000 | $32,000 | **Largest structural save** — volume framed in the factory |
| Building envelope | $42,000 | $28,000 | Walls, roof planes, and much weather barrier arrive with the boxes |
| Systems + interior | $147,000 | $128,000 | Factory rough-in and finishes cut site labor; kitchen/bath packages still dominate |
| Landscaping & hardscape | $13,000 | $13,000 | Same yard work after set |
| Crane, transport & staging | — | **$22,000** | **Grows** — modules do not walk themselves onto the pad |
| **Total** | **$320,000** | **$288,000** | **$360 / sq ft** |

Read the table in two passes. First pass: framing, envelope, and systems drop by **$49,000** combined — that is the real factory advantage (about **21%** on those three phases). Second pass: soft costs and foundation barely move, landscaping does not move, and **$22,000** of logistics walk back onto the ticket. Net: **$32,000**.

That pattern matches what Southern California contractors report in 2026: modular hard costs often run **10–25% below** a comparable site-built shell **before** crane and transport. After logistics, many projects land closer to **5–15%** all-in — still real money, not magic.

## Where modular savings land

**Framing and structure.** Site crews stick-build walls, floors, and roof one day at a time. A modular plant builds volumetric boxes or panelized assemblies under a roof, on a schedule that does not care about your neighbor's sprinklers. Labor hours per square foot fall hard. That is why this phase drops from $48,000 to $32,000 in the model.

**Envelope.** Sheathing, WRB, windows, and much of the roof assembly travel with the modules. Site envelope work shrinks to seams, connections, and punch. Savings here are large when the design stays close to the factory catalog.

**Systems and interior.** Rough electrical and plumbing can be stubbed in the plant; cabinets and finishes can ship installed. You still pay for one kitchen and one bathroom — those do not become half-price because the walls were built in Perris instead of in your backyard. The [400 vs 800](/posts/400-sq-ft-adu-cost-california/) lesson still applies: wet rooms dominate.

## Where modular costs grow — or refuse to shrink

**Foundation still exists.** Modules need a pad, stem walls, piles, or an engineered crawlspace matched to the factory's anchorage details. The [800 sq ft site-work line](/posts/800-sq-ft-adu-cost-california/) at $38,000 only dips slightly here. Anyone selling "no foundation needed" for a permanent California ADU is selling a different product category.

**Crane, transport, and staging — $22,000 in this model.** Typical band for an 800 sq ft multi-module set in LA County: roughly **$15,000–$30,000** depending on distance from the plant, number of modules, overnight escorts, and whether the crane needs a second day. Narrow streets, low wires, and HOA staging rules push the top of the band.

**Soft costs do not disappear.** You still need local permits, plan check, energy docs, and a design professional who will stamp the site adaptation. Factory plans help; they do not replace the [soft-cost phase](/posts/adu-soft-costs-permits-california/). Our model trims $3,000, not $20,000.

**Customization tax.** Every departure from the manufacturer's standard plan — taller walls, nonstandard windows, a second bath configuration — adds engineering, plant change orders, and schedule risk. Site-built absorbs design changes differently; modular punishes them at the factory door.

## When modular wins

- **Street access is clean.** Truck + crane can reach the pad without temporary power shutdowns, tree removal drama, or a second crane for a blind set.
- **The plan is close to stock.** Catalog or lightly adapted plans capture most of the factory savings.
- **Schedule matters.** Factory lead time plus a short on-site set often beats a long stick-built framing weather window — useful when a construction loan is burning interest (see [all-in cost](/posts/adu-all-in-cost-california/)).
- **Labor markets are hot.** When local framing crews are scarce or priced at a premium, the plant's labor rate advantage widens.
- **You want a predictable shell.** Factory QA reduces some on-site variability in the dry phases.

## When modular loses — or should not be forced

- **Access is bad.** Cul-de-sacs, overhead lines, hillside switchbacks, or lots that only admit a pickup truck. Crane and temporary road work can exceed the framing savings.
- **Heavy customization.** If the design is unique enough that you are fighting the plant on every wall, you are paying factory premiums without factory efficiency.
- **Foundation or soils dominate.** Soft soils, steep grade, or retaining that already push site work toward $60,000+ make the modular vs site-built delta a smaller share of the total.
- **Local inspectors and factory detailing clash.** Some jurisdictions are smooth with modular ADUs; others create serial RFIs on mating joints and utility ties. Ask for recent local references, not only plant marketing.
- **You needed a [garage conversion](/posts/garage-conversion-adu-cost-california/) instead.** Reusing an existing shell is a third path; do not compare modular only to site-built if conversion is viable.

## Schedule note (not a promise)

Site-built detached ADUs in this series commonly run **nine to fourteen months** drawing-to-final. Modular projects often compress on-site construction to weeks after foundation and utilities are ready — but factory slots, transport booking, and permit timing still gate the calendar. Faster on-site is not the same as a six-month total if plan check sits for three.

### How to check this number against your own address

Most California jurisdictions publish permit records online, including the
**declared construction valuation** for each project. That declared valuation
is the closest thing to a real local baseline you can get without paying
anyone.

1. Open [EPIC-LA](https://epicla.lacounty.gov/) for unincorporated LA County,
   or the [LADBS Permit & Inspection Report](https://www.ladbsservices2.lacity.org/onlineservices/?service=plr)
   for addresses inside the City of Los Angeles.
2. Search recent ADU permits within about a mile of your address.
3. Compare the declared valuation to the phase totals on this page.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a modular ADU cheaper than site-built in California?**

Often yes on hard costs — commonly **10–25%** before crane and transport. In this 800 sq ft comparison, after logistics the modular path lands about **10% lower** ($288,000 vs $320,000). Bad access can erase that gap.

**Do modular ADUs still need a foundation?**

Yes, for permanent California ADUs. The foundation line barely shrinks versus site-built in this model ($36,000 vs $38,000).

**What does crane and transport cost?**

This model uses **$22,000**. Real projects often fall in a **$15,000–$30,000** band for an 800 sq ft set, with outliers above that on constrained lots.

**Are permits easier for modular?**

Not automatically. You still need local plan check and inspections. Factory plans can reduce drafting cost slightly; they do not waive [soft costs](/posts/adu-soft-costs-permits-california/).

**When should I choose site-built?**

When the lot is hard to crane, the design is highly custom, or local modular execution has weak references. Site-built remains the series baseline for a reason — it is the most flexible path on awkward parcels.
