Downey has a split personality, and Ordinance No. 23-1507 is the diagnosis. Below 750 square feet, the city waves ADUs through with **zero impact fees**. At 750 and above, the same project pays fees proportional to the primary dwelling — and gets its building permit through a valuation-based schedule that puts an 800 sq ft ADU around **$4,972** before surcharges. It is the most generous cliff and one of the least generous permit counters in the series, in the same city.

Our six-phase total lands at **$311,000, or $389 per square foot** — a floor, not a total, marked with the asterisk where it appears. The permit example assumes a $150,000 valuation that the Building Official must ultimately set. The proportional impact fee (the base dwelling amounts are not published) and sewer/water capacity charges are marked "verify with city" and excluded from the total. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Downey does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## The split personality: $0 impact fees, $4,972 permits

Read Ordinance 23-1507 in the morning and Downey looks like the most ADU-friendly city in the series. Section 9414.14(i) waives impact fees completely for ADUs under 750 square feet — cleaner than Chula Vista's bulletin prose, since it is written straight into the municipal code. Conversion ADUs get the same generosity on utilities: no new connection, no capacity charges, $0.

Then take that same 800 sq ft project to the permit counter and the mood changes. The fee schedule is valuation-based with plan check at a full **85% of the permit fee** — one of the steepest plan-check multipliers in the series. On our assumed $150,000 valuation that is $2,687.42 in permit fees and $2,284.31 in plan check, subtotal ~$4,972, and the surcharges (issuance, technology, SMIP, building standards) stack on top. The wrinkle is the valuation itself: the city does not publish a per-square-foot valuation for ADUs, so every homeowner's number is a guess until the Building Official rules. The most certain thing on the fee sheet is the least certain thing about the project.

## $4,136 school fees — with a 2024 asterisk

The school fee arithmetic is clean: **$5.17 per square foot** for the Downey Unified School District, from the city's Building Permit Fee Schedule effective August 26, 2024 — $4,136 on 800 sq ft. The asterisk is the date. The city quotes DUSD's rate with the explicit note that fees are "subject to change by the Downey Unified School District," and DUSD's own adopted 2026 resolution — which would show whether the state board's $5.38 adjustment applies — could not be found online. Two years is a long time for a rate to sit still while the state ceiling moves. We count the city's published $4,136, and flag the resolution as the item to confirm before budgeting.

## The proportional DIF with no published base

Downey's cliff formula is admirably explicit about the math and silent about the inputs. At 750 sq ft and above, the ADU pays "ADU area ÷ primary dwelling area × the standard residential fee" — a clean ratio, published in the ordinance. What the city does not publish is the standard residential fee for a new dwelling that the ratio multiplies against, so there is no dollar figure an 800 sq ft ADU can budget from the documents. This is the largest excluded unknown in the total, and the one most likely to move the number: a 1,600 sq ft primary dwelling would put the ADU at half the base rate; a 2,400 sq ft primary at a third. Settle the base amount with the city before doing any arithmetic.

## Sewer and water: the proportional rule with no numbers

The ordinance sets the policy clearly: new-construction detached ADUs need their own sewer and water connections, and the connection fees and capacity charges scale proportionally with the ADU's burden (area or fixture units). Downey's water comes from the City Water Division, its sewer from the LA County Sanitation District — and neither publishes an ADU-specific dollar figure we could find. LACSD's FY26/27 rate table lists single-family at $1,801 per parcel but has no ADU line at all; the LACSD application form has an ADU/JADU box to check, and then the price list goes quiet. Both lines stay excluded and marked "verify with city."

## Pre-approved plans: a program with no plans

Downey's AB 1332 pre-approved standard plans program exists on the Building & Safety website — page, program name, legal basis, and as of July 28, 2026, **zero registered plans**. The city was actively soliciting design professionals, which means the program is a future benefit, not a current one. Every other pre-approved-plan city in the series pairs the plans with fee certainty; Downey pairs a promise of plans with the series' most assumption-dependent permit. If the program fills its roster before the January 2027 review, this line gets rewritten.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Downey's ordinance-based proportional formula cannot survive that math unchanged — an 800 sq ft ADU would owe fees on 50 sq ft of proportional DIF, not 800, shrinking the cliff penalty to a sliver of its current size. We found no mention of SB 1117 in city materials; the January 2027 quarterly review will check for an amended Ordinance 23-1507. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and stay at $4,136 pending DUSD's resolution.
