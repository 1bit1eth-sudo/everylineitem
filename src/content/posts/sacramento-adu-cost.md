The [San Francisco post](/posts/san-francisco-adu-cost/) in this series is about a cliff that barely exists. This one is about a cliff you can measure to the dollar — because the City of Sacramento published the worksheet that proves it.

Sacramento's official ADU fee estimate prices a 750 sq ft new-construction unit at **$10,624** in city fees — **$5,347** of it three impact fees that are $0 at 749 sq ft. One square foot switches those fees on. That is not a modeling choice. Those are the city's own numbers, and they make Sacramento the mirror image of San Francisco — the city in this series where the 750 sq ft line matters most.

So this post does two things. First, the standard six-phase breakdown for an 800 sq ft detached ADU in Sacramento — **$317,000, or $396 per square foot.** Then the fee autopsy: which three fees create the cliff, the one fee that ignores it, and the city's own 747 sq ft escape hatch.

Six phases. The same six used on every build in this series.

| Phase | Cost | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft costs (design, permits, plan check) | $29,000 | 9% |

*Shares rounded.*

One honest caveat before the tables: Sacramento's agencies do not publish phase-level construction costs, and we found no public Sacramento permit-valuation dataset to anchor one. So phases two through six are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline, unchanged — we will not print a number we cannot anchor. What *is* rebuilt from Sacramento sources, line by line, is the soft-cost phase. That is where this city's story lives.

## The fee autopsy: $10,624, line by line

Sacramento publishes something most cities in this series do not: a dedicated ADU fee estimate worksheet ([CDD-0419](https://www.cityofsacramento.gov/content/dam/portal/cdd/Building/Forms/CDD-0419_Accessory-Dwelling-Unit-Fee-Estimate.pdf), revised 12 Jul 2026) with worked columns for 749, 750, and 1,200 sq ft. An 800 sq ft unit falls in the 750 sq ft column's fee band — past every exemption. Here is what that column says:

**Permit and review fees.** Building permit ($1,268.71), building plan review ($532.86), fire department fees ($730.00 — admin $176 + plan review $260 + inspection $294), planning inspection ($526), planning review ($263), public works and utilities review ($205 each) — plus residential construction tax, construction excise tax, strong-motion, debris recycling, business operations tax, technology and general plan surcharges, and the green building fee.

**The three impact fees: $5,347.14.** This is the cliff, itemized:

| Fee | 749 sq ft | 750 sq ft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| STA development impact fee | $0 | **$1,154.64** |
| Park development impact fee (non-incentive area) | $0 | **$2,617.50** |
| SAFCA flood-control development impact fee | $0 | **$1,575.00** |
| **Subtotal** | **$0** | **$5,347.14** |

**Combined city total: $10,624.40** — the 750 sq ft column of the July 2026 worksheet. Treat individual lines as a shape, not a quote — the worksheet's rates move with each revision. What is confirmed current is the structure itself: the city's [ADU Resource Center](https://adu.cityofsacramento.org/design/step-4) still states plainly that *"if the ADU is equal to or less than 749 square feet, the development impact fees are waived"* — state law ([Gov. Code § 66311.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=66311.5)) working exactly as written.

## The 750 square foot cliff, measured

In the [San Diego post](/posts/san-diego-adu-cost/), crossing 750 sq ft triggers $5,000–$8,000 in newly applicable impact fees. In the [San Francisco post](/posts/san-francisco-adu-cost/), the same crossing triggers almost nothing. Sacramento is the San Diego end of that spectrum — with receipts:

| | 749 sq ft ADU | 750 sq ft ADU | 800 sq ft ADU |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| City fees (worksheet, Jul 2026) | impact fees $0 | $10,624 | $10,624+ |
| Marginal cost of the 750th sq ft | — | **$5,347** | — |

The marginal 50 sq ft from 750 to 800 therefore costs roughly **$107 per foot in fees alone** — before a single stud is framed. In San Francisco that same 50 sq ft costs $0 in added fees. The two posts together are the series' cleanest A/B test: identical state law, opposite local fee structures.

Note the Sacramento-only ingredient: the **SAFCA flood-control fee**. Sacramento sits in a floodplain, and its fee schedule says so — $1,575 of the cliff exists because this city manages flood risk. No other city in the series prices its geography quite this literally.

## The city that dodges its own cliff

Here is the part we like best. Sacramento's [ADU Resource Center](https://adu.cityofsacramento.org/) publishes free shelf-ready designs — and the two-bedroom model is drawn at **747 sq ft**. Three square feet under the cliff. The city's own program is dimensioned to land on the $0-impact-fee side of its own worksheet.

Compare that to San Francisco, whose pre-approved plan program has published zero plans, and Escondido, which publishes four free plans with no cliff to dodge. Sacramento is the only city in the series where the pre-approved program is both real and *strategically sized* — the municipality using its own rulebook the way a good builder would.

The practical math: a 747 sq ft shelf-ready build skips the $5,347 cliff *and* the design line. Against our modeled 800 sq ft custom build, that is on the order of **$19,000** in combined fee and design savings — the single biggest lever in this post.

## The fee that ignores the cliff: schools

One line does not care about 750 sq ft. The city's worksheet lists school impact fees with no size exemption — *"Check with District (fees paid at district offices)"* — and the city's official school-fee schedule ([CDD-0226](https://www.cityofsacramento.gov/content/dam/portal/cdd/Building/Forms/CDD-0226_School-Disctrict-Impact-Fees.pdf), revised 9 Sep 2026) sets the rates:

| District (within city limits) | Residential rate |
| --- | --- |
| Sacramento City Unified (most of the city) | **$5.38/sq ft** |
| Elk Grove USD | $7.23/sq ft |
| Twin Rivers USD | $5.38/sq ft |
| Natomas USD | $5.17/sq ft |
| San Juan USD | $5.38/sq ft |
| Robla ESD + Twin Rivers (both apply) | $2.79 + $2.38/sq ft |

On our 800 sq ft unit in SCUSD territory: **$4,304** — due in full, cliff or no cliff. School developer fees sit under a separate Education Code framework from the Mitigation Fee Act impact fees that state law waives under 750 sq ft, and districts interpret the boundary differently. Sacramento's worksheet treats the fee as simply applicable. Know your district before you budget; within one city the spread is nearly 3×.

## Water, sewer, and power: the quiet lines

**City water and sewer: effectively $0.** The worksheet's own notes say ADUs *"generally connect to existing water main or tie into the line of the main dwelling unit"* — no separate connection, no separate meter, no city capacity charge.

**County sewer (SacSewer/Regional San): verifying.** The worksheet punts this one — *"check with County,"* *"paid at County offices"* — and we could not locate SacSewer's residential ADU impact-fee schedule. State law waives most special-district fees for ADUs under 750 sq ft; an 800 sq ft unit is not covered. Until confirmed, treat county sewer fees as an open line — see [Corrections](/corrections/).

**Electric (SMUD): not published.** We could not locate SMUD's ADU connection or capacity-charge policy in its published documents, so no figure is printed here. Typically the unit ties into existing service, but "typically" is not a source.

## What moves the number most in Sacramento

**1. Which side of 750 sq ft you build on.** Nothing else in this post moves the number by $5,347 for one square foot. If the program fits, 749 sq ft (or the city's 747 sq ft shelf-ready plan) is the highest-value design decision in the series so far.

**2. Your school district.** $4,304 in SCUSD territory, up to ~$5,784 in Elk Grove USD — on the same 800 sq ft box. Confirm the parcel's district with the [city's school-fee schedule](https://www.cityofsacramento.gov/content/dam/portal/cdd/Building/Forms/CDD-0226_School-Disctrict-Impact-Fees.pdf) before anything else.

**3. Design: free plan vs. custom.** The shelf-ready catalog exists and is dimensioned around the cliff. Custom design keeps the full $14,000 line; the free plan zeroes it.

**4. County sewer fees.** The one open line that could still move soft costs by four figures. We will update when SacSewer's schedule is confirmed.

**5. ADU type.** This post models new detached construction. A garage conversion bills under alteration rates with the shell already standing; a JADU is exempt from impact fees statewide and, since 2026, from school fees too.
