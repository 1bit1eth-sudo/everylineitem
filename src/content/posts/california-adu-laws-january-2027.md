Three California laws land on ADU projects on January 1, 2027. Two of them rewrite your government fee bill. The third is routinely described as an ADU fee law and is not one — it is about how long the power company may take to energize your unit, and its real deadlines arrive nine months later. This hub is the short, sourced version of all three, with links to the deep dives and to your city's page.

## AB 956 — two detached ADUs per lot, ministerially

**What it does.** From January 1, 2027, a local agency must ministerially approve **two detached ADUs** on a lot with a proposed or existing single-family dwelling (Gov. Code §66323(a)(2), as amended by Chapter 791). Each can be up to 800 sq ft with 4-foot side and rear setbacks and a 16-foot height limit (18 feet near transit). No JADU is required alongside them, and HOA bans on ADUs are further restricted.

**What it does to your bill.** Indirectly, everything: a second unit means a second run through the fee table — under the post-2027 fee rules below.

**Deep dive:** [Two Detached ADUs Per Single-Family Lot From Jan 1, 2027](/posts/ab-956-two-adus-one-lot/)

## SB 1117 — impact fees shrink to the 50 sq ft margin

**What it does.** SB 1117 (Chapter 809) adds Gov. Code §66311.5: for up to two ADUs, a local agency may assess covered impact fees **only on the area in excess of 750 square feet**, proportionate under the existing §66324 framework. An 800 sq ft ADU is billed as if it were 50.

**What it does to your bill.** Covered city/county impact fees collapse — in a worked proportional example, fees fall from ~$10,477 to ~$40. Every jurisdiction page in our [fee index](/fees/) now carries a "What changes on January 1, 2027" block with the same summary.

**What it does not touch.** School developer fees are not covered impact fees — Folsom's $8,208 Level II school fee is identical on January 2. Connection and capacity charges for water and sewer are expressly excluded (§66311.5(c)(2)). Charter cities are covered; a third ADU on the lot is billed the old way.

**Deep dive:** [SB 1117 Kills the 750 Sq Ft Cliff](/posts/sb-1117-kills-750-sq-ft-cliff/)

## SB 1196 — the utility law that is not a fee law

**What it actually is.** SB 1196 (McNerney) amends Public Utilities Code §931 and adds §934.5. The enrolled text contains no ADU provision at all — an earlier 2026 version was ADU-specific, but June 29 Assembly amendments generalized it to **"small energization projects"**: service requests that need no distribution or service-line extension or upgrade, including projects that need only a new meter. Most ADU hookups are exactly that.

**What it does.** It orders the CPUC to establish, **by September 30, 2027**, processing deadlines for energization applications, rules for notifying applicants of defects, and penalties for utility non-compliance. Energization applications may be filed alongside the building permit application, and a utility may not cancel one without the applicant's consent. Section 934.5(d)(5) requires utilities to give applicants their applicable fees and any upgrade-cost estimates up front — a transparency requirement that changes what you are *told*, not what you are *charged*.

**Why it is on this page.** Because "2027 ADU laws" roundups keep listing it next to the fee bills, and the difference matters for budgeting: the law takes effect January 1, 2027, but the deadlines and penalties that could actually shorten a hookup wait do not exist until the CPUC proceeding concludes, due September 30, 2027. Plan 2027 projects on the old energization timelines; treat anything faster as a bonus.
