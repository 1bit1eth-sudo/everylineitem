> **DRAFT — not for publication until reviewed.**

An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Redwood City costs at least **$306,352, or about $383 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — and anyone researching Redwood City ADU fees will notice something unusual about this stack: the city publishes not one but two per-square-foot ADU rates, one for sewer and one for water, both traceable to the same 2024 ordinance and both still standing in the FY 2026-2027 schedule. The building permit is the missing piece, not the impact fees, which the city has temporarily zeroed for ADUs. What follows prices only what the documents price, and names everything else as uncounted.

## The sewer capacity fee comes first, because it is the anchor

The Wastewater Collection Capacity Fee in the FY 2026-2027 Master Fee Schedule, effective July 1, 2026, lists an Accessory Dwelling Unit line at $1.78 per square foot. The footnote to that line states that ADU fees were calculated from the average square footage of an existing single-family dwelling unit in Redwood City, which is 1,780 livable square feet, and that ADUs will be charged capacity fees in accordance with Government Code section 65852.2(f)(5). The same schedule carries the authority as Ordinance 2533, adopted January 22, 2024, and the municipal code history for the sewer capacity section shows no later amendment — so the FY 2026-2027 schedule itself is the evidence that no newer rate has replaced it. This is the fee recorded as FI-244, and it is confirmed unchanged for FY 2026-27 rather than merely carried forward from 2024.

| Counted fee (Redwood City, 800 sq ft) | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Sewer collection capacity fee: 800 sq ft × $1.78 | **$1,424** |
| Water capacity fee: 800 sq ft × $3.19 | **$2,552** |
| Additional Address for ADU (Planning) | **$376** |
| **Counted fees, total** | **$4,352** |
| Transportation, parks, and housing impact fees | $0 (temporary ADU exemption, repealed Apr 1, 2027) |

Two conditions ride along with that table. First, the water capacity fee is collected when a water meter is requested, so a shared-meter ADU may be charged differently; the $2,552 is counted because the schedule publishes an explicit ADU rate, but the meter question is open and appears again below. Second, the $376 address fee assumes a separate address is assigned to the detached unit, which is the normal case for a detached ADU; if the city assigns no separate address, the floor falls by $376. Both assumptions are stated here rather than buried, so either correction is a subtraction, not a rebuild.

> **The 750 sq ft rule, and where the capacity fees sit inside it.** California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. Redwood City's schedule does something narrower and more specific with its capacity fees: it prices ADU sewer and water capacity as flat per-square-foot lines and anchors them to section 65852.2(f)(5), not to the proportional rule in (f)(3)(A). Whether SB 1117 (Government Code section 66311.5, effective January 1, 2027), which changes how impact fees apply above 750 square feet, reaches those per-square-foot capacity lines is not stated in the schedule, so it is flagged verify rather than asserted either way — and SB 1117 is a future rule only, so it changes nothing in this 2026 floor. Separately, the city's own temporary exemption already zeroes the transportation, parks, and housing impact fees for ADUs, but only until April 1, 2027. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The permit valuation.** This is the largest uncounted line and the one with a published starting point that still is not a total. The city's $5,321.71 base estimate for 601–800 square feet is real, but the city warns that valuation-based additions follow case by case, and the schedule's add-ons (plan check at 50% of the permit fee, the 13% technology fee, and the 0.39% General Plan Maintenance Fee) all scale with a valuation the schedule does not publish for an ADU. Get a contractor valuation first; the permit follows from it mechanically, and the $5,321.71 will turn out to be the floor of the permit bill rather than the bill itself.

**2. The water meter question.** The $2,552 water capacity fee is the single most reversible line in the counted stack. The schedule collects water fees when a meter is requested, and nothing in the schedule states whether an ADU on the primary dwelling's existing meter triggers the ADU rate. Ask the Engineering division before budgeting: a shared-meter answer removes $2,552, a separate-meter answer confirms it, and either answer is cheaper to get now than after permit issuance.

**3. Two dates that change the fee stack after this post.** April 1, 2027 repeals the Resolution 16210 exemption, so an ADU permitted after that date may owe transportation, parks, and housing impact fees that this post prices at $0 — and January 1, 2027 brings SB 1117 into effect, whose treatment of the per-square-foot capacity fees is still unverified. [ADU school fees, explained](/posts/adu-school-fees-explained/) covers why a statewide cap cannot stand in for a district rate, which is also why this post counts no school fee: the district rate for a specific Redwood City parcel must come from the district, not from a statewide table. Confirm the district, the meter, and the permit timing together, and the floor converts into a budget by addition.

## Redwood City vs. Los Angeles County

The series baseline is shared, so the comparison isolates local fees rather than construction costs. Our published LA County cities price the same 800 sq ft detached ADU between $313,000 and $320,000, with the spread driven by permits, impact fees, school fees, and utility charges rather than by framing or finishes.

| 800 sq ft detached ADU | Total | What drives the difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Redwood City (verified floor) | **$306,352** | $4,352 counted fees; $0 impact fees; permit excluded |
| Long Beach (LA County) | $313,000 | Reduced ADU impact-fee rates; modest permit fees |
| Burbank (LA County) | $320,000 | Highest permit-plus-plan-check in the LA County set |

Redwood City sits below the LA County range, but the comparison is deliberately uneven: Redwood City's figure is missing its entire permit bill, while the LA County figures include theirs. Add only the city's $5,321.71 base estimate — itself incomplete — and Redwood City reaches $311,674, still below the range, with the valuation-based additions and the school fee still missing. The honest reading is not that Redwood City is cheap; it is that Redwood City's countable fee stack is small, its permit is the open question, and its $0 impact fees have an expiry date.

How to use this floor in practice: treat $306,352 as the number below which the project should not be budgeted, confirm the water meter treatment and the school district with the city and district before permit issuance, obtain a contractor valuation and run it through the schedule's valuation bands for the permit, and diary April 1, 2027, because the impact-fee exemption that makes this stack small does not survive that date. When the city and district answer, the floor converts into a budget by addition, not by revision. That is the point of keeping guesses out of the total in the first place.

## What this post does not claim

No complete building permit figure is claimed, because the city's $5,321.71 is labeled an estimate with valuation-based additions to come, and the schedule publishes no ADU valuation. No school fee is claimed, because no district rate was verified from a district source in this round, and the city itself routes that question to the district. No water service-line or meter installation figure is claimed, because the schedule refers those to the Public Works schedule without an ADU price. No wastewater treatment capacity fee is claimed for the ADU, because the schedule's per-unit treatment rates carry no ADU line and mark the in-law line not applicable to ADUs. No SB 1117 effect on the capacity fees is claimed, because the schedule does not state it. No design saving is claimed from the free plans, because site-specific design is still required. Readers who want the background on plan programs generally should read [California's free ADU plans](/posts/free-adu-plans-california/) and then confirm availability and price with the city before assuming any saving.
