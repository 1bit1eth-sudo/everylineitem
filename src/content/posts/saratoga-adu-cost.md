An 800 sq ft detached ADU in Saratoga costs at least **$304,128, or about $380 per square foot**, on verified figures alone — a total that looks thin only until you see what it refuses to include. Anyone researching Saratoga ADU fees will find a city tax of a dollar a square foot, a school bill split across two districts, and a building permit that cannot be priced from the schedule at all. This post counts the first two lines it can prove, names the rest as open, and stops there.

## The tax and the split school bill

Saratoga's counted fee stack has exactly two lines, and each one comes with a boundary that matters more than its size. The city construction tax is straightforward: the adopted fee schedule effective July 1, 2026 charges $1.00 per square foot of floor area for a building or structure, so an 800 sq ft ADU owes $800. The school fee is the opposite of straightforward, because it arrives in pieces. The Los Gatos-Saratoga Union High School District's February 2026 Developer Fee Justification Study sets its share at 30.77% of the statewide residential rate, giving $1.66 per square foot, and the district's developer-fees page confirms $1.66 per square foot as of June 15, 2026. On 800 sq ft that is $1,328.

| Counted fee (Saratoga, 800 sq ft) | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| City construction tax: 800 sq ft × $1.00 | **$800** |
| LGSUHSD high-school developer fee: 800 sq ft × $1.66 | **$1,328** |
| **Counted fees, total** | **$2,128** |
| Elementary feeder-district developer fee | excluded — verify with district |
| Building permit + plan check | not counted (valuation-based) |
| Proportional city impact fees | not counted — verify with city |

The split is where a careless total would go wrong. The high-school district's page states that separate developer fees are assessed by the high-school district and by the elementary feeder district, and that for properties within Saratoga Union School District boundaries, Saratoga USD collects fees on behalf of the high-school district. It also lists three other feeder districts, so which elementary district applies depends on the parcel's district, and this post does not assume every City of Saratoga parcel sits in Saratoga USD. No Saratoga USD rate was located on an official Saratoga USD source in this round, so the elementary share is left out entirely: a combined school total would require a number this research does not have. Note also the 500 sq ft threshold in the school-fee scheme — fees apply to residential construction above that size, so an 800 sq ft ADU is squarely in fee territory, while a sub-500 sq ft unit would sit outside it.

## The ADU fee rules, in plain terms

California's ADU fee statute, Government Code section 65852.2(f)(3)(A), bars any impact fee on an ADU smaller than 750 square feet, and requires that an ADU of 750 square feet or more be charged only in proportion to the primary dwelling's square footage. Saratoga's own ADU page states the same rule in its impact-fee row. At 800 sq ft, this unit is past the exemption line, which is exactly why the proportional city impact fees are left uncounted: the proportion needs the primary dwelling's size, and no such figure is published for this unit. SB 1117, Government Code section 66311.5, effective January 1, 2027, will change how that proration works for impact fees, but it is a future rule only at the time of this draft, and it does not touch school developer fees, which sit under a separate statute. For the full explanation of how the cliff works, see [The 750 sq ft cliff](/posts/750-sq-ft-cliff-explained/).

## Three things that can still move the number

**1. The permit valuation.** This is the largest uncounted line and the only one that can be resolved with a single document: a valuation built from the International Code Council data with the schedule's 2.9 modifier, as the Building Official applies it. The band formulas then give the permit mechanically, plan check follows at 66%, and the General Plan Update Fee follows at 0.285% of valuation. Get the valuation first; the permit follows from it, not from a guess.

**2. The elementary school share and the parcel's district.** The high-school district's page names Saratoga USD as the collecting entity for Saratoga USD parcels, but it also lists other feeder districts, so the collecting district depends on the parcel's district. Until Saratoga USD's own rate is confirmed from an official Saratoga USD source, the school bill in this post is half a bill by design. Confirm the parcel's district and the elementary rate before paying anything, because the payment procedure runs through the collecting district.

**3. The proportional impact fees and the utility lines.** The schedule's park fee is stated per unit, not per ADU, so its ADU share cannot be computed without the primary dwelling's size. Utility connection and capacity charges likewise have no verified ADU figure in this round. When the city answers on both, those lines join the floor by addition. Readers who want the background on plan programs generally should read [California's free ADU plans](/posts/free-adu-plans-california/) and then confirm availability and price with the city before assuming any saving, since no Saratoga city plan program was located.

## What this post does not claim

No building permit, plan check, General Plan Update Fee, or technology surcharge figure is claimed, because the schedule prices those by valuation or actual cost and publishes no ADU valuation. No elementary school fee figure is claimed, because no Saratoga USD rate was located on an official Saratoga USD source. No combined school total is claimed, because combining a verified high-school share with an unverified elementary share would manufacture a number. No proportional park or other impact-fee figure is claimed, because the ADU share requires the primary dwelling's size. No utility figure is claimed, because none could be verified. No pre-approved plan saving is claimed, because no city program was found. No construction-tax waiver is claimed for a market-rate unit, and no waiver of the construction tax is claimed even for a deed-restricted unit, because the published waiver covers permit fees. No SB 1117 effect is claimed for the current total, because SB 1117 takes effect January 1, 2027.
