Santa Clara is the most transparent permit counter in this series. **Plan review $2,488.80. Permit inspection $2,309.70. Permit issuance $101.** All three are flat fees from a scaled schedule that prices ADUs by size band — our 800 sq ft example sits in the 251–1,000 sq ft band — and every line is named, priced and sourced. Add the $594.53 General Plan surcharge, $352 in trade permits and the $306 planning fee, and the whole permit column comes to $6,152.03 with nothing left to guess at. Our six-phase total lands at **$313,000, or $391 per square foot**.

The other half of the story is what is not there. Water and electric connection and capacity charges for an ADU are **$0**. There are **no impact-fee lines at all** — the sanitary sewer outlet charge ($1.14 per square foot, $912) is a utility conveyance fee, not an impact fee. The school fee is the state maximum $5.38 per square foot from a single district, $4,304. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Santa Clara does not publish phase-level construction costs.

## A scaled permit schedule, fully itemised

Most cities in this series price the permit as a percentage of valuation or a single flat fee, and the homeowner has to take the number on trust. Santa Clara publishes a scaled schedule that prices by size band, and our 800 sq ft example falls in the 251–1,000 sq ft band: plan review $2,488.80 and permit inspection $2,309.70. Those two lines alone are $4,798.50 — more than the entire permit column in most cities we have published. Add permit issuance at $101 and the permit-plus-plan-check subtotal is $4,899.50.

The rest of the permit column is itemised the same way. The General Plan / Advanced Planning surcharge is $594.53. Trade permits — electrical, mechanical and plumbing for new residential — are $352 flat. The planning fee for an ADU or JADU is $306. Nothing is a percentage of valuation, nothing is "to be determined at the counter", and nothing is hidden inside a larger line. The whole permit-side total is $6,152.03, and every dollar of it is traceable to a named line in the fee schedule.

## Water and electric: $0

The schedule lists water and electric connection and capacity charges for an ADU at **$0**. That is not a waiver and not an exemption: it is the priced line. State law (Gov. Code §66311.5) already bars a city from requiring a new or separate water or sewer connection for an ADU on an existing single-family lot, so the example shares the existing service — but Santa Clara goes further and prices the ADU connection and capacity at zero in its own schedule.

The sewer side is the one place Santa Clara does charge, and it is worth reading carefully. The sanitary sewer outlet charge is $1.14 per square foot — $912 on 800 sq ft — and it applies only to ADUs of 750 sq ft or more. It is a conveyance fee, categorised as utility capacity, not an impact fee. That matters for 2027: connection and capacity charges are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition, so this $912 does not change under the new rule.

## No impact fees at all

We found no impact-fee lines for an ADU in Santa Clara's fee schedule — no DIF, no parks, no traffic, no per-square-foot impact charge. That is the second city in this series with a clean sheet on impact fees (Beverly Hills is the other), and it has a practical consequence: SB 1117, which only changes impact fees, has nothing to change here. The 750 sq ft cliff still exists in state law, and the sewer outlet charge still starts at 750 sq ft, but there is no impact-fee saving to compute for 2027.

The school fee fills part of the gap the missing impact fees leave. Santa Clara Unified charges Level I at $5.38 per square foot — the state maximum — $4,304 on 800 sq ft. Santa Clara is a single-district city, so there is no split to track and no second district to reconcile. The 500 sq ft cliff (SB 543) means the full area is levied either way at our size.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Santa Clara has no verified impact-fee lines, so SB 1117 changes nothing we can compute here — there is nothing to reduce. The sanitary sewer outlet charge ($912) is a utility capacity charge, outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition, and it does not change either. See the full comparison at [Santa Clara: 2026 vs 2027, line by line](/sb-1117/santa-clara/). School fees, permit fees and water/electric charges are outside SB 1117 and do not change.
