## Three fees homeowners most often get wrong

**1. Multiplying the full park fee by 800.** The schedule's $7.65 per sq ft park fee and $1.64 growth fee are the rates for a new house, not an ADU. An 800 sq ft ADU does not owe 800 × $7.65 = $6,120 in park fees. Under state law it owes a share proportional to the primary dwelling: an 800 sq ft ADU behind a 1,600 sq ft house pays roughly half the full rate; behind a 2,400 sq ft house, a third. The city publishes no ADU formula of its own, so the payable figure needs the main house's size and a city calculation. From January 1, 2027, SB 1117 narrows the taxable area further, to the square footage above 750, for a homeowner's first two ADUs.

**2. Treating the state school-fee ceiling as the district's rate.** The State Allocation Board's $5.38 per sq ft is a maximum, not a levy. South Pasadena Unified has to adopt its own rate, and no adopted rate could be found on the district's site. Publishing $4,304 for an 800 sq ft ADU would dress a ceiling up as a bill. The one school-fee fact that is solid is the exemption line: ADUs of 500 sq ft or less pay $0 under SB 543, and an 800 sq ft ADU sits above it — so a fee will apply, at an unknown rate.

**3. Counting water deposits as costs.** The schedule's $9,147 water service installation and $1,200 meter installation are explicitly deposit-based. Deposits come back or settle against actual charges; they are not fees, and adding $10,347 to a budget as if it were a sunk cost overstates the project by more than the entire soft-cost phase. The same caution applies to the schedule's technology and general-plan surcharges, whose units were ambiguous in extraction and are not computed here.

## The permit that runs through another government

South Pasadena is a contract city for building services, and its schedule is honest about it: item 89 sends you to LA County. The county, in turn, says fees are based on valuation and that no estimated cost can be provided without one. That chain — city schedule to county table to a valuation nobody publishes for this project type — is why the permit line stays blank. It is also why the $633 ministerial review matters more than its size suggests: it is the only ADU-specific, fixed, city-published fee in the whole schedule, and it is the only fee line in the total besides design.

## Pre-approved plans: announced, not available

The city's planning news page announces pre-approved ADU plans as coming soon. Draft plans went to the Planning Commission on July 14, 2026, and a handout circulated at the October 17, 2026 community fair. But an announcement is not a program: no plans are downloadable, no prices are stated, and no fee waiver is published. Until the program launches, the $14,000 series-baseline design figure stands, and no design savings should be budgeted.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 1, 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. South Pasadena's proportional formula cannot survive that math unchanged — an 800 sq ft ADU would owe fees on 50 sq ft of proportional impact, not 800, shrinking the cliff penalty to a sliver of its current size. School fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition and stay at whatever rate the district adopts. The January 2027 quarterly review will check for a city-published ADU formula and a district rate.
