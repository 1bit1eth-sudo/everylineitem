Until now, a California ADU has been a rental-only asset. You could build it, live in it, rent it out — but you could not sell it apart from the house. AB 1033 changed the state law to allow it. Alameda is now moving to make it real.

## What happened

- **Sep 15, 2026:** Alameda City Council voted **4-0** (one recusal) to introduce an ordinance implementing AB 1033, allowing ADUs to be sold as separate condominiums.
- **Oct 6, 2026 (scheduled):** Final adoption on the consent calendar. Consent items almost always pass as a package — but we do not state adoption as fact until the published minutes confirm it.
- **Effective:** 30 days after adoption, so the first applications would land in early November 2026.

If confirmed, Alameda becomes one of the first Bay Area cities to implement AB 1033 — a small island city moving faster than Los Angeles, whose own "missing middle" housing package is still in hearings.

## What AB 1033 actually does

AB 1033 (2024) is an **opt-in** state law. It does not force any city to do anything. What it does:

- Authorizes cities to adopt ordinances allowing ADUs to be **sold separately** from the primary residence as condominium units (airspace subdivision).
- Requires the ADU to remain subject to all existing **size, design, and development standards** — no zoning change, no density bonus, no height exception.
- Typically requires **HOA formation** with CC&Rs governing shared spaces (driveway, utilities, yard) — even for a two-unit property.

What it does not do: create a right to sell without local action. No ordinance, no sale. That is why Alameda's move matters — it is the local action that activates the state permission.

## The cost ledger nobody headlines

"Sell your ADU" makes a great headline. The costs of actually doing it do not:

| Conversion cost item | Typical range | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Airspace subdivision / parcel map survey | $5,000–$15,000 | Licensed surveyor; varies by lot complexity |
| HOA documents + CC&Rs (legal) | $3,000–$10,000 | Real estate attorney; DRE compliance if applicable |
| Condo plan recordation | $1,000–$3,000 | County recorder fees |
| Ongoing HOA administration | $500–$2,000/yr | Even a two-unit HOA needs books and insurance |

These are third-party market estimates, not Alameda-published figures — the city has published no conversion fee schedule. Treat the headline sale price (one estimate put a 750 sq ft Alameda backyard condo near $580,000 against a $1.42M typical home) the way we treat every number on this site: subtract the costs before you celebrate.

City staff expect only a **single-digit annual increase** in ADU production from the ordinance. This is an exit-path reform, not a production boom.

## Why it matters for ADU economics

Every ADU cost post on this site prices the build as a rental asset — payback through rent, 10–20 year horizons. Condo sale creates a second exit:

- **Rental exit:** $320,000 build → ~$2,400/mo rent → ~11-year simple payback.
- **Sale exit (new):** $320,000 build + ~$15,000 conversion → sell the ADU as a condo, recover capital immediately, keep the main house.

The sale exit only works where the math works: high land values, strong starter-home demand, and a buyer pool for a 750 sq ft condo. Alameda — island city, median home $1.42M — is close to the ideal test case.
