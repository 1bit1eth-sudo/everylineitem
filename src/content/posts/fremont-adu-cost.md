Fremont waives everything the city controls — and then hands you the biggest single fee line in the post, issued by an agency that isn't the city. The City of Fremont exempts **all ADUs, of any size**, from all five of its development impact fee programs: parkland acquisition, park facilities, traffic impact, fire facilities and equipment, other civic facilities. The 750 sq ft cliff is moot, because there is no fee on either side of it. Santa Monica (published earlier in this series) exempted four programs for all sizes; Fremont goes one further and waives five. Our six-phase total lands at **$314,000, or $393 per square foot** — just under the LA County baseline.

But the waiver stops at the city line. Fremont's sewer provider is the Union Sanitary District — a tri-city special district — and it publishes an ADU capacity fee of **$5.09 per square foot**: **$4,072** on 800, plus a $350 permit/inspection subtotal. That one district charge is larger than the school fee and larger than the building permit estimate combined, and it is the honest punchline of the post: the exemption city still bills you through its sewer district. Water, by contrast, is $0 from the Alameda County Water District for an ADU that doesn't install a separate line. Construction phases 2–6 are carried from the Los Angeles County baseline; Fremont does not publish phase-level construction costs. HCD logged 372 ADU permits here in 2022–2024, 43rd statewide.

## $0: the blanket exemption

The city's adopted 2023-2031 Housing Element — an official city document, not a handout — states it outright: "Fremont waives development impact fees for ADUs." All five programs: parkland acquisition, park facilities, traffic impact, fire facilities and equipment, other civic facilities. The Housing Element's own prototype table for an 800 sq ft ADU shows DIF **$0** next to building fees of ~$4,000, a school fee, and utility lines. This is the most ADU-friendly DIF posture anywhere in the matrix: Santa Monica exempts four programs for all sizes; Fremont exempts five. There is no 750 sq ft cliff for city impact fees because there is no city impact fee — the threshold is moot, and the state baseline (no fees under 750, proportional above) only frames what the city could have charged and chose not to. For comparison, the same prototype table puts the city's total per-unit estimate at $14,160 (DIF $0 + Building ~$4,000 + FUSD $3,264 at the then-rate + ACWD $0 + USD $3,776 at $4.72/sq ft).

## $4,072: the sewer district that didn't get the memo

The city's waiver ends where the city's jurisdiction ends. Fremont's sewer service comes from the Union Sanitary District, a special district serving Fremont, Newark, and Union City — and USD publishes an **ADU capacity fee of $5.09 per square foot**: **$4,072** on an 800 sq ft ADU, the single largest fee line in this post. The asterisk is about currency, not existence: the rate is district-published, but its stated effective period (August 1, 2022 through July 31, 2023) has lapsed, and we could not find a current rate on unionsanitary.ca.gov. The $350 permit/inspection subtotal ($50 permit, $150 inspection property-line-to-building, $150 inspection main-to-property-line) is current page content, and the $500 refundable deposit sits outside the total. The structural lesson for homeowners: in Fremont, the city waived five fee programs and the sewer district kept one — and the district's one is bigger than the city's five.

## $3,832: the school fee from 2022

Fremont Unified School District levies developer fees on new residential construction including additions of 500+ sq ft, so an 800 sq ft ADU pays — **$4.79 per square foot (Level 1), $3,832** on 800. The asterisk: the rate comes from the city's 2023-2031 Housing Element with a May 23, 2022 effective date, and it may be superseded. The State Allocation Board has raised the Level 1 maximum twice since (to $5.17 in January 2024 and $5.38 in January 2026); multiple searches for a current FUSD-published rate or post-2022 board resolution surfaced only the 2020 resolution (Level 2 $5.07 / Level 3 $10.82, one-year effectiveness) and the Housing Element's $4.79. So $3,832 is the latest verifiable figure — and if FUSD has since adopted the higher maximum, the true number is higher. (For reference: the Housing Element's own prototype used $4.08, or $3,264 — already dated at publication.)

## ~$4,000: the city's own prototype, and what backs it

Fremont doesn't price ADU permits flat, and it doesn't publish a per-square-foot valuation for ADUs — but it does something most cities don't: the adopted Housing Element prices a prototype. Tables 5-11 through 5-14 put "Building Fees" at **~$4,000** for an 800 sq ft ADU on roughly a 2022 basis, and the rate structure behind it is fully confirmed: building permit $664.47 for the first $25,000 of valuation plus $10.34 per additional $1,000, plan check at 100% of the permit fee, a Community Planning Fee at 15% of the permit fee, a $0.76-per-square-foot construction tax on additions, and SMIP at valuation × 0.00013. We carry the ~$4,000 as the city's estimate, marked — not a quote. One concrete alternative inside the system: the Preapproved ADU Program offers a **flat $1,000 plan review deposit** with 7-business-day review (versus 15 days standard), which may be the cheapest review path in the city.

## The preapproved program in reset

Fremont runs a real Preapproved ADU Program for detached ADUs — program terms confirmed on fremont.gov — but it is mid-reset: all plans preapproved under the 2022 code cycle became invalid on **January 1, 2026**, and the city is re-preapproving plans under the 2025 California codes, adding updated plans to the page as they're approved. The fetched page text did not render the current plan list, so the number of usable plans as of today is unverified — a third-party aggregator suggested only one updated plan was valid as of September 2026, which we treat as unverified and do not count. Terms: 7-business-day review, flat $1,000 plan review fee, but the homeowner must contract with the plan's designer and still submit site-specific plans, studies, and engineering. Design and engineering stays at the $14,000 series baseline.

## A note on SB 1117

Signed September 2026, effective January 2027: for a homeowner's first two ADUs, impact fees may apply only to square footage above 750, calculated marginally. Fremont has nothing to recalculate — its city DIF is already $0 for ADUs of every size, and the January 2027 review will simply confirm the exemption still stands. The USD sewer capacity fee is a special-district charge, not a city development impact fee, and school fees are outside SB 1117's impact-fee definition — neither moves. We found no mention of SB 1117 in city materials.
