This is the first post in our national series, and it is not a build estimate. Every other post on this site prices one plan in one place across the same six phases. This one does something different: it takes the five wildest government-fee findings from our 148-city national dataset — the top three most populous cities in every state, researched line by line from official sources — and puts them side by side.

The through line is the one this site keeps finding: the building permit is rarely the story. The story is the stack.

## 1. Montgomery County, Maryland — the $59,854 square foot

Maryland state law ([Land Use §4–506(A)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/Statutes/landuse)) prohibits any development impact fee on an ADU **under 750 square feet**. At exactly 750, the exemption ends — and Montgomery County's published impact taxes kick in at up to **$31,301 per unit for schools plus $28,553 for transportation: $59,854 combined** ([county impact tax handout, effective July 1, 2025](https://assets.montgomerycountymd.gov/files/Impact_Taxes_Handout_07-01-2025.pdf)).

Two honest caveats, because the number needs them. First, that is an **upper bound**: the county publishes the per-dwelling-unit single-family-detached rate and no ADU-specific category, and §4–506(B) requires any fee at 750 sq ft to be assessed proportionately rather than as a de facto prohibition — a proration the county has not publicly quantified for ADUs. Second, even the building permit is dwarfed: the school impact tax alone is **52 to 54 times** the county's $575.90 residential building permit fee for the same structure. The permit is the cheapest line item by two orders of magnitude.

## 2. Portland, Oregon — the $12,028 park fee that can be $0

Portland's parks system development charge on a 750 sq ft ADU is **$12,028** (the "700–1,199 sq ft" residential bracket, non–Central City) — about **seven times** the city's transportation SDC of $1,762 for the same unit ([Parks SDC schedule, FY26–27](https://portland.gov/parks/documents/fy26-27-sdc-schedule/download)).

And then the waiver: Portland's [ADU SDC Waiver Program](https://WWW.PORTLAND.GOV/ppd/residential-permitting/adu-sdc-waiver) wipes **all** SDCs — transportation, parks, water, environmental services — to **$0** if the owner records a covenant barring short-term rental of the ADU and the house for **10 years**. Break the covenant and the city can claw back 150% of the current rates. One recorded document moves roughly $14,000 off the invoice. Few American cities offer a swing that large for a signature.

For completeness: Portland does charge a school construction excise tax of **$1.70 per square foot** ($1,275 on 750 sq ft), and water Bureau SDC is $0 if the ADU shares the existing meter — $5,483 only if you upsize to a new 5/8" meter.

## 3. Memphis, Tennessee — where our 750 sq ft benchmark is illegal

Every post in our California series prices a 750 sq ft ADU. In Memphis, that unit **cannot be permitted** on a standard lot. The Unified Development Code ([Sec. 2.7.3](https://www.shelbycountytn.gov/DocumentCenter/View/13392/ZTA-13-003-Complete-UDC?bidId=)) caps ADUs at **700 sq ft** — 1,000 only in rural RE/CA districts — and bars them outright on lots under 10,000 sq ft.

The fee stack that remains is instructive for the opposite reason. The building permit for a 750 sq ft dwelling is **$125** (the $0.07/sq ft math never escapes the minimum) plus a $50 application fee — among the cheapest in our dataset, 10 to 40 times less than comparable California cities. But the sewer development fee is **$3,383** for a 3/4" meter — **27 times the permit** — assessed only when the project needs an additional or enlarged water meter ([Ordinance 5793, Sec. 33-62](https://memphistn.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Ord.-5793_SUO.pdf)). Share the existing meter and it is $0. In Memphis the meter decision, not the permit counter, is the fee event. Tennessee, notably, has no statewide school developer fee at all.

## 4. Cheyenne vs. Gillette, Wyoming — the same state, 196 times apart

Wyoming gives us the cleanest same-state comparison in the dataset. In **Cheyenne**, the Board of Public Utilities charges a water System Development Fee of **$11,750** for a 3/4" meter ([BOPU FY 2026 budget, adopted April 2025, effective October 1, 2025](https://www.cheyennecity.org/files/sharedassets/public/v/1/your-government/city-council/wscow-2025/bopu-fy-2026-proposed-budget-doc.pdf)) — a single utility capacity charge larger than the city's entire building-permit, plan-review, and impact-fee subtotal combined. Roughly 80% of Cheyenne's total government cost on our benchmark is water and sewer charges.

Ninety minutes north in **Gillette**, the building permit for the same 750 sq ft structure is a flat **$60** (the 500–1,000 sq ft band), and residential plan review is **$0** — "no charge for plan reviews" for one- and two-family ([Gillette Code Ch. 5, §5-IV-1](https://gillettewy.novusagenda.com/AgendaPublic/AttachmentViewer.ashx?AttachmentID=28744&ItemID=12804)). Gillette's permit is about **19 times** cheaper than Cheyenne's valuation-based equivalent of $1,162. Same state, same structure, same year.

## 5. The school-fee crown: Elk Grove, California — $7.32 per square foot

California's developer-fee system produces the highest school fees in the dataset. **Elk Grove Unified** (Sacramento County) levies **$7.32 per square foot** under Level 2 — $5,490 on a 750 sq ft ADU — adopted June 24, 2025 ([Resolution No. 97 with the April 2025 School Facilities Needs Analysis](https://d16k74nzx9emoe.cloudfront.net/e9f32b60-85c2-4391-92fb-fdf7409fbeec/2025-School-Facilities-Needs-Analysis.pdf)). That is 1.36 times the 2026 Level 1 statutory cap of $5.38.

The highest figure on paper anywhere is Dublin Unified's **$7.36** — but its 2023 adoption capped effectiveness at one year and we could not find a public re-adoption, so we are not calling it current. Elk Grove's $7.32 is the confirmed current high. For scale, that single fee exceeds the *entire* building-permit bill in most of the 148 cities we researched.

## The pattern

Put the five side by side and the pattern is the one California taught us, writ national: nobody's ADU budget is broken by the building permit. It is broken — or saved — by the stack around it: a state-law threshold at 749 vs. 750, a covenant that erases $14,000, a zoning cap that makes the benchmark illegal, a water meter charge bigger than everything else combined, a school district line that costs more than some cities' entire fee schedule.

That is why this site prices the whole stack, line by line, in every city. The 148-city dataset behind this post is the bench we will keep drawing from — one place at a time, same as always.

## What we could not confirm

- **Montgomery County ADU proration:** how the county applies the per-unit SFD impact-tax rate to a 750 sq ft ADU under §4–506(B)'s proportionality rule. Not published; the $59,854 is the upper bound.
- **Dublin Unified $7.36:** whether the district re-adopted Level 2 fees after the 2023 resolution's one-year effectiveness window. Not found in public documents.
- **Portland waiver processing time:** the covenant must be recorded before permit issuance; we did not verify current processing timelines with PP&D.
