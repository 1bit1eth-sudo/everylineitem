Two school districts tax the same 800 sq ft ADU in Campbell, and together they charge **$5.40 per square foot** — $4,320 total, the highest school-fee rate in this series. Campbell Union (K-8) takes $3.79; Campbell Union High takes $1.61. One parcel, two resolutions, one combined bill. Pasadena, by contrast, charges $2.24. The same ADU, the same state, a $2,528 school-fee gap drawn entirely by district boundaries.

Campbell sits in the heart of Silicon Valley, and its fee structure reads like it: valuation-based permits (the city's cut scales with what you build), per-fixture-unit sewer math from a separate sanitation district, and a county-level pre-approved plan gallery instead of a city-run program. Our six-phase total lands at **about $310,000, or roughly $388 per square foot, when soft costs are taken at their schedule caps** — with the sewer line carrying the widest uncertainty in the series.

## Why Campbell stands out

**The $5.40 school fee.** Two districts, two fee resolutions, one parcel. Campbell Union School District charges $3.79/sq ft for residential ADUs over 750 sq ft; Campbell Union High School District charges $1.61. Combined: $5.40 — against Pasadena's $2.24 and the $5.38 tier (Long Beach, Torrance, Anaheim, Fullerton) that used to be the series ceiling. Campbell breaks the ceiling by two cents and a second district. Verify your parcel's district: parts of Campbell fall under Cambrian or Moreland, with different rates.

**Valuation-based permits with a construction tax.** Campbell's FY 2027 schedule (effective July 1, 2026) prices building permits at 2.00% of the first $500,000 of valuation, plus 33% for plan check, plus small add-ons (General Plan maintenance 0.16%, technology 0.04%, training 0.02%). On a $100,000 valuation that core is $2,000 + $660 + $160 + $40 + $20 = **$2,880**; Title 24 plan check ($237), seismic ($13), and SB 1473 ($4) add $254, for $3,134 in permit lines. On top: a **construction license tax of $0.50 per square foot** — $400 on our ADU, a line item most cities bury or skip. Permit-related lines total $3,534. The earlier version of this post showed $3,529 for the permit example and $3,929 all-in; both figures are withdrawn — their $649 remainder did not reconcile with the schedule's published add-ons. Note also that the FY 2026 schedule's cover resolution was #13333, not #13279 as previously cited, and the city's site now lists only the FY 2026-27 schedule; the FY 2026 document link is no longer live.

**The sewer district problem.** Campbell's sewer isn't the city's — it's West Valley Sanitation District, with its own board, its own rate schedule, and per-fixture-unit math. $567.68 per drainage fixture unit (capped at $6,727) plus $86.42 per unit of treatment capacity (capped at $1,024). A typical 800 sq ft ADU with one full bath and a kitchen won't hit the caps, but the meter is running per fixture in a way no flat-fee city replicates. WVSD's permit must be in hand before the city issues the building permit — a sequencing detail that has delayed projects. [To verify: the district's current published schedule could not be located; these rates are carried from FY 2024-25 figures.]

**The county gallery.** Instead of a city-run pre-approved plan program, Campbell points to the Santa Clara County ADU Plans Gallery — a 16-jurisdiction collaborative with free and low-cost designs. It's a different model from Pasadena's city-designed free plans: broader catalog, less city-specific pre-review.

## The 750 sq ft cliff

Standard §66311.5 application: under 750, all three city impact fees are $0; at 800, they're proportional to the primary dwelling, with the published Park In-Lieu cap at $9,328 per unit (FY 2027 schedule). The school fee has its own threshold question — Campbell Union's rate table says "over 500 sq. ft." while its "Who Pays" section says "exceeding 750 square feet"; at 800 sq ft both wordings give the same $3.79 rate, but the district page's mixed wording is worth flagging for smaller units.

## The bottom line

Campbell is the series' fee-maximalist: the highest school fee we've published, valuation-based permits with a per-square-foot construction tax, and the most granular sewer math in the data. At ~$310,000 at schedule caps, it sits near the top of the series — but built from entirely different parts. In Silicon Valley, even the school districts charge Silicon Valley prices.
